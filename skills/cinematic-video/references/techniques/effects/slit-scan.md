# Slit-Scan

Summary: Moving artwork is exposed for a long time through a narrow slit, converting time into a corridor of light converging toward the center.

## Narrative Function

- Turns the passage of time into streaks of light in space, forming layered luminous tunnels.
- Depicts not ordinary photographic space but a non-realistic transitional passage.
- Suited to star gate journeys, mental breakdowns and time-compression title sequences.

## How It Works

- Traditional method: slit mask + illuminated moving artwork + controlled relative motion + accumulated long exposure.
- Digital method: procedurally generate streaks with a stable vanishing point and ordered planes, smearing time along a single axis.

## When to Use

- Use: Sequences continuously passing through layers of luminous planes converging toward the center (star gate mode).
- Avoid: Kaleidoscopic mirroring or time-lapse of real streets.
- Avoid: Unstructured particle nebulae or random neon effects.

## Film Examples

- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Douglas Trumbull's Star Gate sequence, which established the grammar of the structured light corridor.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slit-scan light corridor: two walls of streaked magenta and amber light rush past the camera, each streak smeared along a single horizontal axis, with repeating luminous planes converging steadily toward one fixed vanishing point at the center. Ordered geometric structure, no random particles, no mirrored kaleidoscope pattern.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Chaotic particles with no slit axis or vanishing point → fix a single smear axis and control convergence toward the center.
- Mistaking mirrored patterns for slit-scan → use repeating planes, not mirror symmetry.
