# Zoetrope

Summary: Rotating motion is sliced into crisp, gapped strobe poses, like a Victorian animation toy.

## Narrative Function

- Presents motion as discrete frames rather than smooth motion blur.
- Creates a "drum of poses" effect: strobed slices of a rotating action.
- Reveals that motion is built frame by frame.

## How It Works

- Shoot the action with a strobe, or arrange discrete poses around a rotation.
- Each slice is crisp and sharp, with visible gaps between them.
- Use brief hard light (strobe or shuttered source) to freeze each pose.
- The camera can lock onto the drum or track a repeating cyclic action.

## When to Use

- Use: When you want to emphasize the aesthetics of sampling and frame construction.
- Use: Rotations, walk cycles or animation-toy-style imagery.
- Avoid: Making an ordinary walk choppy as generic slow motion, or stop motion of still objects.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Stop Motion](../time-and-motion/stop-motion.md) | Poses a static setup frame by frame; a zoetrope samples rotating / repeating action |

## Film Examples

- *The Horse in Motion* (1878): Considered a scientific precursor, sampling a horse's gait into a series of stills.
- *Batman Begins* (2005): Imagery of a zoetrope animation toy appears in the story.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A ring of strobe-lit figurines of a leaping dancer spins inside a dark drum, each pose frozen razor-sharp with black gaps between them. As the drum rotates, the discrete poses read as a repeating jump cycle, like a Victorian motion toy, with no motion blur.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Smooth overcranked slow motion, or just a stuttering walk with no true rotating cycle → produce strobe-sharp poses arranged around a repeating rotation.
- No gaps between poses → freeze each frame with brief hard light so the sampling is visible.
