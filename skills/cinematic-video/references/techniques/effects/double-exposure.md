# Double Exposure

Summary: Two or more images are layered in the same frame at once, blending transparently.

## Narrative Function

- Layers two images to create new meaning, conveying memory, duality or a ghostly presence.
- Lets two times or thematic elements "coexist" rather than transitioning from one to the other.
- For example, a face layered with a cityscape shows the link between a character's inner self and the outside world.

## How It Works

- In-camera: After exposure, mask part of the frame, rewind the film and re-expose with matching light.
- Optical: Use bi-pack film or multiple passes on an optical printer.
- Digital: Layer with additive, screen blending or hard mattes; lock off the camera unless both layers need motion.

## When to Use

- Use: When two very different images must occupy the frame at once, such as thoughts over a location or supernatural elements over a landscape.
- Avoid: When two physical characters need to interact, cast shadows on each other or pass objects.
- Avoid: When a simple dissolve would handle the transition better.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dissolve](../editing/dissolve.md) | A temporal transition from A to B; double exposure keeps both present at once |
| [Duplication](duplication.md) | Uses mattes to put the same person in frame twice as solid bodies, not translucent ghosts |
| [Morph](match-morph.md) | Geometric interpolation between forms; double exposure neither deforms nor switches |
| [Match Cut](../editing/match-cut.md) | Links two shots thematically; double exposure holds both within the same frame |

## Film Examples

- *Persona* (1966): Two faces layer over each other, blurring the boundary of identity.
- *The Shining* (1980): Fireplace flames are layered into the frame with a separate exposure.
- *The Tree of Life* (2011): Memory images layered over live-action nature footage.

## Prompt Examples

```text
In-camera double exposure: a woman's profile in soft side light, overlaid at about 50 percent opacity with a misty pine forest so the trees appear to grow inside her silhouette. Both images stay visible for the entire shot, locked-off camera, no morphing and no transition.
```

## Common Mistakes

- One image gradually fades into the other, becoming a dissolve → both images must stay visible simultaneously throughout.
- Two solid characters with no transparency at all, becoming duplication → specify opacity or matte placement explicitly.
- Confusing double exposure with morph → state clearly whether the second layer is a translucent overlay or a matte composite.
