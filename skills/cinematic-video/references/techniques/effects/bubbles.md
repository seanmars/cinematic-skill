# Bubbles

Summary: Soap bubbles act like tiny lenses in the air, with iridescent film sheen at their edges, refracting the subject.

## Narrative Function

- Brings emotional ambiguity: depending on lighting, it can feel joyful or threatening.
- Renders air as a transparent, glass-like substance.
- The subject stays recognizable among the bubbles.

## How It Works

- Backlight or sidelight the soap film to reveal highlights and refracted reflections of the subject.
- Each sphere has thin-film interference iridescence at its edge and a wet-looking surface.
- Keep a shallow depth of field so the subject stays clear among the bubbles.

## When to Use

- Use: Joy, a drowning sensation, magical moments, underwater scenes, spell effects.
- Avoid: Dry interiors that only need dust, or conference-room scenes.
- Avoid: Substituting fake circular bokeh for real bubbles.

## Film Examples

- *The Shape of Water* (2017): Water optics merge with floating spheres and characters.
- Music videos: Often use soap-film refraction as a visual metaphor.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A little boy stands in a sunlit garden as dozens of soap bubbles drift past the camera, each one showing swirling rainbow film at its edges and a tiny upside-down reflection of him. Strong backlight makes their wet surfaces sparkle, with shallow depth of field keeping his face clear.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only bokeh discs or particle haze, with no surface detail → render the soap film as a real lens, with highlights and wet edges.
- Bubbles block the subject → keep a shallow depth of field so the subject reads clearly among the bubbles.
