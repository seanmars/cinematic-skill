# Diorama

Summary: Shoot physical miniatures so they read as full-scale environments, building a tactile world from real light on real surfaces.

## Narrative Function

- Builds tactile worlds from physical models, such as spaceships or elaborate architecture.
- The illusion comes from real light falling on real surfaces, not visual tricks.
- Gives fantasy or futuristic settings a handcrafted solidity.

## How It Works

- Build fine surface texture to scale.
- Use a hard, distant key light that imitates sunlight.
- Keep enough depth of field and use slow camera moves to sustain the full-scale illusion.

## When to Use

- Use: Detailed miniature environments, such as spaceships, industrial skylines, fairy-tale settings.
- Avoid: Shallow depth of field, which exposes the tabletop scale of the model.
- Avoid: When you just want to make a real city look toy-like (that is tilt-shift).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Tilt-Shift](../lenses/tilt-shift.md) | Uses selective focus to make real landscapes look like toys; diorama uses real models with deep depth of field |
| [Forced Perspective](forced-perspective.md) | Scales real people and sets from a single viewpoint; diorama shoots models only |
| [Lens Flare](lens-flare.md) | Expressive light treatment, unrelated to scale illusion |

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Industrial cityscapes and building exteriors shot as miniatures.
- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Model photography of spaceships and space stations.
- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): A fairy-tale miniature world as a designed environment.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow gliding pass over a detailed physical miniature of a rain-soaked industrial refinery at dusk, every pipe, rivet, and grimy window finely textured. A single hard, distant key light imitates a low sun, and a deep stop keeps the whole model sharp from foreground to horizon so it reads as full scale. No shallow toy-like focus band.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Focus band too thin, movement too fast → stop down and slow the camera move.
- Coarse surface texture, fake-looking liquids → increase surface detail and use scale-appropriate effects.
