# Cyclope

Summary: Extreme fisheye distortion makes the frame look like a single giant pupil, presenting a non-human gaze.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a mechanical or alien surveillance presence.
- Depicts non-human (machine, monster, deity) seeing.
- The distortion itself becomes a character trait; unsuited to flattering portraits.

## How It Works

- Use an extreme fisheye lens or a circular iris mask.
- Place the subject extremely close to the lens, filling the frame.
- Keep the barrel distortion at the edges and only a single catchlight.
- Avoid Dutch angles unless the creature itself is off-axis; camera height matches the creature's eyes.

## When to Use

- Use: Surveillance, monsters, HAL-style machines, non-human gaze POVs.
- Avoid: Polite portraits, human first-person narration, ordinary close-ups.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

## Film Examples

- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): HAL's red lens becomes an eye of surveillance.
- *The Lighthouse* (2019): The gaze is transformed into a dizzying organ.

## Prompt Examples

```text
An extreme fisheye close-up of a single enormous mechanical eye filling the frame, its glowing iris bulging toward the lens with heavy barrel distortion at the edges. One sharp catchlight, a dark minimal background, framed at the machine's own eye height as it stares directly at us.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Just a normally proportioned wide-angle close-up → exaggerate near features into a huge sphere and emphasize the single pupil.
- Background too busy → keep the background minimal so the pupil is the only focal point.
