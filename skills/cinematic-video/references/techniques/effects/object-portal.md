# Object Portal

Summary: The opening of an everyday prop becomes a wormhole holding another space the room couldn't possibly contain.

## Narrative Function

- Treats the object as a passage, creating impossible spatial geography.
- Lets a character or the camera pass through a prop to reach a place the room itself can't hold (a filing cabinet leading to a tunnel, a mirror to a corridor, a phone swallowing a body).

## How It Works

- Shoot the prop with its thickness and rim visible.
- Composite or project the destination space inside the opening.
- Use parallax between the rim and the interior to build depth.
- Keep the surrounding room unchanged; hands and shadows interacting with the opening sell the effect.

## When to Use

- Use: Sequences where a character crawls into a prop and emerges somewhere else.
- Avoid: Generic glowing doorframes.
- Avoid: Passing through architectural walls, treating the wall as incidental (use pass through).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *Being John Malkovich* (1999): A character crawls through a small office door into another person's mind.
- *The Matrix* (1999): Mirrors and props become passages between worlds.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A woman slides open an ordinary office drawer and inside it lies a vast moonlit desert stretching to the horizon. The drawer's wooden rim and thickness stay sharp in the foreground, the desert shifts with parallax as the camera leans in, and the fluorescent office around it remains unchanged.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Just a glowing door with no three-dimensional prop → use a recognizable object and show thickness and rim parallax.
- The whole scene melts together → let two distinct spaces meet at the opening while the surrounding room stays still.
