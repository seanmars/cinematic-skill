# Shallow Focus

Summary: Only a very thin slice of distance is sharp, and the surroundings all dissolve into blur.

## Narrative Function

- Isolates the subject by "excluding" the surroundings, creating intimacy.
- Suited to moments when only one person or object deserves the audience's attention.
- Keeps background reactions or clutter unreadable, focusing on the character's inner state.

## How It Works

- Determined by three factors together: aperture, focal length and camera-to-subject distance.
- The focal plane usually sits on the eye nearest the lens.
- Blur quality (bokeh) depends on the lens itself, not just the aperture size.

## When to Use

- Use: Portrait moments in crowded settings, such as a station ticket clerk or a face in an apartment.
- Use: Scenes where background characters' reactions must be unreadable.
- Avoid: When blocking spans depth, geography matters, or action at a distant doorway still needs to be seen.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Bokeh](bokeh.md) | Describes the quality of the blur; shallow focus describes the thin sharp focal plane |
| [Deep Focus](deep-focus.md) | The opposite approach, keeping foreground and background sharp |
| [Rack Focus](rack-focus.md) | Starts with shallow focus, then moves the focal plane |

## Film Examples

- *Central Station* (1998): Separates portraits from the surroundings in a bustling station.
- *Her* (2013): Intimate close-ups separate the protagonist from the environment.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Medium close-up of a ticket clerk behind a glass booth in a busy train station, shot at T1.4 on a 50mm lens: focus sits precisely on her nearest eye, her far ear already softening, while the crowd behind dissolves into creamy blur with round highlight discs. Real optical falloff with depth between subject and background, no masked cutout.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Subject looks like a pasted cutout with unnatural edges, or both ears are sharp → keep real distance between subject and background.
- Blur looks like a digital mask → use optical falloff with recognizable bokeh highlights.
