# Transformation

Summary: A body, room or object turns into something else under the camera's continuous gaze.

## Narrative Function

- Delivers strong psychological impact when "becoming" is itself the action.
- Suited to memory shifts, the stages of a creature transformation, and matter gradually taking shape.
- Requires the audience to watch the change happen rather than skipping it with a cut.

## How It Works

- First find the anchors: elements that stay stable (pose, contact points, key objects).
- Change the surrounding world through movement, occlusion, projection, set changes or tracked compositing.
- Body changes are shown in stages via makeup, puppetry or digital techniques.
- Lighting must suit both the old and new states.

## When to Use

- Use: Story beats where "becoming" is the key action, memory rooms, visible transformation processes.
- Avoid: Simple feature-point morphs between two mismatched still images.
- Avoid: Opening a new scene, or generically warping everything at once.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Morphing](morphing.md) | Interpolates between contours; transformation uses physical or architectural change, or replaces the world around a stable figure |

## Film Examples

- *Vertigo* (1958): During a spinning embrace, the background set transforms into a remembered location.
- *Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind* (2004): Memory spaces collapse and shift around the characters.
- *Terminator 2: Judgment Day* (1991): The T-1000 changes form within the scene.
- *The Abyss* (1989): A water tentacle forms a human face.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A man stands still in the center of his childhood bedroom as the walls, furniture and light slowly shift around him into a snowy forest, one element at a time. His pose and face stay steady as the anchor, and the lighting blends naturally from warm lamp glow to cool winter daylight.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Face and walls generically melt and warp at the same time → establish a stable visual center as an anchor for the audience.
- Change is undesigned with no visible stages → plan a clear sequence of state transitions.
