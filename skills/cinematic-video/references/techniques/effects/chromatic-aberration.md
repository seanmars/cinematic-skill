# Chromatic Aberration

Summary: Red-cyan color fringes appear on high-contrast edges, growing stronger toward the frame corners.

## Narrative Function

- An optical lens flaw where different wavelengths of light cannot focus on the same point.
- Suggests old, wide-angle or cheap glass rather than a clean, perfect digital image.
- Brings a dreamlike, lo-fi or subjectively distorted atmosphere.

## How It Works

- Common in fast wide-angle lenses, especially older lens designs.
- Lateral CA: Fringing grows stronger the farther from frame center.
- Longitudinal CA: Color halos appear in out-of-focus areas (magenta in front, green behind, or vice versa).
- Fringes appear only at high-contrast boundaries, not as a full-image channel separation.

## When to Use

- Use: Neon signs or high-contrast night scenes, when you want a cheap or vintage lens texture.
- Use: Dream sequences that need optical rather than color-graded distortion.
- Avoid: Full-frame RGB separation (that is channel offset, not chromatic aberration).

| Technique | Difference |

| [Lens Flare](lens-flare.md) | Produces ghosts and light scatter, not edge color fringing |

## Film Examples

- *Spring Breakers* (2012): Cheap color fringing and analog errors overlay the party lights.
- *Enter the Void* (2009): Optical errors become the grammar of subjective night vision.
- Many music videos also use lateral CA in wide-open night scenes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night street lit by pink and white neon, shot on a cheap vintage wide-angle lens wide open: thin red and cyan fringes cling only to the hard edges of the signs and the silhouette of a passerby, growing stronger toward the frame corners while the center stays clean. No full-frame RGB channel split.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The whole subject splits into three colored copies, or only rainbow stripes remain → limit fringing to high-contrast edges.
- Severe misregistration at frame center as well → keep the core image aligned, with fringing stronger toward the edges.
