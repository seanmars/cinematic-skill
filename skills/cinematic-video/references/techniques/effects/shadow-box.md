# Shadow Box

Summary: Multiple layers of black paper-cut silhouettes stack in front of a strong backlight, like a miniature shadow-puppet theater.

## Narrative Function

- Creates theatrical visual storytelling with layered, dimensional paper cutouts.
- Emphasizes shape over detail, evoking fable, stage or night drama rather than realistic space.

## How It Works

- Separate figures and architecture into independent layers stacked in front of a hard backlight.
- Add no fill to faces, keeping graphic edges.
- Light behind, camera in front; camera movement creates parallax between layers.
- Minimal color, mostly from the backlight or tinted layers.

## When to Use

- Use: Sequences needing theatricality, paper-cut aesthetics, night atmosphere or fable storytelling.
- Avoid: Scenes where facial detail, skin texture or key light on the face matters.

## Film Examples

- *The Adventures of Prince Achmed* (1926): Silhouette animation built from stacked theatrical planes.
- Kara Walker's cut-paper processional works: A related visual language.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A fairy-tale scene built from layered black paper cut-outs: a castle, twisting trees and a fleeing princess stacked on separate planes in front of a glowing amber backlight. The figures stay pure black with crisp edges, and a gentle camera slide reveals parallax between the layers.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a single silhouetted figure, or a lit diorama with visible skin → use multiple stacked silhouette layers.
- Figures get fill light that reveals detail → keep a hard light source behind and add no fill to the figures.
