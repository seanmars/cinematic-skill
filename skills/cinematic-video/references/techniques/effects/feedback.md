# Video Feedback

Summary: A camera films a screen playing its own live feed, forming an endlessly receding picture-in-picture tunnel of ever-smaller layers.

## Narrative Function

- Produces recursive, self-referential images, like a signal watching itself.
- Expresses themes of being trapped in transmission, broadcast or looping consciousness.
- The nested "corridor" effect brings a hypnotic, no-exit feeling.

## How It Works

- Point the camera at a screen playing its own live signal.
- An off-axis angle creates a corridor with depth; facing the screen head-on creates concentric nested rectangles.
- Screen bloom, latency and phosphor persistence determine how fast the tunnel shrinks and breaks down.
- Keep the subject close to the screen so the first generation is recognizable.

## When to Use

- Use: Stories involving a signal watching itself, hypnosis, or being trapped inside a monitor.
- Avoid: As a background for dialogue scenes that need clear spatial relationships.
- Avoid: Sequences that only need a simple time loop.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Kaleidoscope](kaleidoscope.md) | Mirrors a single image into symmetrical slices; feedback is nested generations of images |
| [Infinite Loop](../time-and-motion/infinite-loop.md) | A time loop joining the last frame back to the first; feedback is optical recursion |

## Film Examples

- 1960s video experiments: Early artists pointed cameras at screens to explore recursive images.
- *Doctor Who* title sequence (1963): The best-known broadcast TV feedback example, a spinning tunnel of light.
- Contemporary music videos: Often use recursive corridors as the main visual.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A camera films a CRT monitor showing its own live feed, creating an endless tunnel of nested screens that shrink toward a vanishing point. The subject's face sits close to the glass in the first generation, while deeper copies smear with phosphor bloom and lag.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Making mirrored wedges (kaleidoscope) or a simple video loop → make sure the camera films its own output, with visible screen generations.
- First generation unrecognizable → keep the subject close to the screen so the outermost layer is clear.
