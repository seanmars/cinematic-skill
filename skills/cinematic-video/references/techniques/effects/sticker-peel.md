# Sticker Peel

Summary: A die-cut sticker layer peels off its backing, its curling edge revealing what lies beneath.

## Narrative Function

- When "peeling off" is the key moment, conveys that identity is removable and artificial.
- Suited to titles, posters, or shots that treat a face as a vinyl sticker.

## How It Works

- Build a die-cut layer with a paper or vinyl texture.
- Animate it with a directional peeling motion.
- Show the sheen of the adhesive side and the exposed backing; rim the curl with hard light.
- A peeling hand can be added; keep the subject readable throughout.

## When to Use

- Use: Titles, identity reveals, tearing down posters.
- Avoid: Chase sequences or transitions that need clear spatial logic.
- Avoid: Static layered imagery.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A glossy vinyl sticker of a smiling man's face slowly peels from the corner, curling back under hard side light that catches its die-cut edge. The adhesive underside glints as it lifts, revealing a blank white backing sheet and a different face printed beneath.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The poster merely exists in the scene with no peeling motion → add the peel animation, specular highlights on the adhesive and the exposed layer beneath.
- The peeled layer has no thickness → add a curl direction and hard-lit edges.
