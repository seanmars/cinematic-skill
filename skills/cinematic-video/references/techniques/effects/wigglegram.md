# Wigglegram

Summary: Two or three still frames from slightly different viewpoints alternate rapidly, creating a jittery depth like a stereoscopic postcard.

## Narrative Function

- Makes a still subject "come alive" like a lenticular postcard or a 3D GIF.
- Creates depth through viewpoint differences rather than camera movement.
- Emphasizes dimensionality without actual motion.

## How It Works

- Lock two to three still frames shot from viewpoints a few centimeters apart.
- Every frame has exactly the same height and lens.
- Flash back and forth in an A-B-A rhythm.
- Foreground shifts more than background, creating parallax; frames have no motion blur.

## When to Use

- Use: Portraits, product shots or playful moments of depth.
- Use: When you want a lenticular or depth GIF texture.
- Avoid: Full room tracking shots, or simulating handheld shake.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A wigglegram portrait of a girl holding a sunflower: two perfectly sharp still frames taken a few centimeters apart alternate rapidly in an A-B-A rhythm. The flower in the foreground shifts noticeably while the garden behind barely moves, with no motion blur and no camera drift.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Motion blur or a continuous camera path appears → use two sharp stills shot very close together and flicker cleanly between them.
- Baseline too wide makes the image jump jarringly → keep a small baseline with the subject locked at the center.
