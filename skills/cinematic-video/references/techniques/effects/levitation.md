# Levitation

Summary: The subject hangs silently in the air against gravity, while the gap beneath the feet and hanging fabric make it feel real.

## Narrative Function

- Presents "hovering" as a fact of the story, conveying weightlessness, the supernatural or the divine.
- The visible gap beneath the feet and naturally hanging clothing are the key evidence of the "impossible."
- Suited to takeoff, landing and hovering moments, not to falling.

## How It Works

- Physically support the subject with wires, a crane arm or a platform, then hide or remove it in post.
- Light to emphasize the gap beneath the subject.
- Fabric and hair hang naturally under real gravity to maintain realism.

## When to Use

- Use: When a character or object must hover in the air as a narrative fact.
- Avoid: Falling sequences that need weight and the consequences of gravity.
- Avoid: When the descent itself is the dramatic point.

## Film Examples

- *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* (2000): Airborne fights across rooftops and bamboo forests.
- *The Matrix* (1999): Fight moves extend and hang in midair.
- *Mary Poppins* (1964): Descends impossibly from the sky holding an umbrella.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A monk in loose robes hovers silently a meter above a stone courtyard, a clear gap of light visible beneath his bare feet. His sleeves and hem hang straight down under real gravity as he drifts upward slightly, with no wires or supports in view.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Floats like a balloon with limp limbs → keep the body's weight and add a takeoff or settling motion.
- Fabric floats upward too → clothing should hang naturally.
- Gap beneath the feet is unclear → light so the gap below reads.
