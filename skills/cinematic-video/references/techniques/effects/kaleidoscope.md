# Kaleidoscope

Summary: A single subject is mirrored and duplicated into an ornate pattern radiating symmetrically around a center.

## Narrative Function

- Transforms the subject into jewel-box-like decorative geometry.
- Suits musical numbers, decorative moments or subjective narrative ruptures rather than realistic depiction.

## How It Works

- Mount a kaleidoscope prism or mirror tube in front of the lens.
- Or shoot top-down with radially arranged blocking (dancers in a circle).
- Requires a clear axis of symmetry and repeating mirrored fragments, or the image looks cluttered.

## When to Use

- Use: Decorative geometry, musical numbers, jewel-like visual effects.
- Avoid: Stretching the subject into a vanishing-point tunnel (that is slit-scan).
- Avoid: A plain top-down shot without repetition, or collage-style torn elements.

## Film Examples

- *42nd Street* (1933): Busby Berkeley's top-down choreography forms kaleidoscopic geometry.
- *Gold Diggers of 1933* (1933): The chorus forms radial patterns in top-down shots.
- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Jewel-like radial fragmentation appears in the Star Gate sequence.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Kaleidoscope effect on a dancer in a sequined gown: her spinning figure is mirrored into eight identical wedges arranged in perfect radial symmetry around the center of frame, the facets rotating slowly like a jewel. Clean mirror seams, no tunnel streaks, no torn collage edges.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with slit-scan streaks, a plain top-down shot or collage layers → establish a clear mirror axis.
- Fragments with no pattern → repeat the fragments around the center for jewel-like symmetry.
