# Floating Fall

Summary: The body sinks slowly while hair and clothing react to gravity late, like a fall in a dream.

## Narrative Function

- Depicts the moment after going over the edge, combining dreamlike suspension with catastrophe.
- Establishes a sense of height and adds emotional weight through time distortion.
- Best for the beat right after being pushed or jumping.

## How It Works

- The body keeps a downward trajectory, but gravity is delayed on secondary elements such as hair, coat and debris.
- The body falls faster than fabric and accessories.
- The camera can stay still or fall along; when falling along, the environment slides past with parallax.
- A side key light separates the subject from the void behind.

## When to Use

- Use: The instant after going over an edge, dream sequences, stunts that need a sense of suspension.
- Avoid: True levitation (no net descent), slapstick falls, real-time falls with the ground rushing up.
- Avoid: Ordinary slow motion where every element slows uniformly.

## Film Examples

- *Inception* (2010): The physics of the corridor turn falling into drifting through architecture.
- *The Matrix* (1999): In the rooftop and street sequences, bodies hover in a state of delayed time.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A woman in a long red coat drops backward from a rooftop into a dark night sky, sinking slowly downward. Her coat and hair lag behind her body, rippling upward as if gravity reaches them late, while city lights slide past below and a side key light carves her out of the void.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The subject never descends, becoming levitation → ensure net downward movement and a visible ground reference.
- Everything slows uniformly, becoming ordinary slow motion → delay the fabric physics so it lags behind the body's speed.
