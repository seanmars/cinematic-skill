# Argus

Summary: Countless eyes or lenses cover a wall or body, turning "being watched" into visible anatomy.

## Narrative Function

- Makes surveillance concrete as a visible body or object: a whole wall of cameras, or a deity covered in pupils.
- Expresses the oppressive "excess of gaze" of paranoia, divinity or networked omniscience.

## How It Works

- Treat eyes as physical objects, counted and arranged on skin, walls or around a still subject.
- Keep one recognizable central subject as the anchor for the cluster of eyes.
- For organic eyes, give each pupil a tiny catchlight; for cameras, add tiny indicator lights.
- Keep the eyes from blurring into visual noise.

## When to Use

- Use: Themes of paranoia, deities or surveillance networks.
- Avoid: Simple portraits that need only one pair of eyes.
- Avoid: Neat two-panel split screens.

## Film Examples

- *Doctor Strange* (2016): An excess of viewing angles appears in a kaleidoscopic tunnel.
- *The Fountain* (2006): Multiplied visionary sight.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A pale ancient deity sits motionless in a dark temple, her arms, shoulders and the surrounding walls covered with dozens of blinking human eyes, each iris catching its own tiny glint of light. Her calm face stays the clear center while every eye turns to stare at the viewer.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a glow on a face or a simple two-panel split as Argus → make sure the pupils / viewpoints are countable and form a cluster.
- A cluster of eyes with no center → keep one recognizable central subject.
