# Night Vision

Summary: The world seen through the green (or white) phosphor image of night-vision goggles, with heavy grain, blooming highlights and a field of view framed by the device.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys hunting, hiding or surveillance, with a restricted point of view.
- Emphasizes "seeing through a device" rather than natural vision.
- Uses the circular frame of goggles or a scope to build low-light urgency.

## How It Works

- Amplifies residual photons via a photocathode and microchannel plate.
- Usually shows green (or white) phosphor with visible grain and highlight bloom; high gain produces scintillating noise and limited dynamic range.
- Needs in-story motivation: goggles, a rifle scope or a surveillance monitor.

## When to Use

- Use: When characters operate night-vision gear in the dark.
- Use: POV shots in raids or tactical sequences.
- Avoid: Moonlit scenes, pale infrared forests, or simply grading a normal night scene green.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Thermal](thermal.md) | Detects heat instead of amplifying light; bodies appear as temperature mattes |

## Film Examples

- *The Hurt Locker* (2008): Image-intensified footage as the combatants' point of view.
- *Zero Dark Thirty* (2012): Goggle and raid imagery establish the operational visual language.
- *The Silence of the Lambs* (1991): Puts the audience behind night-vision goggles in the dark.

## Prompt Examples

```text
First-person view through military night-vision goggles during a nighttime raid: the image is a grainy phosphor green inside a circular tube mask, noise shimmering in the shadows, and a porch light blooming into a bright smeared halo. No thermal color palette, not a simple green-tinted night grade.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Grading a normally exposed image green → add high-gain noise, true phosphor color and highlight bloom.
- Confused with thermal or infrared → add an in-story device frame (goggles, scope).
