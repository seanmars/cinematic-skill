# Altered State

Summary: Smears, color casts and time distortion intertwine so the image shows the character's disordered nervous system rather than objective reality.

## Narrative Function

- Visualizes an altered state of consciousness: edges and colors both become unstable.
- The image reflects the character's perception, not the outside world.
- Best for moments where "perception itself" drives the story.

## How It Works

- Combine long-exposure smears, step-printing time breaks, wrong white balance, afterimages, optical distortion and occasional superimpositions.
- The subject must stay recognizable to maintain a narrative anchor.
- Use recurring visual markers so the altered state becomes a coherent language.

## When to Use

- Use: Intimate interior scenes where the character's consciousness wavers.
- Use: Moments of drug use, dizziness, death or mental breakdown.
- Avoid: Establishing shots that must clearly convey geography.

## Film Examples

- *Enter the Void* (2009): Camera movement, smears and color are tied to a disembodied consciousness.
- *Requiem for a Dream* (2000): Speed, optics and editing convey drug intoxication.
- *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas* (1998): Wide angles and distorted color depict a psychedelic state.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A man sways alone in a dim hotel bathroom as his perception unravels: his movements leave smeared trailing edges, the white balance drifts from sickly green to magenta regardless of the light, and time stutters in step-printed jerks. He stays clearly recognizable in the center of frame; the same trails and color drift recur throughout.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using a single decorative effect (fisheye, datamosh) as an altered state → build a language from 2-3 motivated, recurring visual markers.
- Layering one glitch over a clean room, or making the image completely unreadable → keep the subject locatable.
