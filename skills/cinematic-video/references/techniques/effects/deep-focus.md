# Deep Focus

Summary: Everything from the nearest foreground to the farthest background is sharp, so the whole space reads at once.

## Narrative Function

- Presents multiple spatial layers at once, letting the audience decide where to look.
- The director gives up using focus to steer the eye and tells the story through staging instead.
- Suits scenes where cause and effect play out across the whole space at once, such as foreground action and a distant reaction.

## How It Works

- Combine a shorter focal length, small aperture, ample lighting and carefully calculated subject distances.
- Achieve continuous sharpness in a single take, with no focus pull and no seams.
- Some classic examples actually hide in-camera compositing while looking like a purely optical effect.

## When to Use

- Use: When the spatial relationships of the entire room matter to the story.
- Avoid: When blur is needed to separate foreground and background layers, or there isn't enough light to stop down.
- Avoid: When isolating a single subject is the narrative point.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Shallow Focus](shallow-focus.md) | Isolates a single focal plane; deep focus refuses isolation |
| [Split Diopter](../lenses/split-diopter.md) | A visible blur line between two sharp distances; deep focus is continuous space |
| [Layered Depth](../composition/layered-depth.md) | Compositional layering that works with any focus approach |
| [Rack Focus](rack-focus.md) | Uses a focus pull to direct attention; deep focus leaves the choice to the audience |
| [Forced Perspective](forced-perspective.md) | Often requires deep focus so blur doesn't reveal true distances |

## Film Examples

- *Citizen Kane* (1941): While adults talk in the foreground, young Kane stays clearly visible far outside the window.
- *Touch of Evil* (1958): Near and far characters are equally sharp and readable.
- *The Best Years of Our Lives* (1946): The whole room is uniformly sharp, becoming a single dramatic unit.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Wide interior shot on an 18mm lens at f/11: a man signs papers at a desk in the extreme foreground while, through the window at the back of the room, a child plays in the snow, and both are equally sharp. One continuous volume with natural perspective, no rack focus, no split-diopter seam.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistakenly using a split diopter or layered compositing, producing a vertical blur band, inconsistent grain or a hidden focus pull → make sure it is one continuous space.
- Mismatched foreground and background perspective with visible seams → keep perspective consistent and leave no join marks.
