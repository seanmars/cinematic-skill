# Anamorphic Flare

Summary: A point light source hitting the cylindrical elements of an anamorphic lens draws a horizontal streak across the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Serves as visual evidence of "shot on widescreen scope lenses," not just a brighter image.
- Streak color (blue, cyan, magenta) varies with lens coating, creating a distinct genre texture.
- Often used to heighten night, urban and sci-fi atmospheres.

## How It Works

- Shoot in anamorphic format (usually 2x squeeze).
- Place hard point sources (headlights, practicals, the sun) in frame or at its edge.
- Keep the cylinder axis consistent so streaks stay horizontal; stopping down makes bokeh more polygonal.

## When to Use

- Use: Night scenes with headlights or neon sweeping across the frame.
- Use: Wet streets and urban environments full of practical sources.
- Avoid: Spherical lenses, or when you want circular aperture ghosts.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Neon and car lights draw horizontal streaks across anamorphic glass.
- *Super 8* (2011): Abundant horizontal streaks build a period scope visual language.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night city street shot on 2x anamorphic lenses: oncoming headlights throw thin horizontal cyan-blue streaks that stretch across the full width of the frame. Out-of-focus neon signs render as vertical oval bokeh, and every streak stays locked to its visible light source.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Round bokeh paired with horizontal streaks pasted in post, unrelated to any light source → bokeh should be oval, and streaks should stay horizontal and locked to visible point sources.
- Inconsistent streak direction → keep the lens cylinder axis fixed.
