# Light Leak

Summary: Stray light that bypasses the lens burns an orange-red or white fog into the frame edge, as if the camera body were wounded.

## Narrative Function

- Implies a body fault (loose magazine, lens cap not seated), symbolizing damaged time and memory.
- Makes an equipment accident part of the story's emotional texture.
- Evokes analog, home-video nostalgia.

## How It Works

- Physical cause: a gap in the magazine or a part lets stray light fog the film during exposure.
- The fog usually blooms from one edge and shifts position as the film advances.
- Digital simulation: add a warm additive fog from the edge, entirely unrelated to light sources in frame.

## When to Use

- Use: Scenes about memory, accidents or failing analog equipment.
- Use: Super 8-style home movie aesthetics.
- Avoid: As a default warm filter, or when the edge glow actually comes from a light in frame.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Lens Flare](lens-flare.md) | Light passes through the lens and creates ghosts; a light leak bypasses the lens entirely |
| [Halation](halation.md) | Glow around existing highlights; a light leak is an independent localized stain |
| [Film Grain](film-grain.md) | A texture, not a fog |
| [Light Flash](light-flash.md) | The whole frame overexposes instantly; a light leak concentrates at the edge |

## Film Examples

- *Spring Breakers* (2012): Light leaks and other analog damage render the texture of party memories.
- *Super 8* (2011): Treats magazine damage as a period accident, echoing home-movie grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Super 8 home-movie footage of a backyard birthday party: a warm orange-red fog bleeds in from the right edge of the frame, swelling and drifting as the film advances, as if light slipped through a gap in the cartridge. The stain is unrelated to any lamp or sun in the scene.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Colored fog follows a light source or forms a red ring around a bulb, becoming lens flare or halation → the fog should emanate from a gap in the camera body at the edge.
- Fog is tied to light sources in frame → keep the light leak fully independent of scene lighting.
