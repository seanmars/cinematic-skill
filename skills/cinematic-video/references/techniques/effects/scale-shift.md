# Scale Shift

Summary: The set stays the same while a person suddenly becomes a giant or shrinks to toy size relative to the environment.

## Narrative Function

- Use when "relative size" itself is the point of the story.
- Creates surreal situations: a person becomes a toy in a familiar space, or a monster in a dollhouse.
- A different concept from "time scale" in editing (intercutting storylines of different durations).

## How It Works

- Keep the set locked and consistent.
- Change the subject's size with oversized furniture, miniatures, compositing or reprinting.
- Shadows and contact points must match the new scale.
- Static size alignment can use forced perspective.

## When to Use

- Use: When the size change is central to the plot or the source of comedy.
- Avoid: When you only need a magnified close-up.
- Avoid: Confusing it with editing techniques that intercut different timelines.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Forced Perspective](forced-perspective.md) | Statically aligns real sizes; a scale shift is a "change" between two states |
| [Magnification](../lenses/magnification.md) | Extreme close-up of detail; doesn't change the body-to-room scale |

## Film Examples

- *Honey, I Shrunk the Kids* (1989): Shrunken kids placed among out-of-scale furniture and grass.
- *Ant-Man* (2015): The human body juxtaposed with grass and everyday objects at the wrong scale.
- Counter-example: *Dunkirk* (2017) and *Inception* (2010) use time scale in editing, not optical scale shift.

## Prompt Examples

```text
In an ordinary kitchen, a woman shrinks to the height of a coffee mug while the room stays exactly the same. She stands on the countertop beside a towering toaster, her small shadow falling correctly on the tiles, feet firmly in contact with the surface.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Just an enlarged face in a normal room, like a close-up or zoom → keep the set consistent and truly change the body-to-room scale.
- Shadows and contact points don't match the new size → align shadow direction, size and contact position.
