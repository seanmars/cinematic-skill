# X-Ray

Summary: Seeing through surfaces to internal structure, with bones, metal and wiring as the subject of the image.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals hidden interiors when the story needs to see through objects.
- Skin becomes a translucent barrier, and internal structure becomes the focus.
- Serves medical, diagnostic or mechanical inspection contexts.

## How It Works

- Depicts transmission rather than reflection; denser materials read more like structural line art.
- High-density parts such as bone and metal read like diagrams.
- Requires strong, even backlight that passes through the subject.
- Keep internal structure graphic and readable, avoiding glowing blurry blobs.

## When to Use

- Use: Medical scenes, mechanical inspection, suitcase contents, chest interiors.
- Avoid: Heat visualization or infrared foliage effects.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Thermal](thermal.md) | Shows heat distribution in false color; x-ray shows transmitted structure |
| [Infrared](infrared.md) | Near-infrared reflection (foliage, skin); x-ray passes through objects |

## Film Examples

- *The Abyss* (1989): HUD overlays and diagnostic visualizations.
- Science films: Used as lab film or instrument overlays.
- Superhero X-ray vision: Has become a widely familiar visual grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
An airport security scanner view of a suitcase in grayscale X-ray: fabric and clothes fade to a faint haze while a pistol, keys and a tangle of wires stand out as crisp dense silhouettes. Clean diagrammatic transmission look, no heat colors, no normal-light background showing through.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mixing in a thermal camera's false-color palette → keep a monochrome transmission structure.
- Ghostly double exposure with the normal background → make bones or wiring the main graphic content.
