# Thermal

Summary: The camera reads temperature instead of light; bodies are glowing blobs of heat, and recently used objects glow too.

## Narrative Function

- Presents the point of view of a predator, forensics or a targeting system.
- Heat becomes image information: warm bodies, a just-vacated seat, walls that may be dark even when lit.
- Suited to hunting, rescue and reconnaissance situations centered on "heat sources."

## How It Works

- No conventional key light; contrast comes from temperature differences.
- Choose a consistent palette (white-hot, ironbow, black-hot); warm cores, cooler edges.
- Glass blocks long-wave infrared, so a face behind a window may not show at all.

## When to Use

- Use: Heat-focused scenes, such as riders on a cold ridge or exhaled breath becoming heat plumes.
- Use: Thermal reconnaissance and search and rescue.
- Avoid: As a generic sci-fi grade on normally lit scenes, or confusing it with night vision or near-infrared photography.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Night Vision](night-vision.md) | Amplifies residual light, usually green |

## Film Examples

- *Predator* (1987): In the alien hunter's view, warm human bodies stand out against the cool jungle.
- *Sicario* (2015): Thermal and electro-optical imagery depicts border raids and surveillance operations.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Thermal camera view in an ironbow palette of a mountain rescue at night: two hikers glow yellow-white against the deep purple snow, their breath rising as warm orange plumes, and a boulder one of them just sat on still holds a faint warm imprint. No green night-vision tint and no near-infrared foliage look.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as night-vision green or pale infrared foliage → let temperature determine the tones: warm subjects, cold surroundings.
- Brightness follows light placement rather than temperature → specify an explicit palette (such as ironbow or white-hot).
