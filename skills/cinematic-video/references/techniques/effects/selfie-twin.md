# Selfie Twin

Summary: The same person appears twice in a phone-selfie composition, as if posing with themselves.

## Narrative Function

- Expresses an identity gag, or a character unable to escape their own image.
- Brings a social-media-style dual self perspective.

## How It Works

- Mimic an arm's-length phone selfie composition: wide focal length, a visible or implied arm, slight barrel distortion.
- The same face appears twice in frame and must match precisely.
- Both are lit by the same source (screen glow, window light or on-camera light).
- Wardrobe can be deliberately identical or contrasting; keep background people to a minimum.

## When to Use

- Use: When the subject appears to be posing for a selfie with themselves.
- Avoid: Two-shots of two look-alike strangers.
- Avoid: True twin spectacle or traditional studio drama staging.

## Film Examples

- *The Parent Trap* (1961 / 1998): One actor plays both twins.
- *Enemy* (2013): The double becomes a threat.
- These are more dramatic relatives; selfie twin is the casual arm's-length selfie version.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A phone selfie at arm's length: the same young woman appears twice, cheek to cheek, grinning at the lens, one in a yellow hoodie and one in a black blazer. Wide phone-camera lens with slight barrel distortion, her outstretched arm visible at the frame edge, both faces lit by the same window light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using two look-alike actors, or a long lens with no phone grammar → duplicate the same identity at arm's length with small wide-angle camera distortion.
- Lighting on the two doesn't match → light both faces with a single source.
