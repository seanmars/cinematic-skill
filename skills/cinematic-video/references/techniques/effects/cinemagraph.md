# Cinemagraph

Summary: A still photograph in which only one element moves in an endless loop.

## Narrative Function

- Creates tension between stillness and life.
- Suits fashion, posters, or moments where only a subtle bit of motion "seeps" through.
- The moving element must form a believable loop.

## How It Works

- Shoot a looping action (steam, rain, flicker) on a locked-off camera.
- In post, mask the moving area and freeze everything else on a single frame.
- Make the masked region loop seamlessly with no visible join.

## When to Use

- Use: Subjects where only one detail needs to live, such as rain on a window, steam in a cup, a single blink.
- Avoid: When the whole scene needs motion or complete stillness.
- Avoid: Multiple moving elements at once.

## Film Examples

- Originated in fashion photography and advertising, not narrative film.
- *La Jetée* (1962): Explores the relationship between still images and motion, but is not strictly a cinemagraph.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Cinemagraph of a quiet café table by a window: a thin ribbon of steam curls up from a coffee cup in a seamless endless loop, while everything else, including the woman reading beside it, the rain-streaked glass, and the street outside, stays completely frozen. Locked-off camera, no other motion, no visible loop seam.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Multiple elements moving at once, an obvious loop seam, or everything frozen with no living element → isolate only one masked element, with an invisible join.
- Background not taken from the same frame → freeze the rest of the image on a single frame.
