# Generative

Summary: A world or face is assembled step by step according to some rule, so the image shows "being generated" rather than a finished product.

## Narrative Function

- Lets the audience see the system that builds the image, emphasizing the process of "taking shape" by rules.
- Suits story beats where a world or character is being assembled or born.

## How It Works

- Treat the matter taking shape as a lit subject.
- Specify the generative principle explicitly (particles, iteration, growing geometry).
- Specify element scale and light quality (hard or soft light).
- Lock off the camera so the "growth" itself is the motion; the subject stays recognizable throughout assembly.

## When to Use

- Use: When the assembly of a world or face is the plot point.
- Avoid: As a style filter on a finished portrait.
- Avoid: When you need corrupted compression (use datamosh instead) or continuous transformation like person-to-beast (use transformation instead).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Datamosh](datamosh.md) | Deliberate compression failure and errors; generative builds by rules |
| [Transformation](transformation.md) | One form continuously becomes another; generative shows a system building the image |

## Film Examples

- Process films such as *Koyaanisqatsi* (1982): Organize the image through system-driven growth of imagery.
- Music videos: Often make the iterative process the core visual event.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A human face assembles itself from thousands of small glowing cubes that snap into place in growing clusters, following a visible iterative pattern. Locked camera, soft side light modeling each block, the face fully recognizable once the last pieces settle.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Blocky codec smearing appears, or liquify warping with no clear rule → specify a visible iterative rule and countable elements.
- Final subject unrecognizable → make the subject clearly recognizable at the end.
