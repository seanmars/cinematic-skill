# Halation

Summary: A soft red-orange halo grows from the edges of bright light sources, so highlights are no longer sharp hard edges.

## Narrative Function

- Gives lamps, windows and candlelight a "breathing" soft glow, bringing a film texture.
- Contrast softens around highlights, while faces and environmental detail stay readable.
- Pairs well with tungsten practicals and similar sources, evoking a nostalgic or warm mood.

## How It Works

- Film capture: Reduce or omit the anti-halation layer, or choose Super 8 or 16mm, which scatter easily in the base.
- Digital emulation: Add a warm or red fringe only to the brightest edges, while preserving grain and eye texture.
- It is not a full-frame soft-focus diffusion filter.

## When to Use

- Use: Amber practicals, neon signs with a warm halo, windows glowing in the rain.
- Use: When you want highlights to behave like film rather than digital.
- Avoid: Situations that would blur the whole frame or eat highlight information; halation acts only on specific bright edges.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Lens Flare](lens-flare.md) | Scatter inside the lens glass; halation is emulsion-layer scatter around existing highlights |
| [Film Grain](film-grain.md) | A texture, not a halo |
| [Vintage Cine Glass](../lenses/vintage-cine.md) | Different cause: vintage lenses get soft falloff from their coatings, while halation comes from the emulsion |

## Film Examples

- *Barry Lyndon* (1975): Candlelit interiors with bright highlights that never clip to dead white.
- *Atonement* (2007): Soft glowing light through summer windows of a country estate.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Candle-lit parlor shot on 16mm film: each flame and the bright window edge bloom with a thin reddish-amber halo that bleeds only a few pixels into the surrounding darkness. Faces, eyes, and fabric weave remain crisp; no overall haze or soft-focus filter.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using Promist, bloom filters or full-frame defocus → limit the halo to bright edges.
- Highlights crushed to dead white with no density or red fringe → keep the warm edges and facial detail.
