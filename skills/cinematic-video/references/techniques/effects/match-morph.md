# Morph

Summary: One form's features and contours continuously slide and warp until it becomes another complete form.

## Narrative Function

- Turns one identity into another through vertex movement rather than opacity change.
- Passes through unstable in-between states before settling into a new physical form.
- Reserve for stories truly about "transformation"; don't use it as a flashy dissolve.

## How It Works

- Establish point-to-point correspondence between the two forms.
- Interpolate the geometry over time, usually layered with a texture cross-dissolve.
- The duration itself is the narrative grammar.

## When to Use

- Use: One face or body becomes another within about 6 seconds, with visible feature movement.
- Avoid: When two images only need to trade dominance, coexist, or echo shapes across a cut.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dissolve](../editing/dissolve.md) | Transitions by opacity; forms need no correspondence |
| [Double Exposure](double-exposure.md) | Both images exist simultaneously |
| [Match Cut](../editing/match-cut.md) | Similar shapes cut together directly, with no interpolation |
| [Duplication](duplication.md) | Two physical versions exist simultaneously |

## Film Examples

- *Terminator 2: Judgment Day* (1991): The T-1000 shifts between liquid metal and human form.
- *The Mask* (1994): Human anatomy exaggerated and warped as a morph target.
- *Willow* (1988) and the music video *Black or White* (1991): Early digital morphs that established the "correspondence + time" grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Continuous morph over six seconds: an old fisherman's weathered face slowly reshapes into the face of a young girl, the nose narrowing, the jaw softening, and the eyes sliding into new positions through awkward in-between shapes before settling into one solid face. Not a dissolve and no double-exposure ghosting.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with a dissolve, double exposure or match cut (transparent ghosting, hard cuts or texture fades only) → make the facial features actually move.
- In-between states are too clean → show implausible transitional forms that finally settle into a single solid form.
