# Distortions

Summary: Straight lines bend, space stretches, and the whole geometry becomes untrustworthy.

## Narrative Function

- Uses warped geometry to make space unreliable and unsafe.
- Expresses subjective, voyeuristic or deceptive architectural space.
- Suits depicting altered mental states.

## How It Works

- Choose an optical source: ultra-wide or fisheye for barrel distortion, telephoto for pincushion distortion, anamorphic for a wavy feel, diopters or physical objects (water, rain) to melt the edges.
- When a wide-angle lens is close to the subject, perspective distortion stacks on optical distortion.

## When to Use

- Use: Tense interiors where space feels untrustworthy and geometry bends.
- Avoid: Mixing with a normal 50mm master shot with straight lines.
- Avoid: Applying distortion in post like a sticker.

## Film Examples

- *A Clockwork Orange* (1971): Extreme wide-angle lenses make rooms and faces aggressive.
- *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas* (1998): Real lenses and close distances melt space, visualizing intoxication.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Tense interrogation in a cramped office shot on a 12mm ultra-wide lens pressed close to the suspect: the ceiling lines bow outward, the door frame curves, and his face swells toward the camera while the detective behind him shrinks away. Real optical barrel distortion across the whole frame, not a post-production warp or flare effect.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with lens flare, or only a circular fisheye crop → choose a specific lens family.
- Only color smearing while wall lines stay straight → bend lines across the whole frame and let near objects swell.
- Distortion unrelated to actual optics → anchor the distortion to a real lens.
