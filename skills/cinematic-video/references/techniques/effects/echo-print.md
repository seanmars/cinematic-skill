# Echo Print

Summary: A just-completed action leaves an offset, translucent afterimage, like a lingering stain on film.

## Narrative Function

- Keeps the aftertaste of a completed action (a turn, a strike, a departure) on screen.
- The past pose doesn't vanish but lingers as a faint "chemical residue," suggesting psychological aftershock.

## How It Works

- Lock off the camera or move it only minimally.
- Duplicate the previous few frames, offset them slightly in pixels / time along the direction of motion, and layer them back at lower opacity.
- Light only the real subject with a single key light; the afterimage gets no separate lighting.

## When to Use

- Use: After an important action ends, when you want the residual pose to stay in frame.
- Avoid: When you need to introduce a "physically present" double character.
- Avoid: When both figures must be equally clear and evenly exposed.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Double Exposure](double-exposure.md) | Layers two complete images; echo print is a faded offset of the same action |
| [Step Printing](../time-and-motion/step-printing.md) | Repeats discrete frames; echo print is a trail caused by misregistration |

## Film Examples

- No specific titles listed on the source page; common in experimental optical printing works and video-echo-style music videos.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A dancer spins and stops; behind her, a faint translucent afterimage of the previous pose lingers, slightly offset along the direction of the turn. Locked-off camera, one key light on the live dancer only, the ghost copy unlit and fading like a stain on film.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Making two equally solid, keyed-out bodies, like a double exposure → keep one real subject plus one fainter, offset copy.
- Lighting the afterimage separately → keep a single key light only; the afterimage stays unlit.
