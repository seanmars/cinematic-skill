# Typography (Kinetic Type)

Summary: Text becomes a physical object in the frame, occupying space and taking part in blocking, rather than a caption floating on top.

## Narrative Function

- Places text inside the shot's space as part of the scene's composition.
- Teaches the audience a set of visual rules before or during the action.
- Title sequences can tell the story directly through type.

## How It Works

- Establish one consistent visual rule (ink becomes a map, letters are set pieces, text tracks the subject).
- Light letters as surfaces with edges.
- Leave enough space for the spelling to stay clearly readable, and let the text evolve step by step.
- The camera can dolly so the text gains parallax.

## When to Use

- Use: When text must share physical space with characters.
- Use: Title sequences that tell the story through type.
- Avoid: Lower-thirds, single title cards, or hard-to-read scribbles laid over portraits.

## Film Examples

- *Vertigo* (1958): The spiral title sequence establishes the theme of obsession with graphics and type.
- *Se7en* (1995): Hands, notebooks and handwriting form an unsettling, ritualistic title sequence.
- *Catch Me If You Can* (2002): Animated letters condense the chase plot.
- *Annie Hall* (1977): Subtitles reveal what the characters are really thinking.
- *Scott Pilgrim vs. the World* (2010): Text and graphic symbols merge into the action scenes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
The word "HOME" is built from wooden letters standing on a real dining table, each letter about thirty centimeters tall and lit by the same warm window light as the room. A child walks between the letters as the camera dollies past, the spelling staying sharp and fully readable.
```

## Common Mistakes

- AI-generated letters are illegible, or text floats with no sense of space → specify the exact words and define the surface and position the text sits on.
- Text animation follows no rule → establish one visual rule that evolves in sequence.
