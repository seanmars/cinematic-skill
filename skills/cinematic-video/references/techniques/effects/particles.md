# Particles

Summary: Backlight makes dust, sparks and pollen in the air glint as individual, countable specks rather than a uniform haze.

## Narrative Function

- Builds "countable" atmospheric weather: dust, sparks, pollen, each catching the light.
- Turns the air itself into textured matter rather than a uniform haze.

## How It Works

- Use strong backlight so each particle gets a specular highlight.
- Specify the particle type explicitly (dust, embers, pollen, ash).
- The subject must stay readable within the particle cloud.
- Concentrate the effect in a directional beam rather than filling the frame; let particles drift naturally.

## When to Use

- Use: Sacred spaces, barns, post-battle scenes, flour-dusted kitchens.
- Use: When you want the air to become countable matter.
- Avoid: When the air should be invisible, or only a clean beam without particles is needed.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Backlight makes particles and droplets individually visible.
- *The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford* (2007): Backlit dust becomes part of the weather.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Inside an old wooden barn, a strong low sun shines through a gap in the boards behind a farmer, and countless individual flecks of hay dust glitter in the directional beam as they drift. The rest of the barn stays clear, and his face remains readable through the sparkling specks.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only fog or haze layers with no individual particles → use backlight, specify the particle type and ensure each speck is countable.
- Particles fill the frame and obscure the subject → confine them to a directional beam.
