# Projection Mapping

Summary: Images are physically projected onto a screen or architectural surface while actors perform in front, and the camera captures the composite in one take.

## Narrative Function

- Places images on physical surfaces that actors can interact with.
- The projector is both a light source and part of the set, creating an in-camera composite.
- Unlike digital backgrounds, it must actually project onto a tangible surface.

## How It Works

- Front projection: a beam splitter aligns camera and projector on the same axis, with a retroreflective screen and foreground lighting to keep the background from washing out.
- Rear projection: a translucent screen sits behind the actors; background plate brightness must match.
- Projection mapping: the projector is registered to an architectural surface, then rephotographed by the camera.

## When to Use

- Use: Blending foreground actors with large environmental backgrounds under unified lighting.
- Use: Scenes where architecture itself becomes a display surface.
- Avoid: Simple double exposures, or floating hologram effects with no physical surface.

## Film Examples

- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Front projection renders the barren Dawn of Man landscape.
- *To Catch a Thief* (1955): Driving dialogue scenes use rear projection.
- *Vertigo* (1958): A controllable foreground paired with pre-shot surroundings.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Two actors sit in a vintage convertible on a soundstage while a winding coastal road plays on a rear-projection screen behind them. The projected plate shares the camera's perspective and vanishing point, the key light on their faces matches the plate's afternoon sun, and the background moves only gently. No translucent ghost overlay.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Translucent overlay ignoring the wall, or mismatched vanishing points → foreground and background share the same camera viewpoint.
- Implausible parallax drift in the background → project onto a clearly specified surface and restrain background motion.
- Foreground and background lighting don't match → match the foreground lighting to the projected image.
