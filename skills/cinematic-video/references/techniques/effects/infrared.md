# Infrared

Summary: Shot in near-infrared light, foliage turns pale and glowing, skies go deep black and skin looks waxy, making real landscapes look otherworldly.

## Narrative Function

- Creates strange, forbidden or alien settings while keeping photographic realism.
- The tonal relationship between foliage and sky reverses, bringing a surreal unease.
- Unlike the "instrument view" of thermal imaging or night vision, it is still reflected-light photography.

## How It Works

- Use an IR-pass filter, or a camera with the IR-cut filter removed that is sensitive to 700-900nm near-infrared.
- Relies on reflected sunlight or tungsten radiation, not heat emitted by objects.
- Set exposure to emphasize the contrast between white vegetation and a dark sky.

## When to Use

- Use: When real landscapes need to look like an alien world, such as forbidden forests, concert documentation or surveillance aesthetics.
- Avoid: Depicting total darkness (use image-intensified night vision instead).
- Avoid: When body heat must be detected (use thermal instead).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Night Vision](night-vision.md) | Amplifies residual photons onto a phosphor screen; usually green and coarse-grained |
| [Monochrome](../color/monochrome.md) | Visible-light black-and-white; infrared tones come from infrared reflectance |

## Film Examples

- *The Village* (2004): The forest sequences use infrared film stock, making the woods unfamiliar territory while staying photographically real.
- Concert infrared photography: Pale skin, darkened dyed fabrics, and no green phosphor artifacts.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Near-infrared photograph of a summer forest at midday: the leaves glow a ghostly white, the sky above is nearly black, and a girl walking the path has pale, waxy skin. Reflected-light realism with no green phosphor tint, no heat palette, and no goggle overlay.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with night vision (green grain), thermal imaging (false-color heat mask) or ordinary black-and-white with dark trees → reverse the tonal relationship of vegetation and sky.
- Adding goggle interface elements → keep a reflected-light photographic aesthetic and remove any device frame.
