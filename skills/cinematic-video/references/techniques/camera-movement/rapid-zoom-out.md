# Rapid Zoom Out

Summary: Snaps instantly from the subject to wide, the environment bursting into frame, then holds.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals scale or situation as a shock or a comic punctuation.
- Instantly turns a close subject into a tiny figure within a bigger problem or scene.

## How It Works

- The camera body stays fixed while focal length snaps to the wide end within a few frames.
- After widening, hold steady on the expanded frame.

## When to Use

- Use: Revealing a situation unexpectedly.
- Avoid: Passing off a slow zoom out as a snap.

## Film Examples

- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): snap zoom outs as graphic punctuation, dropping characters into trouble.
- *Scott Pilgrim vs. the World* (2010): likewise uses snap zoom outs as comic-book rhythmic punctuation.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Rapid zoom out on a locked camera: from a tight close-up of a smug man lighting a cigarette, the lens snaps wide in a few frames to reveal he is standing in the middle of a field surrounded by dozens of armed police. Then the wide frame holds, no camera travel.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Done as a physically retreating pull out → lock the camera position, snap the lens to wide, and hold on the expanded composition.
