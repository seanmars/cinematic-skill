# Super Dolly Out

Summary: A long, high-speed backward camera move that reveals the environment, shrinking the subject into part of a vast spatial system.

## Narrative Function

- Places a person in a larger constructed world, emphasizing isolation.
- Retreats far enough for geography to take over the frame, turning intimate space into map-like territory.

## How It Works

- A long reverse move along the lens axis, starting close and ending wide, at high speed.
- Uses parallax to "open up" space rather than compress it.
- Physically moves the camera; no zoom.

## When to Use

- Use: Dropping a character into a system that suddenly feels enormous.
- Avoid: Short pull-outs just to adjust headroom; the move must be big and impactful enough.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dolly Out](dolly-out.md) | Gentler retreat, showing scale difference without a strong geographic reveal |
| [Aerial Pullback](aerial-pullback.md) | Leaves the human sightline entirely, letting geography win from the air |

## Film Examples

- *The Truman Show* (1998): the camera retreats until the artificial world swallows the protagonist.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Super dolly out: from a tight close-up of a lone office worker at her desk, the camera races backward in one long physical move, rows of identical cubicles bursting open around her with wide parallax until she is a tiny figure lost in an enormous open-plan floor. No zoom.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Substituting a zoom out or a short pull-out → needs a long physical retreat that makes the room "burst open," with the environment dominating the frame.
