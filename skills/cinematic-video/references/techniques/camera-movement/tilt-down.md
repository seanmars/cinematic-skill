# Tilt Down

Summary: The camera position stays the same while the lens pitches from top to bottom, establishing the space above before landing on the subject below.

## Narrative Function

- Guides audience attention downward, revealing people, objects or details below.
- Establishes scale or context above first, then lands on the subject.
- Keeps a vertical sense of spatial hierarchy through a vertical sweep rather than moving the camera.

## How It Works

- The camera is fixed on a tripod, pitching downward only.
- Compose the high start point and low end point in advance.
- Watch exposure control when moving from a bright sky or ceiling to a darker subject.
- Move slowly and deliberately.

## When to Use

- Use: Establishing the space above first, then landing on a character or object.
- Use: Linking ceiling details to things below.
- Avoid: When the camera needs to physically descend to eye level, or enter a street from the air.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Tilt Up](tilt-up.md) | Opposite direction, used to reveal height and spectacle |

## Film Examples

- *Citizen Kane* (1941): vertical compositions link ceilings to power relations.
- *The Godfather* (1972): moves from the room to a face or object, establishing status hierarchy.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Measured tilt down from a locked tripod inside a grand cathedral: the frame starts on the painted ceiling and golden chandeliers, then pitches slowly downward to land on a small girl kneeling alone in the front pew. Camera height never changes, no boom or pedestal move.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistakenly made into a crane down, with floor parallax and changing camera height → fix the tripod and do a pure pitch rotation.
- End point lands on empty space → end on the subject below.
