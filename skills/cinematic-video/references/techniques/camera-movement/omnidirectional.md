# Omnidirectional

Summary: 360-degree spherical capture; the viewer can look in any direction, with no hidden off-screen space.

## Narrative Function

- There is no "behind the camera"; crew, lights and set lose their traditional hiding places.
- Blocking must be designed for a full sphere, not a rectangular frame.
- Off-screen space can no longer serve as a narrative tool or a source of threat.

## How It Works

- Use a spherical camera rig, or stitch paired fisheye lenses, to produce equirectangular images.
- The nodal point represents a position in 3D space, not a rectangular frame.

## When to Use

- Use: VR films, or situations needing 360-degree environmental awareness.
- Avoid: When off-screen space serves the story, or traditional composition is needed.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Omnidirectional 360-degree spherical view from the center of a bustling night market: stalls, crowds and lanterns surround the viewer in every direction with no hidden off-screen space. Stationary nodal point, seamless stitching, a complete look-anywhere world.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Faking spherical capture with a 360-Degree Orbit → it must be true spherical blocking, with the crew hidden and free viewing in any direction.
