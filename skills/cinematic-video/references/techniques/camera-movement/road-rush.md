# Road Rush

Summary: The camera is mounted at bumper height close to the ground; streaking lane lines and the asphalt itself are the star.

## Narrative Function

- Treats the road surface itself as the event, conveying speed and menace.
- Lanes, light trails and road surface shift from background detail to dynamic compositional elements.

## How It Works

- Camera at bumper height or lower, with a wide-angle lens.
- Emphasize motion blur.
- Prioritize lane markings, reflectors and wet pavement in frame to strengthen the streaking.

## When to Use

- Use: Chase sequences, night highways, moments when driving turns menacing.
- Avoid: Ordinary vehicle beauty shots, or aesthetic shots from standing height.

## Film Examples

- *Tron: Legacy* (2010): highway lanes become glowing directional guides.
- *Death Proof* (2007): low angles close to the asphalt emphasize the car's menace.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Road rush: a camera skims inches above wet asphalt at bumper height on a night highway, white lane dashes streaking into long blurred lines and red taillight trails smearing across the frame. The road surface dominates the wide shot, pure speed and menace.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot from chest height like a car commercial, with clear vehicle detail and little road → drop the camera height drastically, blur the lane elements, and let the road fill the frame.
