# Rapid Zoom In

Summary: With the camera body still, the lens zooms in at near-cut speed to the subject, then holds.

## Narrative Function

- Instantly emphasizes a subject (face, weapon, sign); a powerful narrative tool.
- So fast it reads like an axial cut with motion trails rather than a leisurely zoom.

## How It Works

- Lock the camera body, zoom to the longest focal length in under a second, then hold steady.
- No parallax movement.

## When to Use

- Use: When information must land suddenly like a punch rather than through gradual approach.
- Avoid: Using it on every line and every action; it loses force.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Crash Zoom In](crash-zoom-in.md) | Still recognizable as a zoom; rapid is faster and closer to a cut |
| [YoYo Zoom](yoyo-zoom.md) | Pulls back again; rapid stops at the end point |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Gradual pressure; rapid is instant |

## Film Examples

- *Hot Fuzz* (2007): fast zooms as punctuation for comedy and violence.
- *Kill Bill: Vol. 1* (2003): snap zooms emphasize standoffs and moments of violence.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Rapid zoom in from a locked tripod: in just a few frames the lens snaps from a wide saloon interior to a tight close-up of the stranger's hand resting on his revolver, then holds dead still. Almost like a cut, no dolly, no parallax.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Parallax appears or the magnification is too leisurely, becoming a dolly in or a slow crash zoom → lock the camera position, snap to a tight shot within a few frames, and hold.
