# Trucking

Summary: The camera translates sideways alongside a walking subject, keeping the subject's size constant.

## Narrative Function

- The camera acts like a polite companion, moving parallel to the pedestrian.
- Builds closeness during dialogue and movement, without the directionality of a frontal tracking shot or the industrial rhythm of a conveyor.

## How It Works

- Translate sideways on a wheeled platform, matching the subject's walking speed.
- Commonly 35-50mm, at eye or chest height.
- The foreground shifts noticeably while background walls move more slowly.

## When to Use

- Use: Following pedestrians talking while walking down a street or corridor.
- Avoid: Static portraits; it becomes a purely decorative slider move.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dolly Left](dolly-left.md) | Lateral move without rotating the lens; can survey space or accompany pedestrians |
| [Tracking Shot](tracking.md) | Broader category, including frontal and rear follows |
| [Conveyor](conveyor.md) | Lateral move at a fixed industrial rhythm, treating the subject as cargo |

## Film Examples

- *Touch of Evil* (1958): moves sideways along a small-town street, the environment peeling back layer by layer with parallax.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): lateral movement through service spaces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Trucking shot: the camera slides sideways alongside a young couple strolling down a rainy sidewalk, matching their walking pace so they stay the same size in a medium two-shot. Parked cars and lampposts peel past in the foreground while the shopfronts drift more slowly behind.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Following pedestrians with a pan, with subject size fluctuating and only the background turning → physically translate sideways, keep the subject's size, and let environmental layers peel back naturally.
