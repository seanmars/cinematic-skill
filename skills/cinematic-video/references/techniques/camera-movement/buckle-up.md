# Buckle Up

Summary: The camera is mounted inside a moving vehicle's cabin; passengers stay sharp while the world outside blurs past.

## Narrative Function

- Turns the vehicle into a moving set: the cabin is the stage, motion is the narrative grammar.
- Emphasizes dialogue and action at speed, e.g. chases, night drives, dates in the car, making the road part of the emotion.

## How It Works

- The camera is mounted inside the cabin (dashboard, passenger area, etc.), framed wide enough to hold both faces and windows.
- The interior stays sharp while the exterior blurs from speed, engine vibration and acceleration.

## When to Use

- Use: Interior scenes in a moving vehicle, e.g. high-speed chases, night drives, conversations while driving.
- Avoid: Dialogue in a parked car, or product shots of the vehicle exterior.

## Film Examples

- *Baby Driver* (2017): Interior-mounted shots tell the story in sync with the music.
- *Drive* (2011): Faces stay sharp while window lights streak in the night.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Buckle-up shot from a dashboard mount inside a speeding taxi at night: the driver and nervous passenger stay sharp in a wide frame, while neon signs and headlights streak past every window. The cabin shudders with each hard acceleration.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as an exterior beauty shot, with no passenger focus or an unclear interior → Put the camera inside, keep faces sharp, and let the road blur outside the windows.
