# Through Object Out

Summary: The camera starts on the subject and passes outward through an object, moving from an interior space to the outside world.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes "leaving" by passing through a physical barrier into a wider world.
- The viewpoint can abandon the subject, letting key events happen outside the frame's attention.
- Absence itself becomes the dramatic core.

## How It Works

- Design the room, the object and the exterior space as one.
- Use removable barriers, rig handoffs or compositing.
- As the camera shifts focus, exterior action and off-screen sound carry the story.

## When to Use

- Use: Passing through an object that acts as a portal to achieve an exit or scale reveal.
- Avoid: Plain pull-outs or jib rises with no object as a passage.

## Film Examples

- *The Passenger* (1975): the hotel room shot moves slowly toward and through the window bars, then looks back at the room.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Through-object-out shot: starting on a prisoner asleep on his cot, the camera drifts backward and slips out between the bars of the cell window, the iron bars filling the frame for a moment, then emerges above a sunlit prison yard where guards are gathering. One continuous move.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a zoom out or crane up, with no object filling the frame as a passage → the object must fill the lens during the pass-through before the larger world is revealed.
