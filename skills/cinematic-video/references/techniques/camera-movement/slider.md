# Slider

Summary: A short, precise linear move on a 60-120cm slider, adding parallax to a static frame.

## Narrative Function

- Proves spatial depth with minimal movement, as foreground objects slide past the background subject.
- Deliberately restrained motion that brings a static frame "to life" without upstaging it.

## How It Works

- Fix the short slider level on the ground or a tripod and move at constant speed.
- Mainly lateral, optionally with a slight push; don't sneak in zoom during the slide.
- Both start and end are steady, settled frames.

## When to Use

- Use: When a locked-off frame is too rigid but laying a full dolly track is overkill, e.g. a foreground lamp slowly sliding past in an interview over about 6 seconds.
- Use: Product close-ups, interview shots.
- Avoid: Chase scenes, large spaces or curved paths.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slider shot: the camera glides about one meter to the right on a short rail over six seconds, at constant speed, as a desk lamp in the foreground drifts across a seated interviewee. Straight linear path, subtle parallax, no zoom, settling into a still frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Travel distance too long, like a full dolly, or a curved path like a small orbit → emphasize short rail, straight line, subtle parallax and a still ending.
- Mixing zoom into the slide → keep focal length unchanged.
