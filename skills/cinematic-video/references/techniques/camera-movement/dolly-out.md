# Dolly Out

Summary: The camera physically retreats on a track; the subject shrinks as surrounding space slides into frame layer by layer.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys emotional weight through real distance, not a lens trick.
- "The camera leaving the character" directly expresses abandonment or isolation.
- Reveals how small the character is within a larger environment or system.

## How It Works

- Set the tight and wide end compositions before shooting.
- Focal length fixed; the shrinking frame comes entirely from actual distance.
- The camera retreats on track or wheels, with foreground objects entering naturally from the frame edges.

## When to Use

- Use: Pulling away after a confession to reveal the emptiness of the whole office.
- Use: Expressing abandonment, or establishing the character's relationship to the environment.
- Avoid: When only a flat widening is needed; a zoom out is enough.

## Film Examples

- *A Clockwork Orange* (1971): Retreats from Alex to reveal the whole Korova Milk Bar.
- *The Godfather* (1972): Pulls back from Bonasera to frame the entire office meeting.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow dolly out on a track from a close-up of an old man sitting alone at a kitchen table, fixed 35mm lens, no zoom. As the camera retreats, door frames and chairs slide into the edges with real parallax, and he shrinks inside the dim, empty house.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a slow zoom out, the frame just widens flatly → Camera physically retreats with a fixed focal length.
- No new spatial layers appear → Let furniture and architecture slide in from the edges to show parallax.
