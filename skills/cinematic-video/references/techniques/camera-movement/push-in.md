# Push In

Summary: The camera slowly and physically approaches the subject, landing emotional weight before the audience notices.

## Narrative Function

- Emphasizes the instant a character has an epiphany or understands something.
- Tightens the frame, applying psychological pressure on the subject.
- Should be nearly imperceptible: the audience feels the emotion before noticing the camera move.

## How It Works

- Physically approach with a dolly, slider or Steadicam, or advance a virtual camera along the Z axis.
- Focal length stays fixed to preserve parallax; this is what distinguishes it from a zoom.
- Usually pushes from a medium shot to a medium close-up, slowly, with slight foreground shift.

## When to Use

- Use: The moment an idea takes shape, or a character realizes they've fallen into a trap.
- Avoid: Pushing in on every line.
- Avoid: Establishing geography or crossing a room over long distances.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dolly In](dolly-in.md) | Longer travel distance, emphasizing spatial change |

## Film Examples

- *The Silence of the Lambs* (1991): slow physical approach during dialogue raises tension.
- *The Shining* (1980), *There Will Be Blood* (2007): subtle push-ins convey psychological atmosphere.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Very slow push in over about six seconds on a soldier reading a letter at a desk, fixed 50mm lens, moving from a medium shot to a medium close-up. A lamp in the foreground drifts subtly across the frame, and the move ends just as his expression hardens.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Faking it with a zoom, with no visible parallax → fix the focal length, physically advance the camera a short distance, and stop on the emotional beat.
