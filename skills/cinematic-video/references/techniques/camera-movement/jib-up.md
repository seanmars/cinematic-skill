# Jib Up

Summary: A short jib rises slightly from the subject, revealing the surrounding room at human scale.

## Narrative Function

- Leaves a face or a table to reveal who else is in the space.
- An intimate rise, not a grand city reveal.

## How It Works

- The short jib starts on the subject and rises, ending on a shot that reveals the whole room.
- Capture parallax on near objects such as table edges, lamps and shoulders; without parallax it is just a tilt.

## When to Use

- Use: Leaving a face to reveal others in the room.
- Avoid: When the scene needs to turn people into part of the landscape.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Crane Up](crane-up.md) | Rises to rooftop and map scale, often with a pull back for a large-scale reveal |
| [Jib Down](jib-down.md) | Reverse, descending into a conversation scene |

## Prompt Examples

```text
Jib up: starting on a nervous man's face at a poker table, a short jib arm rises smoothly, the table edge and a hanging lamp shearing past in the foreground, until it reveals the whole smoky back room and the other players watching him. Human-scale lift, real parallax, no tilt-only move.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Doing a tilt up where only the angle changes and the table lamp doesn't shift → physically raise a short jib, bring out near-object parallax, and end on the room reveal.
