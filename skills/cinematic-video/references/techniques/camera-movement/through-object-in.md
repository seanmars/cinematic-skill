# Through Object In

Summary: A virtual camera glides through a physical object (cup, keyhole, speaker, mouth), using it as a door to reveal the subject on the other side.

## Narrative Function

- Uses a specific prop as a passage rather than a wall, creating impossible reveals or humor.
- Maintains the object's thickness and shape throughout.

## How It Works

- Build a 3D camera path linking exterior live-action footage with a digital interior space.
- Lens optics stay believable, with controlled speed changes.
- Space stays continuous while passing through the object.

## When to Use

- Use: Achieving an impossible reveal through a specific object.
- Avoid: Cutting directly while claiming a pass-through; it must continuously pass through the prop's interior.

## Film Examples

- *Panic Room* (2002): explores the whole townhouse through unreachable openings.
- *Fight Club* (1999): pulls out from microscopic neural imagery to the narrator's situation.
- *The Secret in Their Eyes* (2009): a continuous shot diving from the air into a crowd.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Through-object-in shot: the camera glides toward an old brass keyhole, slips through the dark metal tunnel with its shape intact, and emerges on the other side revealing two conspirators whispering over a candlelit map. One continuous move, controlled acceleration, no cut.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Cutting straight from a cup to a face with no interior passage → pass through the prop, keep its shape, and arrive on the other side.
- Object surface warping or deforming → keep a rigid structure and consistent perspective.
