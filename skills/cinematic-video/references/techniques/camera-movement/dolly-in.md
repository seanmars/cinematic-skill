# Dolly In

Summary: The camera, mounted on a dolly, physically advances toward the subject; the foreground slides away and space closes in step by step.

## Narrative Function

- Takes the audience into a character's state of mind, a room or a threat through real spatial movement.
- Replaces cuts with a continuous shot, so the change is felt gradually.
- Space itself tightens, emphasizing the weight of consequences.

## How It Works

- The camera advances toward the subject on a wheeled dolly or track along a fixed axis.
- Focal length stays constant (commonly about 40mm), eye-level height stays level.
- Foreground objects produce parallax during the advance; time the arrival to an emotional or story beat.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments that need an actual change in space, e.g. a two-shot gradually tightening to catch a lie forming on an actor's face.
- Avoid: Faking a dolly with a digital zoom; shots that only need instant impact; when only optical compression is wanted without spatial change.

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): Steady advances down hotel corridors make rooms into traps.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): The camera pushes forward, amplifying social pressure.
- *Jaws* (1975): The camera slowly pushes in as Quint recounts the Indianapolis story.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Smooth dolly in on a track toward a woman frozen at the end of a dim hallway, fixed 40mm lens, no zoom. Door frames and wall sconces in the foreground slide past with clear parallax, starting wide and ending in a medium close-up as her expression breaks.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Faked with a slow zoom or crop, walls magnify but don't slide → Must have real camera travel and foreground parallax.
- Focal length or eye height changes midway → Fix the focal length and eye height.
- Stop point decided by duration rather than story → End the push on an emotional beat.
