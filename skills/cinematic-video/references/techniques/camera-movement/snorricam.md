# SnorriCam

Summary: The camera is strapped to the actor facing their face; the face stays steady and centered while the world behind lurches violently.

## Narrative Function

- Traps the audience in a body experiencing panic, drunkenness or dissociation.
- The face locked in frame center while the world tilts and flips creates a sense of psychological confinement.

## How It Works

- Securely mount a lightweight camera on the actor's torso, lens facing the face.
- As the subject moves, the camera moves with the body, keeping subtle body sway but no face drift.
- Usually paired with a wide-angle lens at close range; can also be recreated in a virtual environment.

## When to Use

- Use: Sequences of a character walking in panic or a disoriented psychological breakdown.
- Avoid: First-person views meant to show what the character "sees"; SnorriCam faces the wearer.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Point of View](../camera-angles/pov.md) | Shows what the character sees; SnorriCam shows the character |
| [Handheld](handheld.md) | Camera operator holds it alongside, not mounted on the body |
| [Locked-On](locked-on.md) | Locks the camera onto the subject externally; SnorriCam mounts directly on the wearer |

## Film Examples

- *Requiem for a Dream* (2000): body-mounted shots as characters lose control.
- *Pi* (1998): as the protagonist moves through the city, the frame continuously unsettles his psychological stability.

## Prompt Examples

```text
SnorriCam rig strapped to the woman's chest, wide lens pointed up at her face: her face stays locked dead center while the crowded subway platform lurches and swings wildly behind her. Frantic, disoriented, as if the world is sliding away from her.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a POV or a follow from behind → explicitly describe the camera mounted on the body, facing the face, with violent background motion.
- Face drifting in frame → keep the face fixed and centered; only the background shakes.
