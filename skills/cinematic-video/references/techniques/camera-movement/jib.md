# Jib Shot

Summary: A short-arm jib makes small rises, drops or arcing swings at human scale, light and intimate.

## Narrative Function

- Creates intimate camera movement over short distances (e.g. tabletop to standing height).
- Common in MVs and dialogue scenes, with a gentle feel close to the characters.
- Not suited to revealing skylines or making geography the narrative focus.

## How It Works

- The short arm swings on a fixed base as a pivot, with a fluid head compensating the angle in sync.
- The subject holds composition, with moderate parallax.
- Height change stays at human scale, with a small arc radius.

## When to Use

- Use: Small height changes in intimate scenes, e.g. gently rising from a two-shot at a dinner table.
- Use: Adding accent camera moves to a drum kit or dialogue.
- Avoid: When a city or large-scale geography needs revealing.

## Film Examples

- *La La Land* (2016), *Whiplash* (2014): small swinging rises over a pier, rooms and a drum kit, keeping an intimate scale.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Short jib move in a small jazz club: the camera lifts gently from table height over a drummer's snare and cymbals, sweeping in a small arc just a few feet up to frame his focused face. Intimate human scale, subtle parallax, no wide reveal of the room.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turning it into a large crane or pedestal, even reaching the skyline → explicitly limit it to a short arm, a few feet of travel and intimate composition.
