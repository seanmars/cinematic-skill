# Lazy Susan

Summary: The subject rotates on a motorized turntable while the camera stays fixed, or the camera circles a static object at a fixed radius.

## Narrative Function

- Inspects an object or costume from every angle without cutting.
- Lighting stays focused on the subject; the environment recedes.
- Not suitable when spatial geography matters to the story.

## How It Works

- Place a motorized turntable under the subject, with the camera locked at eye or product height, often 50-85mm.
- Alternative: a circular slider orbits a fixed subject at a constant radius.
- Nodal distance stays constant; no zoom.

## When to Use

- Use: Full showcases of products, costumes, weapons or sacred objects.
- Avoid: Dialogue scenes; rotation does nothing for performance.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [360-Degree Orbit](orbit-360.md) | Camera moves through real space; walls and extras shift visibly |

## Film Examples

- Mainly commercials and music videos: perfume turntable showcases, rotating album covers, hero props displayed like specimens.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Lazy Susan product shot: a glass perfume bottle slowly revolves on a motorized turntable while the camera stays locked at product height with a 70mm lens. Constant distance, no zoom, clean dark backdrop that never moves, soft key light tracing the rotating facets.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistakenly made into an orbit, with the background moving visibly and lighting dragging → lock either the camera or the subject on the turntable, keep the radius constant, and let only the object rotate.
- Adding zoom to change size → no zoom; keep a fixed distance.
