# FPV Drone Shot

Summary: High-speed first-person flight, banking and threading close past obstacles, delivering an impossible sense of speed and proximity.

## Narrative Function

- Creates incredible speed and closeness rather than distant observation.
- The lens banks and rolls with the airframe; it is not a stable composition tool.
- Windows, steel beams and buildings are treated as real flight obstacles, not compositional elements.

## How It Works

- A small quadcopter piloted in first person, with the camera stabilized or only lightly damped.
- Sharp acceleration, banked turns, motion blur at the frame edges, close passes over surfaces.
- When describing it, specify the flight path, endpoint and obstacles along the way.

## When to Use

- Use: High-speed threading through tight spaces and between buildings to convey speed.
- Use: Showing impossible entry angles and racing energy.
- Avoid: Static interviews, gentle landscape reveals or slow establishing shots.

## Film Examples

- *Top Gun: Maverick* (2022): Established high-speed proximity flight as a visual grammar.
- Music videos: Popularized threading through buildings at racing speed.

## Prompt Examples

```text
FPV drone shot in first-person view: the camera dives off a rooftop, banks hard left through the steel girders of a construction site, threads an open window into an office, and bursts out the other side above a busy street. Motion blur streaks the frame edges, ending in a sweeping climb toward the skyline.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a slow aerial pullback or crane, hovering with a stable horizon and no banking → Emphasize first-person flight, banked turns, speed, named obstacles and a clear endpoint.
