# Dolly Right

Summary: The whole camera slides laterally to the right without rotating, gradually bringing offscreen space on the right into frame.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals information by sliding in a specific direction, e.g. an eavesdropper, a doorway or a potential threat.
- Unlike a pan, the horizon stays level; space is "walked past" rather than "turned toward."
- The direction of travel must serve the story to have power.

## How It Works

- The camera translates right on wheels or track.
- Height and tilt stay unchanged; no compensating pan unless deliberately designed.
- The background shows multiple layers of parallax, with new space entering from frame right.

## When to Use

- Use: When moving right reveals exactly the information needed.
- Use: When depth parallax is needed that a pan cannot show.
- Avoid: Decorative moves without narrative purpose; drifting left and right in the same shot for no reason.

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): Lateral movement carries the camera through real streets.
- *The Player* (1992): Sideways slides woven into the studio opening long take.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Steady dolly right along a library aisle, the camera sliding sideways without panning while the lens stays aimed straight ahead. Bookshelves in the foreground peel past faster than the reading tables behind, until a hidden figure listening behind a shelf slides into the right side of frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Accidentally adding a pan or orbit, with the frame veering and the horizon sinking → Keep a straight track and a fixed lens direction.
- Camera-to-subject distance fluctuating → Keep a consistent rightward speed and distance.
