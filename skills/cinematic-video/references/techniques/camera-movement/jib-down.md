# Jib Down

Summary: A short jib lowers slightly, near foreground sliding past, for an intimate arrival at the subject.

## Narrative Function

- Creates an intimate sense of entry, like a guest sitting down at the table.
- Establishes "arrival" without a large exit from a cityscape or architecture.

## How It Works

- Use a short jib, starting slightly high and lowering to subject height.
- Near objects (cups, lamps, railings) shift visibly through parallax.

## When to Use

- Use: Entering a conversation, a secret, or the opening of a song.
- Avoid: Large-scale environment reveals, or when a castle or skyline needs to exit frame.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Crane Down](crane-down.md) | Large-scale descent from a rooftop or other height, dropping from a god's-eye view to a person |
| [Jib Up](jib-up.md) | Reverse, rising away from the subject while keeping an intimate scale |

## Film Examples

- Common in music videos and intimate dramas, where the final frame arrives at the subject rather than a skyline.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Jib down: a short jib arm starts just above a candlelit dinner table and gently lowers to eye level with an elderly couple holding hands, wine glasses and the flickering candle sliding past in the foreground. A small, intimate human-scale arrival.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Faking it with a tilt down on a fixed tripod → the jib must actually descend, with human-scale foreground parallax.
