# Parallax

Summary: As the camera moves, the relative speed difference between near and far objects proves the world has depth.

## Narrative Function

- Foreground moves faster than background, building spatial layers.
- Provides proof of three-dimensional space that zoom alone cannot.

## How It Works

- The camera physically translates (dolly, tracking, aerial or handheld all work).
- Keep near objects in frame, moving faster than the distant horizon.
- The speed difference between planes is the source of depth.

## When to Use

- Use: When the environment needs weight, e.g. walking past a row of columns or driving past trees.
- Avoid: Adding digital shake to a still image and calling it camera movement.

## Film Examples

- *The Tree of Life* (2011): with a moving viewpoint, foreground nature shifts against the distant sky.
- *Gravity* (2013): as the camera translates, debris and faces move at different speeds.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Parallax shot: the camera slides sideways past a row of stone columns in the foreground that whip across the frame, while the distant cathedral and hills behind barely drift. Real camera translation with clearly layered speeds, no zoom.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using zoom instead of translation, magnifying the whole frame uniformly → physically move the camera and keep foreground objects to show layered speeds.
- Adding fake shake to a static frame → it must be real spatial displacement.
