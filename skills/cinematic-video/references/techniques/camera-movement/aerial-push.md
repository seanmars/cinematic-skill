# Aerial Push In

Summary: The camera descends and advances from high altitude, singling out one person from the whole landscape.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes the whole system or landscape first, then locks onto a single character.
- Lets the audience grasp the situation before caring about the person.
- Must start at altitude; not for eye-level scenes.

## How It Works

- Starts from true aerial height and keeps descending and/or advancing, with clear spatial orientation.
- Done as continuous camera travel, not a fast zoom from a static extreme wide.
- Ends with a recognizable character in frame.

## When to Use

- Use: Singling out one person from a desert, crowd, city or convoy.
- Use: Showing geography first, then revealing the character.
- Avoid: When already at ground level, when a rooftop crane is enough, or when the opening frame is a close-up.

## Film Examples

- *Sicario* (2015): Locates characters from altitude among desert and border facilities.
- *Children of Men* (2006): Finds the individual in a collapsing public space.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Aerial push in from high above a sprawling refugee camp at dawn: the drone descends and glides forward in one continuous move, the grid of tents giving way to a single path, until it settles on a young boy carrying a water jug, clearly recognizable at the end.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a crane down, or a zoom in on a static extreme wide → Ensure a true high-altitude start, continuous descent and advance, landing on a clearly visible character.
