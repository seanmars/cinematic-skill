# Eyes In

Summary: The camera pushes into the subject's gaze until the iris fills the frame, turning a portrait into an inner space.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys inner states such as realization, deception or memory by pushing into the eye rather than cutting to a close-up.
- The eye itself becomes a "location."

## How It Works

- Starts on the face and slowly pushes toward the iris, without tilting.
- Maintains catchlight and character identity continuity throughout.
- The last few centimeters can be done with a macro or close-up lens.

## When to Use

- Use: Introspective moments or psychological turning points.
- Avoid: Using it as a default beauty close-up; don't use it when the eye isn't a narrative location.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

## Film Examples

- *Requiem for a Dream* (2000): Pushes into the pupil, treating it as an enterable space.
- *The Passion of Joan of Arc* (1928): Treats eyes architecturally, mostly without camera movement.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Eyes-in push: a slow, continuous move from a close-up of an old sailor's weathered face straight into his left eye until the grey-green iris and its catchlight fill the entire frame. Same man throughout, no cut, no tilt.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Stopping at a pretty full-face close-up without pushing into the iris → End with the iris filling the frame, pushing slowly and continuously while keeping the character's identity.
- Cutting to another face midway → A single continuous shot.
