# Dolly Zoom Out

Summary: The camera retreats while zooming in; the character holds its size but the background behind presses closer and grows, flattening spatial depth.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a sense of psychological distance by physically retreating while keeping the subject prominent.
- The background closes in and crowds behind the subject, a pressure of contracting space unlike a plain pull back.

## How It Works

- Dolly backward along the lens axis while lengthening the focal length (zoom in).
- Timing keeps the subject size constant; the longer focal length magnifies the background and compresses the sense of distance between foreground and background.

## When to Use

- Use: Keeping the face as the compositional focus while moving away, so the environment behind seems unnaturally close.
- Avoid: Using it as a simple headroom adjustment or an ordinary retreat.

## Film Examples

- *Poltergeist* (1982): Makes the hallway feel eerie and unnatural.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): Uses the effect to make spatial relationships unsettling.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Dolly zoom out: the camera rolls backward away from a boy standing at the end of a dark hallway while the lens zooms in to compensate, keeping him the same size while the hallway behind him looms closer and compresses, the far wall swelling up behind his shoulders. One continuous unsettling shot.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as an ordinary pull back, with the subject simply shrinking → Must combine a backward dolly with a zoom in, with clear background distortion.
