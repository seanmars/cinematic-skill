# Crash Zoom In

Summary: With the camera position fixed, the lens zooms violently onto the subject in under a second, then stops hard on a tight frame.

## Narrative Function

- Accents key moments: hits, character entrances, weapon close-ups, comedic punchlines.
- Deliberately makes the audience aware of the camera; the effect "hits" the viewer rather than approaching slowly.
- Hold after the zoom; further movement dilutes the impact.

## How It Works

- Camera body fixed, subject centered, focal length lengthens rapidly within a second.
- The zoom produces radial motion blur and lands locked on a tight frame.
- Use a fast zoom lens, or simulate the optical effect with a high-resolution crop; avoid autofocus hunting.

## When to Use

- Use: Revealing the truth, exaggerated reactions, comedic timing, clear story beats.
- Avoid: When the goal is to "move closer" through space, or internal drama that needs a slow build.

## Film Examples

- *Django Unchained* (2012): Western pastiche, using crash zooms to stress standoffs and reactions.
- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Sudden reframing for comedic effect.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Crash zoom in: from a static medium-wide shot, the lens snaps in under half a second onto the villain's eyes, with a burst of radial zoom blur. The camera body stays fixed and the subject stays centered, then the frame locks hard on the extreme close-up and holds.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a fast dolly or whip pan, with parallax or horizontal smear → Camera body stays still; only a centered zoom.
- Still shaking or moving after landing → Stop hard after the zoom, avoiding extra jitter.
