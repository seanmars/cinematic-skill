# Tilt

Summary: The camera pitches up or down from a fixed position, revealing height without moving.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals vertically stacked content from the same standpoint, e.g. from shoes to face, or from base to roof.
- Creates no spatial displacement, only changes the angle of the eyeline.

## How It Works

- Mounted on a tripod with a fluid head, pitching around the horizontal axis.
- Start and end on fully composed frames; don't overshoot unless intentional.

## When to Use

- Use: Revealing a tall subject from a fixed position.
- Avoid: When parallax or foreground shift is needed; don't substitute a tilt for a crane.

## Film Examples

- *Jurassic Park* (1993): vertical tilts convey the dinosaur's enormous scale.
- *Citizen Kane* (1941): vertical composition and tilts convey power relations.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow tilt up from a locked tripod: the frame starts on a pair of muddy boots and pitches upward along the soldier's body to his scarred face, then continues up to the towering statue behind him. Camera position stays planted, rotation only.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Done as a crane up, with foreground sliding, the support rising and the subject shrinking → lock the position and only pitch.
- Incomplete start or end frames, or overshooting → both start and end points must be fully composed frames.
