# Arc

Summary: The camera moves around the subject along a circular arc while staying aimed at it, shifting the background.

## Narrative Function

- Reorients the scene without cutting, from front to profile or reverse angle, changing spatial and power relationships.
- The subject stays relatively stable while the environment turns around it.

## How It Works

- Uses curved track, a circular dolly, or a Steadicam walking a fixed-radius arc, with the lens aimed inward at the subject.
- Height usually stays at eye or chest level.
- Commonly a quarter or half circle.

## When to Use

- Use: Switching the axis of view without cutting, e.g. revealing a two-shot arrangement at a dining table or a character behind the subject.
- Avoid: Repeated 180-degree arcs; reserve them for meaningful compositional shifts.

## Film Examples

- *Goodfellas* (1990): Moves around booths and tables, echoing shifting alliances between characters.
- *Atonement* (2007): Arcs within a long take shift perspective without cutting.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Arc shot: the camera travels a half-circle around a woman seated at a diner counter, lens locked on her face, moving from her frontal view to her profile to reveal the stranger sitting behind her. Eye-level height, constant radius, background booths sliding past with clear parallax.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a pan: the camera body stays put and only rotates, with no foreground shift → Must travel a curved path, lens locked on the subject, with clear background shift.
- Unsteady radius makes the subject grow and shrink → Keep a fixed radius and height.
