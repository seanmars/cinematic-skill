# Aerial

Summary: A high-altitude view turns geography into a readable map, with characters dwarfed by the landscape.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes the scale of the environment relative to characters or vehicles.
- Makes the location a dominant force, not just a backdrop.
- Not suited to intimate moments or scenes that need readable expressions.

## How It Works

- Uses a helicopter, drone or digital path, keeping the horizon stable.
- Adds foreground such as treetops or ridgelines to heighten the sense of altitude.
- Keeps a visible subject in frame as an anchor so it doesn't read as weather footage.

## When to Use

- Use: Emphasizing landscape over character, e.g. a single car on a mountain road, a rider on a ridgeline.
- Avoid: Using it as a default establishing shot when street-level composition already conveys the geography.

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): Opening aerial following a car along a mountain road.
- *Skyfall* (2012): Sweeping landscape and journey sequences.

## Prompt Examples

```text
High aerial shot drifting smoothly over a vast desert canyon at golden hour, the horizon perfectly level. A single red jeep crawls along a winding dirt road far below, a tiny speck against the readable map of ridges and dry riverbeds.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with FPV flythroughs: flying low through buildings, horizon rolling → Stay high, keep the subject a small dot, and keep the landscape clearly readable.
- No subject, so the frame looks like empty weather footage → Include a visible character or vehicle as an anchor.
