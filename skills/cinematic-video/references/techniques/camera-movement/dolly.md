# Dolly Shot

Summary: The camera, mounted on a wheeled platform, physically travels through space, producing real parallax between foreground, midground and background.

## Narrative Function

- Proves space through actual camera travel rather than lens magnification.
- Approaching a face, leaving a room, moving down a corridor: real movement establishes geographic relationships.

## How It Works

- The camera rides level track or wheels, fixed at eye or chest height.
- Can move forward and back along the lens axis (in / out), or laterally (truck).
- The horizon stays stable while real parallax is preserved.

## When to Use

- Use: When spatial relationships must change through camera movement.
- Avoid: Faking a dolly with a zoom.
- When track can't be laid on the ground, switch to a vest-mounted stabilizer or crane.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dolly In](dolly-in.md) | Enters space, pushing toward the subject |
| [Dolly Out](dolly-out.md) | Retreats, revealing the subject's scale within a system |
| [Tracking Shot](tracking.md) | Accompanies a moving subject, forming a continuous journey |
| [Zoom](zoom.md) | Camera body still, only focal length changes; no parallax |
| Push In | A looser push in on a vest-mounted stabilizer rather than a wheeled platform |

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): Wheeled movement turns the hotel's geometric space into a trap.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): Combines wheeled and stabilizer movement to build continuous spatial geography.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Dolly shot: the camera physically rolls forward on a smooth track at chest height down a long library aisle, shelves in the foreground sliding past faster than the reading desk at the far end. Real parallax, level horizon, no zoom.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a zoom, with near and far magnifying together and no spatial shift → Explicitly specify actual camera body travel and real parallax, and state the direction (in / out / lateral).
- Uneven track causing shake → Keep a smooth wheeled path.
