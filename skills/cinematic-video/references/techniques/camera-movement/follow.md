# Follow Shot

Summary: The camera trails close behind a moving subject; the back of the head fills the foreground as the space ahead unfolds with each step.

## Narrative Function

- Makes the audience a follower or close observer, deliberately withholding the subject's face.
- Takes the audience "into" the space with the character rather than watching from outside.
- Depending on distance and composition, can suggest a hunting or protective relationship.

## How It Works

- The camera holds a fixed distance and position behind the subject.
- Done with Steadicam, gimbal or stable handheld.
- The subject's head is a stable foreground element while the environment ahead keeps revealing itself; the path matches the subject's.

## When to Use

- Use: Chases, the unease of being stalked, or leading the audience into an environment, e.g. following a child down a long corridor.
- Avoid: Dialogue scenes that need both characters' expressions at once.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Tracking](tracking.md) | Often from the side, showing full body movement |
| [Steadicam](steadicam.md) | The rig that achieves a follow shot, moving fluidly through architecture |
| [SnorriCam](snorricam.md) | Strapped to the actor facing them; the face stays fixed while the world moves |

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): Following behind Danny, the hotel's corners appear one by one as he experiences them.
- *Enter the Void* (2009): Trailing and floating shots build the aesthetic of the afterlife.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): Entering the Copa nightclub through the back door, the space pushing forward continuously.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Follow shot from behind a small girl in a yellow raincoat walking down a dim hospital corridor, the camera trailing at a fixed distance with the back of her hood filling the lower foreground. Doors and flickering lights reveal themselves ahead as she advances; her face is never seen.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a side tracking shot or chest-mounted POV, showing profile or front face → Stay behind the subject at a fixed distance, head in the foreground, space unfolding ahead.
