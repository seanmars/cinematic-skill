# Slow Zoom In

Summary: The camera position stays completely still; only a slowly lengthening focal length tightens the frame on the subject.

## Narrative Function

- Tightens the frame optically and gradually, creating psychological pressure rather than "walking up" to the character.
- Conveys scrutiny, surveillance or isolation, with the observer staying put.
- Preserves the original spatial position, as if the audience is staring from afar.

## How It Works

- Tripod locked; camera position, angle and viewpoint unchanged throughout.
- Focal length increases smoothly over several seconds; depth of field becomes shallower, requiring synchronized focus correction.
- Spatial compression gradually intensifies, the background appearing closer to the subject, but with no parallax.

## When to Use

- Use: Building tension on a still character while keeping environmental information.
- Use: Surveillance, analytical or detached observational sequences.
- Avoid: When expressing "entering the space" or a character's epiphany; use a physical push instead.

## Film Examples

- *The Conversation* (1974): surveillance footage slowly locks onto the target in a plaza crowd.
- *Barry Lyndon* (1975): uses zooms to present painterly group compositions.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A slow optical zoom in from a locked-off tripod: the lens gradually tightens over about eight seconds onto a lone figure seated at the center of an empty office. The camera body never moves, so there is no parallax; only the background compresses closer behind the subject.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Done as a dolly push or a digital crop → make sure the body is locked and only the real focal length changes.
- Parallax or shake at the end → stop steadily on the tightest composition.
