# Flying Cam Transition

Summary: One unbroken flight passes through an architectural opening such as a wall or window and lands directly in a new room, without a cut.

## Narrative Function

- Maintains visual continuity while moving between spaces.
- Suits character entrances, dreams, or exploring connected environments as a single choreographed move.

## How It Works

- An FPV-style camera flies along a pre-planned 3D or live-action path.
- Passes through a clear threshold (doorway, window, opening) into the destination space.
- The subject waits at the destination or travels with the camera, and the move settles at the end.

## When to Use

- Use: Scene changes completed in a single flight.
- Avoid: Overusing it as the default transition between scenes.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Pass Through](pass-through.md) | Passes straight through solid matter; flying cam uses architecture as the passage |
| [FPV Drone](fpv-drone.md) | Is the flight platform; flying cam emphasizes designing the spatial join |
| [Set Transition](../editing/set-transition.md) | Broader designed join; flying cam refers specifically to architectural thresholds |

## Film Examples

- *Enter the Void* (2009): Continuous flight through walls and windows.
- *Birdman* (2014): Unbroken movement weaving through spaces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Flying cam transition: one unbroken FPV-style flight sweeps down a rainy street, dives through an open third-floor window and glides across a cozy apartment, settling gently on a girl reading by the fireplace. The window frame is clearly visible as the camera passes through.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A hidden cut on black, with the room jumping abruptly → Keep a visible flight through the specified threshold, with clearly readable spatial geography.
