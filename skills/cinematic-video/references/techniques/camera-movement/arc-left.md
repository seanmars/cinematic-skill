# Arc Left

Summary: The camera circles about 90 degrees to the left around the subject at a fixed radius, keeping the subject centered while the background shifts.

## Narrative Function

- Changes the audience's viewing angle while staying focused on the subject.
- The background turns, revealing new walls, windows or other characters.
- The subject is the center; the environment rotates around them.

## How It Works

- Moves along an arc path, keeping the same distance from the subject.
- The lens stays aimed at the subject throughout.
- Travels about 90 degrees.

## When to Use

- Use: Moving to a new axis in a dialogue or room without cutting.
- Use: Bringing in secondary characters or environmental details.
- Avoid: When a full circle is needed, or for straight-line moves that change distance.

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *Goodfellas* (1990): An arcing approach used as an emotional shift.
- *Atonement* (2007): Beach and interior sequences use arcs to rearrange crowds and spatial orientation.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Arc left around a detective standing in a cluttered office, the camera curving about 90 degrees at a steady distance while keeping him centered. As it moves, the background shifts from a window of city lights to a doorway where his partner silently appears.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a straight dolly or an uncontrolled full rotation → Keep a fixed-radius arc, limited to about 90 degrees, with the subject centered.
