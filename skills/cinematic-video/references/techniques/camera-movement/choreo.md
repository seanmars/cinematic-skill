# Choreographed Camera

Summary: The camera syncs with the performer's body into one complete phrase, moving like a dance partner rather than an observer.

## Narrative Function

- The blocking itself is the shot's rhythm; camera and actor hit the same beats.
- Suited to dance, musicals and fight choreography; not to routine coverage of ordinary scenes.

## How It Works

- Rehearse actors and camera as one unit, using Steadicam, dolly or jib.
- Set floor marks, keep a consistent tempo, usually at eye level.
- Parallax changes align with the musical beats, and everything stops together on the same point.

## When to Use

- Use: Dance, fights or walks built as a single phrase with a shared rhythm.
- Avoid: Using it as default coverage, or forcing it onto scenes that have no choreography.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Steadicam](steadicam.md) | The rig that enables the move; focus is spatial connection rather than rhythmic sync |
| [Oner](../time-and-motion/one-er.md) | Emphasizes long-take duration and structure rather than beat sync with the body |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Adds pressure through composition only; space doesn't change |

## Film Examples

- *La La Land* (2016): Musical numbers where color, movement and camera merge into one.
- *The Umbrellas of Cherbourg* (1964): Camera and actors together form choreographed phrases.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Choreographed camera: a Steadicam sweeps with two dancers across a moonlit plaza, rising, circling and pausing on the same musical beats as their spins. When the final pose lands, the camera stops in the same instant, no extra drift or leftover orbit.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The Steadicam keeps wandering after the performer finishes the phrase → Design to the same count, stop in sync, and cut extra movement.
- Gratuitous showy orbits → Keep only motivated movement synced with the body.
