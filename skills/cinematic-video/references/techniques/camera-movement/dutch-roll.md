# Dutch Roll

Summary: The camera slowly rotates around the lens axis, gradually tipping the horizon from level into a Dutch angle.

## Narrative Function

- Creates unease through "the process of tilting" rather than a frozen canted composition.
- Gravity and architecture gradually lose balance within the shot, echoing the character's worsening state.

## How It Works

- Roll only (rotation around the optical axis); pan and tilt stay still.
- Starts level and rolls gradually to a cant; can also reverse from a cant back to level.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments where unease needs to build over time, e.g. a medical episode, mental breakdown.
- Avoid: When only a fixed cant is needed (use Dutch Angle) or a continuous 360-degree rotation (use Camera Roll).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dutch Angle](../camera-angles/dutch-angle.md) | Static canted composition with no rotation process |
| [Camera Roll](camera-roll.md) | Rotates continuously until the direction of gravity disappears, or uses a rotating set |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Adds pressure by closing in on the subject, not by rotating |

## Film Examples

- *The Third Man* (1949): Canted compositions convey the disorder of postwar Vienna.
- *Do the Right Thing* (1989): Camera attitude includes rolls, conveying the neighborhood's tension.

## Prompt Examples

```text
The shot opens on a perfectly level horizon, then the camera slowly rolls around its lens axis until the frame settles into a pronounced Dutch tilt. No pan or tilt, only a gradual roll that makes the hallway feel like it is sliding off balance.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Starts already canted, or rotates past 45 degrees without stopping → Start level, roll slowly, stop at a deliberate cant.
- Mixing in pan / tilt muddies the move → Keep only the roll around the optical axis.
