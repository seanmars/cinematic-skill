# Arc Right

Summary: The camera circles about 90 degrees to the right around the subject at a fixed radius; the shifting background brings in new information on the right.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals or hides elements such as weapons, windows or characters through the background shift.
- Changes which party in a dialogue is visible, adjusting the power dynamic.
- Stop as soon as the new information appears; don't overuse.

## How It Works

- Moves along an arc, keeping a fixed distance from the subject.
- Focus stays on the subject while the background shifts.
- Must be a true circular arc, not a straight slide.

## When to Use

- Use: Revealing a new wall, window or secondary character on the right.
- Use: Moving from one speaker to another without cutting.
- Avoid: When a simple truck move would do, or when every side of an object needs inspecting.

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): Moves around bodies and vehicles, bringing in danger from new angles.
- *Goodfellas* (1990), *Atonement* (2007): Left and right arcs used together, changing the visible walls and stopping on story beats.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Arc right around a nervous bride sitting at her vanity, the camera curving roughly 90 degrees at a fixed distance with focus locked on her. The mirror behind her slides away and an open door comes into view on the right, revealing a stranger in the hallway; the move stops there.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Slides into a straight dolly right, or needlessly circles all the way around → Enforce an arc path at a fixed radius, and stop when the new element appears.
