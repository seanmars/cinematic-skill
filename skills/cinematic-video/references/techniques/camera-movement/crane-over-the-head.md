# Crane Over The Head

Summary: The crane rises until the lens points almost straight down, turning people into an overhead map.

## Narrative Function

- Ends on a downward gaze, reducing characters to a geographic pattern.
- The camera passes from eye level through an oblique angle to a near top-down view, unlike a fixed overhead shot.

## How It Works

- Starts at eye level or a three-quarter angle on a jib or Technocrane.
- Rises up and over the subject while tilting until nearly 90 degrees down.
- Booms out slightly so the subject doesn't slide out of frame.

## When to Use

- Use: Ending a sequence by looking down on a person as a spatial element.
- Avoid: Stopping at a high three-quarter angle and calling it done.

## Film Examples

- *The Untouchables* (1987): Crane rises to look down in several scenes.
- *Birdman* (2014): The crane passes over theater spaces until performers become compositional patterns.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Crane over the head: starting at eye level on a woman kneeling beside a grave, the crane rises and arcs over her until the lens points nearly straight down, turning her, the headstones and the scattered flowers into a flat overhead map. The move ends only at the top-down view.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Stopping at an oblique angle, or substituting a fixed overhead shot → Keep raising the crane until the lens points almost straight down.
