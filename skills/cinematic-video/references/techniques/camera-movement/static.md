# Static Locked-Off

Summary: The camera is fully locked off; only actors and environment move within the frame, with composition and duration telling the story.

## Narrative Function

- Relies on composition, blocking and shot length rather than camera movement.
- Builds tension through stillness: the camera won't move to "rescue" the audience, letting pressure accumulate.
- Suited to comedy, ritual and dread, when the space shouldn't change.

## How It Works

- The camera sits on a heavy tripod and doesn't move at all.
- Only actors and environmental elements move within the fixed frame.
- Use a level to ensure stability; no adjustments once rolling.

## When to Use

- Use: When blocking and rhythm themselves carry the emotion.
- Use: Domestic or ritual scenes where movement would distract.
- Avoid: When space needs exploring; also don't add slider drift just to "add life."

## Film Examples

- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): static spacecraft frames read like geometric diagrams.
- *A Quiet Place* (2018): stillness structurally amplifies subtle sounds.
- *Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles* (1975): household chores unfold one by one in an unchanging frame.
- *Caché* (2005): the opening films the house from a static, surveillance-like viewpoint.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Static locked-off shot on a heavy tripod: a wide, symmetrical view of a quiet farmhouse kitchen where an old woman slowly peels potatoes at the table. The frame never moves, drifts or zooms; only her hands, the steam from a kettle and the curtain in the breeze show motion.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Sneaking in slider drift or handheld float → lock off absolutely, with no parallax or virtual camera move.
