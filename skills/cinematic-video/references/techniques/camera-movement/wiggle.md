# Wiggle

Summary: Rocks slightly back and forth between two adjacent viewpoints, giving a static frame stereoscopic depth so it "comes alive."

## Narrative Function

- Brings life to a locked-off frame without a full camera move.
- Creates depth and parallax with minimal displacement: near objects shift slightly, the background barely moves.

## How It Works

- The nodal point moves only a few centimeters, or a pair of still images is looped alternately.
- The subject stays mostly still while the camera moves back and forth between two close positions.

## When to Use

- Use: When you want a static frame to come alive without a real camera move.
- Avoid: Actors walking around or crossing the frame.

## Film Examples

- The grammar comes from lenticular stereo photos and stereo wiggle videos on TikTok, like a moving postcard rather than a narrative camera move.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Wiggle stereo effect: a still shot of a quiet lakeside cabin rocks gently back and forth between two viewpoints just a few centimeters apart, the wooden fence in front shifting slightly while the mountains stay put. A living-postcard loop with a tiny sense of depth.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The camera moves too far, becoming a slider or dolly → only rock between two close points (centimeters, not meters).
