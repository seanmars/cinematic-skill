# Dolly Left

Summary: The whole camera slides laterally to the left with the lens direction unchanged, so foreground and background shift at different speeds.

## Narrative Function

- Surveys space or follows action without rotating the head.
- Foreground and background move at different speeds, producing layered, dimensional parallax.
- Lets new information enter gradually from frame left.

## How It Works

- The camera body translates left on track or wheels, with the lens direction fixed.
- Horizon locked, height constant throughout.
- Can keep the subject in frame while the surroundings flow, or discover a new subject along the way.

## When to Use

- Use: When there is genuinely new information on the left and "moving over" means more than "turning to look."
- Use: Sweeping past a row of objects, or accompanying a walking character.
- Avoid: When only the viewing direction needs to change; a pan is enough.

## Film Examples

- *There Will Be Blood* (2007): Lateral movement reads a room as an interconnected system.
- *In the Mood for Love* (2000): Glides along corridors, with dialogue and hesitation hidden in staggered layers of depth.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Lateral dolly left along a straight track past a row of market stalls at dusk, camera facing forward the whole time with no rotation. Hanging lanterns in the foreground slide by quickly while the distant rooftops drift slowly, horizon perfectly level.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a pan, with the horizon rotating and no real travel → Ensure a lateral translation with the lens direction unchanged.
- No foreground/background layering, so no visible parallax → Compose with objects at different depths.
