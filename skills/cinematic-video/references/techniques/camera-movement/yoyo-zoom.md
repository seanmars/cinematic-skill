# YoYo Zoom

Summary: With the camera still, the lens rapidly zooms in and back out along the same optical axis in one fluid motion.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a "hit and release" rhythm for comic timing, a sudden realization or a stinging moment.
- Like a visual breath, emphasizing coming and going.

## How It Works

- The body stays locked while the zoom lens snaps toward the subject and back.
- Both directions happen in one breath, with no dolly or body movement.

## When to Use

- Use: Comic punctuation, gags, a fleeting thought.
- Avoid: Slow, wandering optical searches, or solemn funeral-type scenes.

## Film Examples

- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): fast zooms as comic grammar, including in-and-out optical breaths.

## Prompt Examples

```text
YoYo zoom on a locked-off camera: the lens snaps in tight on a waiter's shocked face the instant the cake hits the floor, then springs straight back out to the wide shot of the dining room, all in one quick in-and-out breath. No camera movement, zoom only.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Zoom too slow, or pushing in without coming back → do a crisp two-way zoom on a locked camera position, with no wheeled movement.
