# Crash Zoom Out

Summary: With the camera position fixed, the focal length shortens violently in an instant, flinging the wider environment around the subject into frame at once.

## Narrative Function

- Suddenly reveals scale or danger, for shock or comedic effect.
- Breaks an intimate mood, letting the audience instantly see a bigger world.
- An optical reveal, not a physical camera retreat.

## How It Works

- Camera body fixed, zoom centered, so the subject doesn't drift sideways.
- Widens within a second, with radial blur during the move.
- Plan the widest landing composition in advance and stop steadily at the end.

## When to Use

- Use: Instantly revealing a startling environmental scale from a close-up.
- Use: Using strong contrast for a laugh or horror.
- Avoid: When the camera needs to physically retreat, or the newly revealed space needs layered parallax.

## Film Examples

- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): Snaps from a face out to a wider comedic scene.
- *Evil Dead*: Violent optical widening used for scares.
- *Hot Fuzz* (2007): Alternates crash zooms in and out, each one stopping to finish.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Crash zoom out: starting on a tight close-up of a surprised hiker, the lens snaps wide in a fraction of a second with radial zoom blur, instantly revealing a colossal monster looming behind him. The camera body never moves; the frame lands on a locked wide shot and holds.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a dolly out, with the foreground sliding → Lock the camera body; optical zoom only.
- Direction reversed into a push in → Confirm the landing is a wider composition first, then pull wide fast and hold.
