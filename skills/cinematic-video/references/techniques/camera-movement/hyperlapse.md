# Hyperlapse

Summary: Time-lapse combined with large camera travel through space, compressing time and distance together into a single glide.

## Narrative Function

- Compresses long stretches of time into a sense of speed along a route.
- Unlike a fixed time-lapse, the viewpoint itself moves, so the audience feels time and space compressed at once.
- Crowds and weather stream rapidly through the frame.

## How It Works

- Along a pre-planned route, shoot one frame at intervals (or build it in 3D), then assemble for high-speed playback.
- Each frame's position advances only a small step, so the motion reads as a continuous glide rather than jumps.

## When to Use

- Use: Compressing hours into a rapid approach or passage, e.g. crowds converging on a landmark over several hours.
- Avoid: Real-time dialogue or situations that only need an ordinary follow shot.

## Film Examples

- *Koyaanisqatsi* (1982): Time-compressed moving viewpoints present traffic and weather.
- *Baraka* (1992): Similar moving time-lapse sequences show crowds and urban rhythms.
- Music videos made frame-by-frame advancing camera routes a common grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Hyperlapse moving steadily down a busy city avenue toward a lit clock tower: the camera glides forward as hours compress into seconds, crowds blur into streaks and clouds race overhead, day sliding into night. Smooth continuous forward travel, no jumps.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a fixed-position time-lapse, or just a real-time follow shot with speed ramping → The camera position must advance between frames, with time compressed at the same time.
- Discontinuous travel causes jumps → Keep even spacing between steps so it reads as a smooth glide.
