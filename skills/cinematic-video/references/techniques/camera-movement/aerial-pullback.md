# Aerial Pullback

Summary: The camera rises and retreats at altitude simultaneously; the character shrinks to a dot as the landscape swallows the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Ends a sequence by handing focus from the character to the landscape.
- Depending on timing, can bring a sense of release or erasure.
- Makes geography the dominant force in the frame.

## How It Works

- Helicopter or high-altitude drone rises and retreats at the same time.
- The subject is recognizable at first, then shrinks to a dot.
- The horizon usually stays level throughout.

## When to Use

- Use: Closing a chapter on the landscape rather than a face.
- Avoid: Openings that need a readable face; when a ground crane is enough.
- Avoid: When the final frame is still a medium or full shot of the same person.

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): High-altitude pullback turns the character into a dot in the desert landscape.
- *The Lord of the Rings* (2001-2003): High-altitude pullbacks reduce characters to markers in a vast landscape.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Aerial pullback from a drone: starting close above a lone horseman crossing a salt flat, the camera rises and retreats in one continuous move with the horizon level. He stays trackable as a shrinking dot until the endless white plain and distant mountains dominate the frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a jib-sized rise, or a straight cut to an extreme wide → Keep a continuous rise plus retreat, with the subject trackable until the landscape dominates.
