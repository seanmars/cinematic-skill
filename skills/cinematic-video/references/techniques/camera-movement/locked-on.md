# Locked-On

Summary: The camera is rigidly bound to a single subject; the subject stays fixed in frame while the surroundings stream past.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes the only stable focal point in a world in total motion.
- Makes one object "the only law," with everything else flowing past it.

## How It Works

- The camera is effectively bolted to the subject or vehicle, or simulated via match-move.
- Distance and angle to the target stay nearly constant; background motion and rotation can be extreme.

## When to Use

- Use: When an object must be a stable anchor in motion, e.g. a steering wheel and dashboard with the city blurring past the window.
- Avoid: Ordinary walking follow shots in a hallway.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [SnorriCam](snorricam.md) | Mounted on the actor facing the face; Locked-On is a broader rigid lock |
| [Tracking Shot](tracking.md) | Keeps distance but the relationship can loosen; Locked-On is fully rigid |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Raises pressure without changing space |

## Film Examples

- *Gravity* (2013): the camera locks onto a falling body and helmet visor while Earth turns behind.
- *Baby Driver* (2017): locked on the driver, steering wheel and car interior while streets blur past the window.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Locked-on camera bolted to the motorcycle's handlebars: the rider's gloved hands and fuel tank stay perfectly fixed in frame at a constant angle, while the neon city streaks and tilts past at high speed.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as an ordinary Follow Shot or chest-mounted shot → state the mount point explicitly, keep subject size and angle constant, and let only the world move.
- Subject drifts or scales in frame → lock distance and angle.
