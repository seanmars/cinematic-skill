# 360-Degree Orbit

Summary: The camera circles a full revolution around the subject as center, the background flowing behind like a continuous ribbon.

## Narrative Function

- Condenses time around a single instant, such as a kiss, a threat or a product reveal.
- Shows every side of the subject and its surrounding spatial relationships in one move.
- A full circle makes "space itself" the focus, bringing romantic or predatory centripetal energy.

## How It Works

- Set a circular path with a fixed center, keeping radius and height constant (unless rising or lowering at the same time).
- Keep the subject centered throughout; no zoom during the move.
- The background wraps past as a continuous band.

## When to Use

- Use: When a moment needs to feel wrapped around one person.
- Avoid: When dialogue only needs a 90-degree turn to change the axis; use an arc.
- Avoid: When the subject rotates while the camera stays still, or showcasing a costume like a turntable.

## Film Examples

- *The Matrix* (1999): bullet-time orbit around rooftop action suspended in midair.
- *Vertigo* (1958): orbits an embracing couple as a remembered scene emerges.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A full 360-degree orbit around a samurai standing still in falling snow: the camera circles him at a constant radius and eye height in one unbroken take, keeping him centered while the forest behind wraps continuously around him. Natural real-time speed, no zoom.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The subject rotates while the background stays still, or it looks like just a pan → move the camera along a circular path with the subject roughly static.
- Distance shifts in and out during the orbit → keep a fixed radius so the background fully wraps around.
