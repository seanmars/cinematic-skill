# Falling

Summary: The camera falls through the air, accelerated by gravity, as the ground rushes up and wind floods the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Gravity itself becomes the core of the story.
- Captures jumps, slips or falls from rooftops; the camera falls with the subject, or is itself the subject.
- Duration usually matches a real fall, just a few seconds.

## How It Works

- Cables, descender rigs or digital animation accelerate the camera downward.
- A wide or medium-wide lens emphasizes the rapidly expanding ground below.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments where gravity is key, e.g. a leap, a misstep, a fall from a building.
- Avoid: Using it as a stylized transition into a room or space.

## Film Examples

- *The Walk* (2015): The POV plunges above the city.
- *Point Break* (1991): Freefall is the core of the scene, not decoration.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Falling camera: the view tips off the edge of a rooftop and plunges straight down, accelerating as the street below rushes up and swells to fill the wide frame. Wind roars, the horizon lurches, a two-second drop with real weight.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a crane descent or slow drone descent, with steady speed, stable horizon and no weight → Accelerate downward, let the ground rise, add a sense of wind, keep it brief.
- Fall drags on too long → Keep it within a few seconds, matching a real fall.
