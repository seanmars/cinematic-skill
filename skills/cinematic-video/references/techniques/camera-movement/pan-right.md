# Pan Right

Summary: The camera rotates horizontally right in place, like an onlooker turning their head, landing the eye on the next story beat.

## Narrative Function

- Presents an onlooker's view: able to turn the head, but not follow.
- The end point must land on a story beat, not on empty space.
- Not suited to fast action that needs motion blur or matched speed.

## How It Works

- Tripod locked; the fluid head rotates horizontally only (yaw).
- Keep the horizon level and decelerate smoothly near the end.
- Watch for exposure changes when turning toward bright areas such as windows.

## When to Use

- Use: Linking two elements in the same location without moving the camera position.
- Use: Turning off-screen following a line or a character's eyeline, e.g. the speaker finishes and the camera finds the listener.
- Avoid: Chase scenes that need to keep up with movement speed.

## Film Examples

- *There Will Be Blood* (2007): sweeps across interiors and wasteland, stringing themes together.
- *The Rules of the Game* (1939): fluid pans through the château, using deep focus to link class and the hunt.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow pan right on a fixed tripod across a tense boardroom: the camera starts on the CEO finishing his sentence, rotates smoothly with a level horizon, and eases to a stop on a young employee's stunned face at the far end of the table. No camera movement other than rotation.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The camera travels, creating unwanted parallax or blur → lock the tripod and do pure horizontal rotation.
- Overshooting the end composition then correcting back → set the end point in advance and stop decisively.
