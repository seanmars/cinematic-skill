# Gimbal Shot

Summary: Moving shots on a handheld motorized 3-axis stabilizer, smooth with a slightly weightless, floating feel.

## Narrative Function

- Moves fluidly without a bulky Steadicam vest.
- Motorized stabilization (rather than mechanical springs) produces a light, floating texture.
- Not suited to scenes that need handheld-shake realism or rehearsed architectural paths.

## How It Works

- A 3-axis motorized head with IMU sensors stabilizes the image.
- The operator carries it on foot or in a vehicle; the horizon can be electronically locked.
- While electronically stabilized, boom and pan can be done manually.

## When to Use

- Use: Mobile shooting, e.g. up and down stairs, through crowds or vehicles, while staying smooth.
- Use: When mobility is needed without laying track or wearing a vest.
- Avoid: Scenes that need handheld energy or an organic breathing feel.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Steadicam](steadicam.md) | Mechanical vest and spring arm, suited to long architectural paths |
| [Handheld](handheld.md) | Keeps visible shake and documentary realism |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Adds pressure by tightening composition rather than moving |

## Film Examples

- *1917* (2019): Mixes several stabilization rigs; the gimbal lets the camera move freely through crowds and complex routes.
- *Extraction* (2020): Motorized stabilization for action sequences through crowds and vehicles.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Gimbal shot: a camera operator runs down a crowded subway staircase behind a courier, the image gliding smoothly with the horizon electronically locked. No footstep bounce and no handheld shake, just a sleek, modern floating motion weaving between commuters.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as raw handheld or a full Steadicam effect → Explicitly specify a motorized gimbal, locked horizon, hand-carried, with no footstep bounce or jitter.
