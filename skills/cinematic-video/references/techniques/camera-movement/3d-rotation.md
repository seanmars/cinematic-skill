# 3D Rotation

Summary: A virtual camera circles a character in a studio or void, as if inspecting a sculpture on an imaginary turntable.

## Narrative Function

- Presents the subject as an object to be inspected.
- Works best when the audience expects to see the back.
- Not suited to chases or blocked dialogue in a room.

## How It Works

- The virtual camera moves along a circular or spherical path while the subject stays still.
- Usually in a controlled environment (studio or void background), rotating on a single axis.

## When to Use

- Use: Inspecting a character or prop like a product or model.
- Avoid: Chases, or scenes that need real spatial continuity.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [360-Degree Orbit](orbit-360.md) | Walks around the subject in a real environment; 3D rotation is in a virtual studio space |
| [Lazy Susan](lazy-susan.md) | Camera stays still while the physical object spins on a turntable; 3D rotation is a virtual camera circling |

## Film Examples

- Product videos are the native use.
- *The Matrix* (1999): Frozen-time orbit shots treat bodies as sculptures in a computational space.

## Prompt Examples

```text
3D rotation: a virtual camera circles a dancer frozen mid-leap on an invisible turntable in a seamless white void, revolving smoothly on a single axis until her back and then her face come fully into view. Sculptural, clean, a complete 360-degree reveal.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A small arc in a street scene, with room geometry shifting slightly but the back never shown → Commit fully to turntable composition, with the subject placed like a sculpture in a studio or void.
