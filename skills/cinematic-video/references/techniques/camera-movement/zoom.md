# Zoom

Summary: The camera body stays still and only the focal length changes; space flattens with no parallax.

## Narrative Function

- Changes subject size from a fixed position, serving as surveillance, emphasis or punctuation.
- The background doesn't shift; it only takes up more or less of the frame.

## How It Works

- Camera fixed (usually on a tripod), using a zoom lens.
- Zoom in tends toward a telephoto look; zoom out tends toward a wide-angle look.
- All planes scale uniformly, with no parallax or near-object distortion.

## When to Use

- Use: Changing size without moving position, e.g. surveillance footage, emphasizing a face, gradual tightening.
- Avoid: When actual movement through the environment needs to be shown.

## Film Examples

- *The Conversation* (1974): optical magnification creates a sense of eavesdropping and surveillance.
- *Jaws* (1975): the famous shot is actually a dolly zoom, not a pure zoom; useful for comparison.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow optical zoom in from a fixed tripod across a crowded town square, narrowing onto a man in a grey coat sitting on a bench. The camera never moves; every plane enlarges evenly and the space flattens, with no parallax at all.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with a dolly, with parallax such as lamps shifting and doorframes sliding → lock the camera position, adjust only focal length, and let all planes scale uniformly.
