# Dolly Zoom

Summary: The subject's size stays constant while background perspective stretches or compresses, throwing "space itself" off balance.

## Narrative Function

- Externalizes inner states such as terror, realization or vertigo as spatial distortion, rather than relying on facial expression alone.
- The contrast of a stable subject and an unstable world makes the audience feel "the ground has shifted."
- Its impact comes from rarity; once per sequence is enough.

## How It Works

- The camera advances (or retreats) along the axis while the lens zooms in the opposite direction (retreat + zoom in, or advance + zoom out).
- The two speeds must match so the subject's size in frame stays constant throughout.
- The background compresses / stretches in perspective as focal length changes; focus must be adjusted in sync with both movements.

## When to Use

- Use: The moment a character suddenly realizes danger, the truth or their own situation within a space.
- Use: When the environment itself should feel unstable and threatening.
- Avoid: Repeating it as an ordinary push in or a default stylistic device.

## Film Examples

- *Vertigo* (1958): The vertiginous view down the stairwell.
- *Jaws* (1975): The police chief's reaction shot on the beach as he realizes the shark is there.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): The background gradually distorts during a diner conversation, hinting at the character's tightening situation.
- *La Haine* (1995): Perspective dislocation between the characters and the city background on a rooftop.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Dolly zoom (vertigo effect): the camera physically dollies backward while the lens zooms in, keeping the subject exactly the same size in frame. The background visibly looms closer and compresses behind the still subject. Single continuous shot, no cut, not an ordinary push-in.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a zoom or a digital stretch in post, with no real camera travel → Must have both an opposing dolly and zoom.
- Subject size visibly changes during the move → Match the subject size at the start and end.
- Background distortion looks like a warp filter → Should be a real change in perspective geometry.
