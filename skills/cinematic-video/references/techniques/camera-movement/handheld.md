# Handheld

Summary: The camera is carried directly on the body, keeping breathing and subtle shake in the frame for an immediate sense of urgency.

## Narrative Function

- Creates urgency, documentary-style realism and psychological agitation.
- The operator's physicality becomes visible: breathing, tremor, shifting attention.
- Conveys vulnerability and the immediacy of "being there."

## How It Works

- The camera rides on the shoulder, against the chest or under the arm, with no stabilizer.
- Body weight, breathing and micro-movements all remain in the image.
- Constant slight shake makes focus and exposure more challenging; it can't be corrected with digital stabilization.

## When to Use

- Use: Raids, crowds, disorienting moments.
- Use: Documentary or mockumentary (found footage) styles, and psychological scenes of panic and agitation.
- Avoid: Product stills, formal presentations, or any shot that needs absolute stability.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Steadicam](steadicam.md) | Mechanically isolates footsteps, moving smoothly as if floating |
| [Gimbal](gimbal.md) | Electronic stabilization removes roll and jitter; handheld keeps the operator's physical traces |

## Film Examples

- *The Battle of Algiers* (1966): Shoots street raids with a rough newsreel texture.
- *Children of Men* (2006): The in-car ambush sequence, with the camera path inside the car designed around the passengers' viewpoints.
- *Children of Men* (2006): The operator follows the protagonist through a riot-torn district.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Handheld shoulder-mounted camera pushing through a panicked crowd in a smoky train station, with natural breathing sway and small jolts as the operator steps. Documentary immediacy with visible film grain; no gimbal smoothing or electronic stabilization.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Adding a shake plugin to tripod or gimbal footage, which feels hollow and weightless → Shoot true handheld, keeping the operator's weight and breathing.
