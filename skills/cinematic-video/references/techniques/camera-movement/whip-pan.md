# Whip Pan

Summary: The lens whips horizontally at extreme speed, smearing into motion blur midway, then lands cleanly on a new frame.

## Narrative Function

- Serves as a transition, a gag, or a sudden shift of attention.
- Carries energy between non-adjacent spaces.
- When the blur is matched well, even mundane scene changes become comic rhythm.

## How It Works

- Pan horizontally at extreme speed until the frame becomes unreadable motion blur.
- If cutting mid-whip, blur direction, brightness, duration and acceleration on both sides must match.
- The landing composition must be clear and deliberate.

## When to Use

- Use: Transitions between sequences or comic accents.
- Use: Attention shifts with clear start and landing points.
- Avoid: Whipping past details or information the audience needs to see; showing a journey across a landscape.

## Film Examples

- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): fast transitions compress daily routines and travel.
- *Hot Fuzz* (2007): whip-like moves speed up plot exposition.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Whip pan: the camera holds on a chef plating a dish, then snaps violently to the right, the kitchen smearing into heavy horizontal motion blur, before landing crisply on a waiter bursting through the swinging doors. The middle of the move is completely unreadable.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Details still readable in the blurred middle → fully blur the middle.
- Blur direction or brightness mismatched across the cut, exposing the seam → align blur direction and brightness.
- Unclear landing → finish on a sharp, decisive composition.
