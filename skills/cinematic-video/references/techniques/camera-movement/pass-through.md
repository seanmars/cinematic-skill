# Pass Through

Summary: The camera continuously passes through walls, bodies or windows, as if matter were freely permeable.

## Narrative Function

- Grants omniscient spatial access without cutting.
- Treats architecture as a mechanical structure to move through.
- The barrier's thickness must briefly fill the lens, or it feels like cheating.

## How It Works

- Build a set with removable walls, or plan the path in 3D and composite, preserving the object's thickness and shape.
- Keep lens optics consistent before and after.
- Speed ramps can make the pass-through smoother.

## When to Use

- Use: Entering an adjacent room without cutting, or emphasizing structural relationships.
- Avoid: As a default transition between two unrelated shots.

## Film Examples

- *Enter the Void* (2009): flies through Tokyo rooms and bodies.
- *Fight Club* (1999): a house tour treats the home as a continuous mechanical structure.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Pass-through shot: the camera drifts across a quiet living room and pushes straight through the plaster wall, its thickness of brick and pipes filling the frame for a beat, then emerges into the neighbor's candlelit kitchen. One continuous path, no cut.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Hiding a cut in black frames or behind a foreground occlusion, looking like an instant room change → keep the pass-through visible and the barrier's three-dimensional thickness.
- Not establishing the arrival space → explicitly state the barrier crossed and the space on the other side.
