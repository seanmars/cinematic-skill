# Conveyor Camera

Summary: The camera moves laterally at a relentless constant speed, treating the world as a factory conveyor belt of repeating units.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys industrial process, labor as machinery, inescapable inevitability.
- Can serve comedy or paranoia; subjects are treated as cargo, not companions.

## How It Works

- Moves laterally on a dolly, slider or treadmill-style rig at a metronomic fixed speed.
- A wide or medium-wide lens frames repeating units, with foreground machinery passing faster than the background.
- Industrial lighting with hard practical sources; little beauty lighting.

## When to Use

- Use: Factories, assembly processes, systemic dehumanization.
- Avoid: Casual sidewalk dialogue, or following a route the character chooses.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Tracking Shot](tracking.md) | Accompanies the character's own journey; conveyor ignores the character's will |

## Film Examples

- *Modern Times* (1936): Chaplin's body trapped on the factory production line.
- *The Parallax View* (1974): Industrial geometry conveys entrapment.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Conveyor camera: a relentless lateral track at a fixed, metronomic speed past an endless row of identical workers at sewing machines under hard fluorescent light. Foreground machinery streaks by faster than the background, and the camera never slows or stops for anyone.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as politely accompanying trucking → Enforce constant speed and repeating units, treating the subject as cargo, not a companion.
- Speed rises and falls with the character → Keep a mechanical fixed pace.
