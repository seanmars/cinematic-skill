# Earth Zoom

Summary: One continuous zoom from satellite altitude down to a person's face, compressing nested geographic scales along a single axis.

## Narrative Function

- Places a person as a point on Earth, or gets a laugh from the scale gap between cosmos and individual.
- Needs ordered landmarks (cloud layer, coastline, street grid, rooftop, face) and visual continuity.
- Usually used once per film.

## How It Works

- Build nested environments and scale transitions in 3D or compositing, keeping consistent direction and visual anchors.
- The path is a single continuous zoom, with no pan and no lateral orbit.

## When to Use

- Use: Placing a person within Earth's geography in one move, or for comedic scale contrast.
- Avoid: Opening a dialogue scene or dialogue sequence.

## Film Examples

- *Contact* (1997): The opening works in reverse, pulling back from Earth to cosmic scale.
- *Powers of Ten* (1977): Starting from picnickers, a two-way scale journey outward and then inward.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Earth zoom: one continuous dive along a single axis from orbit above the Pacific, down through the cloud layer, over a coastline, into a city grid, onto a rooftop, and finally stopping on the face of a girl looking up at the sky. Smooth nested scale transitions, no panning.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Just an ordinary aerial or a snap zoom from a rooftop, lacking continental scale, or the final face looks like a stranger → Start from orbital altitude, keep the nested scales in order, and maintain the character's identity throughout.
