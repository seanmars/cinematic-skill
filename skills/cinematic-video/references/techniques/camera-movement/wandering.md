# Wandering Camera

Summary: The camera drifts and explores space with curiosity rather than aiming at a predetermined subject.

## Narrative Function

- "Looking" itself becomes the main action; discovery matters more than preset composition.
- Once the camera locks on and moves toward a target, the wandering effect ends.

## How It Works

- Steadicam or handheld, moving at human eye level with no fixed axis.
- Movement speed is deliberately irregular.
- The frame first sweeps over extras and environmental details before finally settling.

## When to Use

- Use: Opening sequences, or exploration and discovery scenes where the camera hasn't yet chosen a focus.
- Avoid: Scenes where blocking and spatial geography are already central to the story.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Handheld](handheld.md) | Conveys urgency; body shake but can still keep aiming |
| [Steadicam](steadicam.md) | Smoothly floats through architecture, serving the story rather than exploration |
| [Slow Zoom In](slow-zoom-in.md) | Raises tension without changing spatial position |

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): opens with discovery-style framing before events unfold.
- *The Player* (1992): wandering camerawork gradually shifts into structured narrative.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Wandering camera at eye level drifts curiously through a cluttered antique shop, glancing over dusty clocks, a sleeping cat and a customer's hands, pausing, changing direction, then slowly discovering an old woman watching from behind the counter. Unhurried, uneven pace, no fixed target.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The camera seems to chase the protagonist, or follows a predetermined route → let the camera miss details first, then discover the subject naturally.
