# Mouth In

Summary: The camera pushes into a speaking or eating mouth until lips and teeth become the set.

## Narrative Function

- Makes speech, hunger or a kiss feel like a "location" rather than a facial detail.
- The mouth becomes architecture and landscape.
- Unlike a random gore insert shot, the film must first establish the mouth as a space.

## How It Works

- The camera pushes into the mouth, lips as foreground, interior as destination.
- Light the teeth as architectural elements.
- Done in a single unbroken move, without rack focus or whip pan.

## When to Use

- Use: Treating appetite, a spoken threat or a kiss as a set.
- Avoid: Unmotivated body horror without supporting context.

## Film Examples

- *The Cook, the Thief, His Wife & Her Lover* (1989): treats the mouth and appetite as space.
- *Requiem for a Dream* (2000): treats bodily orifices and desire as sets.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Mouth-in shot: one continuous push toward a villain's grin as he whispers a threat, the camera sliding past his cracked lips until his teeth tower like lit marble columns around the lens and the dark throat becomes the space ahead.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Stopping on a static portrait showing teeth, never entering the mouth → the end point must pass the lips and treat teeth as environment; don't stop at a medium close-up.
