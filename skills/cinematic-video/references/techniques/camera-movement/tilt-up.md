# Tilt Up

Summary: The camera stays in place and only raises the lens, revealing from low details all the way up to the subject or scale above.

## Narrative Function

- Delays height, spectacle or a character's face until the end of the shot.
- Links details on the ground to a source above.
- Forces the audience to look up, making "scale" a relative relationship.

## How It Works

- The camera is fixed on a pivot, doing pitch rotation only, with lens height unchanged.
- Compose the low start frame and high end frame in advance.
- If it will tilt into a bright sky, handle the exposure difference beforehand.

## When to Use

- Use: Revealing building height, huge objects, or delaying the reveal of a face.
- Use: Building visual beats, e.g. boots → rifle → face.
- Avoid: Pretending the camera is rising; use a crane when an actual height climb is needed.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Crane Up](crane-up.md) | Camera physically rises through space, with parallax |
| [Pedestal Up](pedestal-up.md) | Vertical rise creates parallax; tilt up stays in place |
| [Tilt Down](tilt-down.md) | Same axis, opposite direction |

## Film Examples

- *Psycho* (1960): vertical rotation links the motel to the house on the hill.
- *Besieged* (1998): vertical movement links different floors of the house.
- *Jurassic Park* (1993): tilts up so the audience feels the dinosaur's size as they recognize it.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow tilt up from a fixed tripod: the shot begins on muddy armored boots planted in the grass, then pitches upward past a dented breastplate to a scarred knight's face against a stormy sky. No camera rise or sideways movement, only rotation.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Accidentally made into a crane up or pedestal up, with eye height changing and foreground parallax → fix the tripod height and only tilt upward.
- No new information to reveal above → bring the real focal point in at the top of the frame.
