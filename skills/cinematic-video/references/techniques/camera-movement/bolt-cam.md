# Bolt Cam

Summary: An ultra-high-speed camera is flung along a rail or robotic arm toward the point of impact, then stops dead.

## Narrative Function

- Makes the audience feel physical force "arrive"; the impact has mass, not just a tighter frame.
- The camera itself is the blow, with near objects smearing heavily in motion.

## How It Works

- The camera rushes toward a target (pad, body, ball) at tens of meters per second via a crash rail, cable or robotic arm.
- On arrival it stops dead or settles in a programmed way.
- A fast shutter captures the violent parallax produced by real camera travel.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments that need emphasized impact, e.g. punches, kicks, crash tests, musical downbeats.
- Avoid: Decorative extreme wides that never contact a surface.
- Avoid: Introspective, quiet moments.

## Film Examples

- The grammar comes mainly from sports impact shots and music video crash sequences.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Bolt cam: a high-speed robotic arm hurls the camera toward a boxer's fist as it lands on a heavy bag, foreground ropes smearing with violent parallax, then the camera snaps to a dead stop at the moment of contact. Ultra-high frame rate, sweat droplets hanging in the air.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as a crash zoom, with no parallax and a magnified background → The camera body must physically travel on the rail, with foreground smear, stopping at the point of contact.
- Rushing toward nothing → Specify the impact target and the moment of contact.
