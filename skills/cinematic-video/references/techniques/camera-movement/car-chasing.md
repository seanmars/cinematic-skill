# Car Chasing

Summary: A chase shot from another vehicle matching speed; the road is the environment and a dynamic forms between the two machines.

## Narrative Function

- Builds tension through the interaction between the two vehicles.
- Both vehicles are moving, so the environment streaks heavily.
- Must be a real chase; a parked car or a single vehicle doesn't count.

## How It Works

- Shot from a camera car, or with a picture car matching speed on a virtual path.
- A long lens compresses the distance between the vehicles; a wide lens shows both vehicles and the road.
- Keep a consistent axis of action throughout.

## When to Use

- Use: Chase sequences.
- Avoid: Parked-car dialogue, or beauty shots of a single vehicle.

## Film Examples

- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): Maintains the relationship between pursuing vehicles across the desert landscape.
- *The French Connection* (1971): Chase dynamics of a car pursuing an elevated train.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Car chase shot from a camera vehicle matching speed on a coastal highway: a battered black sedan swerves ahead while a police cruiser closes in on its bumper. Long lens compresses the gap between them, guardrails and palm trees streaking past with strong parallax.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a single-vehicle beauty shot, with no second vehicle and no motion blur → Ensure another vehicle is truly giving chase, and show environmental parallax.
- Axis of action jumping around → Keep a consistent direction of travel.
