# Car Grip

Summary: The camera is locked directly to the car's exterior, with bodywork in the foreground and the road racing by below.

## Narrative Function

- Makes the machine itself the subject through a rigid connection to the car body.
- Establishes an inseparable bond between car and camera, emphasizing the car as the main subject.

## How It Works

- Mounted with suction cups, a hostess rig or a cage on the hood, door, bumper or wheel arch.
- A wide lens shows bodywork and asphalt advancing in the same motion.
- Dust and mud flecks on the lens are acceptable.

## When to Use

- Use: When the vehicle itself is the subject, or a chase needs a car-body POV.
- Avoid: Dialogue scenes inside the cabin.

## Film Examples

- *Ronin* (1998): Hood and door mounts, bodywork always prominent while the streets fly by.
- *Baby Driver* (2017): Also uses car-body mounts to show driving dynamics.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Car grip shot: a wide-angle camera clamped low on the front fender of a vintage muscle car racing through the desert, gleaming chrome and red paint filling the near foreground while the cracked asphalt rushes beneath. Dust flecks on the lens, the car rigidly locked in frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Floating follow shot from another car, with no bodywork visible → Mount the camera directly on the subject car, keep bodywork in the foreground, and let the road slide by below.
