# Super Dolly In

Summary: A long, high-speed forward camera move that violently compresses spatial depth until the environment vanishes into a tight facial close-up.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys threat, obsession or psychological intensity through accelerating spatial collapse.
- The aggressive approach breaks polite distance; the subject arrives "too fast," causing physical discomfort.

## How It Works

- A long tracking or Steadicam move along the lens axis.
- Usually starts on a wide shot, swallowing as much environment as possible.
- Travels at high speed, ending at extremely close range.

## When to Use

- Use: When a gentle push isn't strong enough, e.g. corridors, threats, invasive focus.
- Avoid: Restrained contemplative or tender moments.

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): super dolly down a corridor, swallowing space before entering a confrontation.
- *Oldboy* (2003): corridor-style high-speed push, full of momentum and aggression.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Super dolly in: starting wide at the far end of a long, flickering hospital corridor, the camera rockets forward along the lens axis, walls and doorways tearing past with violent parallax, and slams into an extreme close-up of a nurse's wide, frightened eyes.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Done as a crash zoom or a short push → needs a sustained physical move path, obvious parallax distortion, and arrival at the face "too early."
