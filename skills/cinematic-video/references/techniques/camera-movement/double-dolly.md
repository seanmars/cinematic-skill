# Double Dolly

Summary: Actor and camera stand on the same moving platform; the character's feet don't move yet they seem to glide through the environment.

## Narrative Function

- Externalizes inner states such as fate, fear or transcendence; the character seems "carried" forward by some thought.
- The unnatural gliding makes the audience sense the character has left the everyday logic of walking.

## How It Works

- A hidden platform (physical or virtual) carries both actor and camera, moving at the same speed.
- The actor's feet stay still and body upright; only the surroundings flow.
- The subject's size in frame stays fixed.

## When to Use

- Use: Key moments when a character is pushed by psychological force, e.g. walking toward fate or violence.
- Avoid: Ordinary walk-and-talk dialogue scenes.
- Avoid: When the actor walks at a normal pace.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |

## Film Examples

- *BlacKkKlansman* (2018): Armed characters glide down a corridor.
- *Malcolm X* (1992): A stylized glide approaches the assassination sequence.
- *Inside Man* (2006): Reaction shot of a dazed character drifting after the hostage incident.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Double dolly shot: the man and the camera ride the same hidden platform, so he glides down the dim street upright with his feet perfectly still while storefronts slide past behind him. Constant medium framing, no walking bob, a solemn sense of being carried toward fate.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The actor shows a normal walking motion, or stays frozen in place → Must share a platform, feet still, background continuously flowing, body upright.
- Frame bobs up and down like handheld → Keep a smooth glide with no step bounce.
