# Trunk Shot

Summary: The camera hides in a car trunk or box looking up as people lean over the opening; the audience becomes the hidden thing.

## Narrative Function

- Inverts power dynamics: those standing dominate the space, while the camera holds a secret vantage point.
- Places the audience in the position of a hidden object or trapped person, creating vulnerability and curiosity.
- The container's rim and opening must be visible to distinguish it from an ordinary low angle.

## How It Works

- Camera placed low inside the container, keeping the container's rim in frame.
- Actors' eyelines point down at the lens position inside the container.
- Top light motivated by the open lid creates shadow contrast inside the container.

## When to Use

- Use: When a character is actually in the trunk, or when opening a container delivers a reveal.
- Avoid: Merely decorating standing figures without any real container context.

## Film Examples

- *Reservoir Dogs* (1992): Characters look down into an open trunk.
- *Pulp Fiction* (1994): Jules and Vincent seen from inside the trunk.
- *From Dusk Till Dawn* (1996) / *Kill Bill* (2003): Reuse the same "pit + lid" grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Trunk shot from inside a car trunk looking up, the dark rim of the trunk opening framing the shot and the raised lid visible above. Two men lean over and stare straight down into the lens, lit from behind by a harsh parking-lot sodium lamp; the camera stays still inside the trunk.
```

## Common Mistakes

- An ordinary low angle in a parking lot, with full standing bodies in frame and no container rim or lid → There must be a rim framing the shot, faces leaning in and downward eyelines, with the camera staying inside the container.
