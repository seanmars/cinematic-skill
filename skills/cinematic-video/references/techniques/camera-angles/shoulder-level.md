# Shoulder Level

Summary: The camera sits slightly below the eyes, around shoulder height, like a companion walking alongside.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a close, informal sense of companionship, following the character through space.
- Lacks the formal equality of eye level and the chaos of handheld.
- Common in follow sequences; the audience walks "with" the character rather than watching from afar.

## How It Works

- Camera about 5-20 cm below the subject's eyes, roughly at shoulder or collarbone height.
- Horizon nearly level, no deliberate upward tilt.
- 35mm works well; use a Steadicam or gimbal to hold height and remove shake.

## When to Use

- Use: Following a character as a companion through interior and exterior spaces, such as a soldier on patrol or a host leading the way.
- Avoid: Using it as a substitute for a deliberate handheld aesthetic.
- Avoid: When you need formal framing for a conversation between equals (use eye level).

## Film Examples

- *Goodfellas* (1990): The walk into the Copacabana kitchen lets the audience arrive with Henry rather than through his POV.
- *The Hurt Locker* (2008): Holds soldiers' shoulder height through streets and rooms, sharing the experience of walking.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shoulder-level follow shot, camera held just below a young medic's eye line at collarbone height, walking beside her through a crowded field hospital tent. Nearly level horizon, no upward tilt, smooth gimbal movement, no foreground shoulder in frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Aligned with eye height (too formal) or tilted up into a low angle → Place at shoulder height, barely tilted up, keeping companion distance.
- A foreground shoulder fills the frame → That is an OTS, not shoulder level.
- The subject's hands reach into frame as if from a POV → If that is the intent, use first-person instead.
