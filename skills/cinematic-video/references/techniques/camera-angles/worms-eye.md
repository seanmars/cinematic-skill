# Worm's-Eye View

Summary: The lens sits on or below the ground tilting sharply up, verticals converging toward the zenith as the subject becomes a towering structure.

## Narrative Function

- Creates overwhelming scale, conveying awe, intimidation or vulnerability.
- Beyond power dynamics, the subject itself becomes architecture.

## How It Works

- Lens placed on the floor, in a pit or at ground level.
- Tilt up more than 60 degrees; use an ultra-wide lens to heighten the collapse of verticals.
- The foreground can include dirt, curbs or grates; body proportions must still be plausible.

## When to Use

- Use: Megastructures, spaceships, or characters who should make the audience feel small.
- Avoid: Ordinary power-dynamic dialogue shots (a low angle is enough).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |

## Film Examples

- *Citizen Kane* (1941): Extreme upward interior angles with oppressive ceilings.
- *Arrival* (2016): The alien ship sequences use upward angles to emphasize human smallness.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Worm's-eye view from a lens resting on wet pavement, tilted almost straight up at a skyscraper and a lone figure standing at its base. Ultra-wide lens, building edges converging sharply toward the sky, a storm drain grate in the near foreground.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot only as a mild low angle with the camera at waist height → Put the camera on the ground, looking nearly straight up.
