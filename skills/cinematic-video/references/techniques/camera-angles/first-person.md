# First-Person

Summary: The camera is the character's body, with hands, breath, tools and footsteps in frame, sustained over time.

## Narrative Function

- Lets the audience "inhabit" the character's body for an extended time.
- Builds a continuous subjective experience, not just a glimpse, through visible hands, breath, tools and movement.

## How It Works

- Camera fixed at the character's head or upper chest, using natural focal lengths (24-35mm).
- Hands, shoulders or tools appear at the frame edges.
- Add subtle sway mimicking breath and footsteps; never show the character's face except in a mirror reflection.

## When to Use

- Use: Action sequences or perception-driven moments where the audience needs to experience it firsthand.
- Avoid: Two-person dialogue scenes; the relationship with the other person is lost.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [POV](pov.md) | Shows only what is seen, fully excluding the viewer's body |
| [Object POV](object-pov.md) | Camera belongs to a prop rather than a person |
| [Over-the-Shoulder Frame](../framing/over-the-shoulder-frame.md) | Views from behind a visible character |

## Film Examples

- *Lady in the Lake* (1947): An early experiment telling a feature-length story through the detective's eyes.
- *Enter the Void* (2009): Consciousness after death travels through space in first person.
- *Hardcore Henry* (2015): Full first-person action with weapons and hand impacts clearly visible.

## Prompt Examples

```text
First-person view from a climber's head height, both gloved hands gripping the rock at the bottom edges of frame, breath fogging slightly in cold air. Subtle rhythmic sway with each reach upward, the climber's face never visible, the valley dropping away below.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a clean POV with no body elements, or as a follow shot showing the character's back → Let hands or tools occupy the frame edges and exclude the character's own face.
