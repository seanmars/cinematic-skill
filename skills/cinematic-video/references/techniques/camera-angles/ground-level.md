# Ground Level

Summary: The lens sits on the ground, making boots, hems, tires and pavement the subject as the world rises sharply from the ground plane.

## Narrative Function

- Makes things on the ground the main visual subject.
- Common in westerns and car films: decisive things often land on the ground.

## How It Works

- Use a low board, sandbags or a dug-out pit to keep the lens just a few centimeters off the ground.
- A wide lens exaggerates the near foreground, contrasting with the distant horizon.
- Keep gravel, puddles or pavement texture in the foreground to reinforce the extremely low camera position.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments that happen at ground level, such as arrivals on foot, vehicle movement or dropped objects.
- Avoid: Ordinary walk-and-talks (use shoulder level or eye level).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Worm's-Eye View](worms-eye.md) | Extreme upward angle from the ground; verticals collapse, almost no horizon |
| [Low Angle](low-angle.md) | Below the eyes looking up, but need not touch the ground |
| [Hip Level](hip-level.md) | Belt height, hands on the focal plane |
| [Overhead](overhead.md) | Opposite extreme: the ground becomes a floor plan, not foreground |
| [Eye Level](eye-level.md) | Neutral default; ground level requires deliberately lowering the camera |

## Film Examples

- *Once Upon a Time in the West* (1968): In the opening, platform, rails and boots dominate the frame; the ground carries the sense of time.
- *Drive* (2011): Cars, asphalt and shoes fill the foreground; the street is the main world.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Ground-level shot with the lens resting a few centimeters above cracked desert asphalt, gravel and dust sharp in the foreground. A pair of worn boots steps into frame and stops, horizon sitting very low, a distant truck approaching along the road.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shot as a low angle from a kneeling position, with a clean floor and visible faces → Place the lens directly on the ground with pavement texture or debris in the foreground.
- Frame is mostly sky and faces → It has become a worm's-eye view; bring the line of sight back to the level ground.
