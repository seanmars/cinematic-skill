# Fourth Wall

Summary: A character looks directly into the lens, acknowledging the audience and pulling the onlooker into the scene as an accomplice.

## Narrative Function

- The audience shifts from invisible observer to participant in the scene.
- Builds complicity between performer and audience.

## How It Works

- Camera at the subject's eye height, pupils aimed at the center of the lens.
- All other blocking stays naturalistic; the direct look is the only break from realism.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments where the story's contract requires audience awareness, such as confession, explanation, provocation or a knowing aside.
- Avoid: Random, unmotivated glances; the break must materially change how the scene works.

## Film Examples

- *Annie Hall* (1977): The protagonist steps out of the realistic narrative to argue directly with the audience.
- *Ferris Bueller's Day Off* (1986): The protagonist explains his tricks and thoughts directly to the audience.
- *Funny Games* (1997): Strategic direct looks make the audience complicit in the violence.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A young woman at a crowded party suddenly turns and looks straight into the lens, both pupils centered on camera, at her eye level, and gives a knowing half-smile. The guests around her keep chatting naturally and never glance at the camera.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Eyeline drifts beside the lens (becoming an interview), or shot as the character's view instead of them looking into the lens → Aim pupils straight into the lens so the audience feels directly addressed, not watching an off-screen conversation.
