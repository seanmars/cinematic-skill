# Three-Quarter Angle

Summary: The face turns about 45 degrees; both eyes are visible, the far cheek recedes, and the head shows three-dimensional volume.

## Narrative Function

- Provides a dimensional portrait while maintaining connection with an off-screen conversation partner.
- Sits between frontal and pure profile; facial structure is ample for lighting and performance.

## How It Works

- Camera 30-50 degrees off the axis of the subject's nose.
- Both irises are in frame; only a little of the ear shows.
- 50-85mm keeps head proportions undistorted; light from the near or far side depending on the desired modeling.

## When to Use

- Use: The default angle for dramatic dialogue when the head needs volume.
- Avoid: When you want to withhold information (use profile instead).
- Avoid: When a direct look into the lens is needed (use fourth wall instead).

## Film Examples

- *Heat* (1995): The diner scene shoots both leads from this angle, with distinct head volume and clear screen direction.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Three-quarter angle close-up of a young woman turned about 45 degrees from camera, both irises visible, far cheek falling away, only her near ear showing. She looks toward an off-screen partner on camera-left; 75mm lens at eye height, soft key from the far side.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turned too far into a profile, or too little into a frontal shot → Aim for 45 degrees, with both eyes visible and only one dominant ear.
- Conflating vertical pitch with horizontal rotation → Describe height and yaw separately.
