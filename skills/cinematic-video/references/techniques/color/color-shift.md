# Color Shift

Summary: Hue changes visibly within a shot or across a threshold, marking a transition of world, truth or memory.

## Narrative Function

- Uses a visible hue change to mark the transition between different worlds, lies or memories.
- Lets the audience "cross the threshold" together with the character.
- Unlike a fixed palette per chapter, the point is the event of "change".

## How It Works

- Define the before / after color states first and match exposure.
- Make the change through a cut, a door opening, or a gradual in-shot grade transition.
- Production design must support the arrival of the new color so it reads as deliberate.

## When to Use

- Use: When the color change is itself a story event, e.g. a new world behind a door, or a color temperature change revealing deception.
- Avoid: When chapters simply keep different fixed palettes.
- Avoid: Faking it with cross process or split toning that has no transition.

## Film Examples

- *The Wizard of Oz* (1939): The farmhouse door opens and the colors of Oz appear.
- *Stalker* (1979): The outside world and "the Zone" are treated completely differently.

## Prompt Examples

```text
In-shot color shift: the scene begins in cold, desaturated grey-blue, and as the character pushes open the door the grade travels smoothly into warm, saturated gold and green. Clear before and after states, with the doorway as the visible threshold of the change.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a fixed palette as a color shift, or hue drifting randomly without clear states → define clear before and after states.
- No visible transition mechanism → use a door, a cut or an in-shot gradient so the audience sees the "crossing".
