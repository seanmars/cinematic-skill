# Desaturation

Summary: Overall color intensity is pulled down while hues remain, making the image look pale and weary.

## Narrative Function

- Creates emotional weight through "deliberately withheld color".
- Conveys desolation, moral fatigue or a harsh environment.
- The few remaining color accents therefore stand out with extra emotional weight.

## How It Works

- Lower overall saturation, adjusting it separately from contrast.
- Keep specific color accents (practical lights, costumes, blood).
- Skin tones, though paler, must stay recognizable.

## When to Use

- Use: Depleted worlds, e.g. refugee scenes, winter landscapes, stories of moral exhaustion.
- Avoid: Using it as a substitute for bleach bypass.
- Avoid: When the story relies heavily on color impact; and unless intended, don't push it all the way to black and white.

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): Wintry England with only stinging traces of color left.
- *Sicario* (2015): The border desert depicted with extremely restrained color.
- *The Road* (2009): A near-completely desaturated post-apocalyptic world.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Muted, desaturated palette: color intensity pulled down across the whole frame while hues remain faintly recognizable, pale but natural skin. One small color accent, like a red scarf, stays noticeable; normal contrast, no metallic bleach look, not black and white.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with bleach bypass (metallic blacks, coarse grain) or black and white → apply only a gentle saturation reduction and keep hues recognizable.
- Blowing out contrast while desaturating → control contrast independently.
