# Tungsten Balance

Summary: White balance set to about 3200K, so interior lights look naturally warm while daylight or moonlight outside the window turns distinctly blue.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes contrast between a warm, safe interior and a cold outside world.
- Expresses spatial and emotional division without dialogue; a single window can divide safety from exposure.

## How It Works

- Set camera white balance to 3200K, or use tungsten-balanced film stock.
- Daylight, moonlight and cool LEDs coming through windows naturally turn blue, while interior practicals stay warm.
- Colored light spill on surrounding surfaces must be consistent.

## When to Use

- Use: "Warm inside, cold outside" images such as night scenes, snow outside the window, indoor parties.
- Avoid: When the whole frame should be evenly warm.
- Avoid: Forcing a warm/cool complementary grade without corresponding light sources.

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): Warm sources contrasted with icy window light.
- *Eyes Wide Shut* (1999): Warm practicals coexist with cool night light.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Camera white-balanced for 3200K tungsten: warm table lamps inside the room look natural and cozy, while the window behind shows cold blue night and falling snow. Color separation comes from real visible light sources, with consistent blue spill near the window and warm spill near the lamps.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Applying amber overall, or splitting faces into teal and orange halves without a motivating source → keep 3200K balance with warm fixtures visible in frame.
- Inconsistent spill of the cool window light → keep the blue spill near the window consistent across surfaces.
