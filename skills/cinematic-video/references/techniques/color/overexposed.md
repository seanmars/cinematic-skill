# Overexposed

Summary: Highlights are deliberately pushed to white, burning out highlight detail and leaving only a few key elements.

## Narrative Function

- Expresses erasure, revelation, sensory overload or blinding light.
- A window can be heaven or a fluorescent hell; faces nearly vanish.
- Conveys the sense that "the light exceeds what the medium can hold".

## How It Works

- Expose until highlights clip or bloom beyond recovery.
- Keep only key elements (eyes, silhouette) and let the rest burn to white.
- Halation and soft focus soften the effect; digital clipping gives harder edges.

## When to Use

- Use: When blown-out white carries narrative meaning, e.g. a glaring doorway, erased memory, a strobing nightclub.
- Avoid: Treating it as a synonym for bright but detailed high-key.
- Avoid: Accidental overexposure; clearly distinguish it from ordinary correct exposure.

## Film Examples

- *The Tree of Life* (2011): Childhood sunlight so intense it exceeds what the film stock can hold.
- *Enter the Void* (2009): Overbright interiors and flashes create sensory overload.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Deliberately overexposed shot: a woman stands in front of a window flooded with white light, the highlights clipped and blooming until the room dissolves into glowing white. Only her dark silhouette and eyes survive the burn; the lost detail is the point.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with high-key (whites still hold detail) or a light flash transition → highlights must clip / bloom and truly lose information.
- Purposeless overexposure that looks like an exposure error → make the "burn" itself the creative focus, and decide which elements survive.
