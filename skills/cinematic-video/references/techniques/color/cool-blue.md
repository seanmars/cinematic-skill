# Cool Blue

Summary: The whole frame leans toward steel blue and cyan, draining warmth and leaving only a few warm lights as emotional anchors.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes institutional coldness, the chill of night, or interpersonal alienation.
- Skin loses warmth, turning porcelain white or greyish pink.
- A warm practical source, if present, becomes a symbolic "hearth".

## How It Works

- Shift the white balance of midtones and shadows toward higher Kelvin.
- Keep skin readable; unless a sickly look is intended, don't let faces go fully cyan.
- When an emotional anchor is needed, keep a single warm practical.

## When to Use

- Use: Cold institutional spaces (offices, clinics, buildings at dawn), night sequences, themes of alienation.
- Avoid: Using it as a substitute for day-for-night, moonlight shaping or teal-orange.

## Film Examples

- *The Bourne Ultimatum* (2007): Offices and streets are all steel blue-green, giving the institution no warmth.
- *Michael Clayton* (2007): Cold corporate spaces and nights, contrasted with a few warm domestic elements.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Cool blue grade: the entire frame biased toward steel blue and cyan, shadows colder than highlights, skin pale and porcelain but still readable. A single warm table lamp glows in the background as the only source of warmth.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Accidentally making teal-orange (peachy faces, cyan environment) → cool the whole frame evenly, reserving warmth for deliberate practicals.
- Mistaken for day-for-night yet keeping high-angle daylight shadows → remove daytime light cues and keep shadow direction consistent.
