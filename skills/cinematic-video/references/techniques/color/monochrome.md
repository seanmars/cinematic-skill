# Monochrome

Summary: Color is removed entirely, leaving only light and dark, texture and shape to carry the image.

## Narrative Function

- Reduces information to its essence; tonality, texture and silhouette replace color.
- Costume and lighting must carry layering and meaning; blood and skin both become grey-scale density.
- Viewed like a print, the audience reads edges and tonal values.

## How It Works

- Shoot on color or black-and-white, then remove all chroma information.
- Since there is no hue to separate the subject, exposure must cover the full tonal range.
- If a period is specified, match the characteristics of orthochromatic / panchromatic film stock; aim for deep blacks, silvery highlights, zero saturation.

## When to Use

- Use: When the image should feel like a drawing, confessional moments, combat sequences, a historical documentary feel.
- Avoid: When the story depends on localized color accents.
- Avoid: When what you actually want is a brownish memory tonality (that's sepia).

## Film Examples

- *The Lighthouse* (2019): Hard black and white emphasizing texture and visual layering.
- *Raging Bull* (1980): Black and white turns boxing into tonal impact.
- *Schindler's List* (1993): Mainly grey scale; the few controlled color exceptions are handled with other techniques.

## Prompt Examples

```text
True black-and-white image with zero color information: deep inky blacks, silvery highlights and a full grey scale in between. Texture, shape and hard light carry the scene; no blue tint in the shadows and no brown sepia cast.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Blue left in the shadows or an overall brown cast, looking black and white but not truly so → remove all chroma for true grey scale.
- Not abandoning the mindset of separating the subject by color → build layering with light, dark and texture instead.
