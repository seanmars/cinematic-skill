# Moonlight Gel

Summary: A single high, cool, directional key light simulates moonlight, carving a silver outline around figures while keeping deep shadows.

## Narrative Function

- Shapes a dimensional night scene where silver rim light and deep shadows coexist.
- Even with no moon in frame, there is clear source logic.
- Can contrast with warm practicals such as firelight or cabin windows to add emotional tension.

## How It Works

- Rig a large cool source up high, gelled with cyan, steel blue or pale green.
- Keep fill underexposed to preserve the night feel.
- Use the key to rim the figure while faces stay readable; control spill to keep directionality.

## When to Use

- Use: Scenes needing dimensional night, such as forests, snowfields, approaching a cabin.
- Avoid: Using it as a blue grade applied to daytime footage.
- Avoid: Flattening the night with even fill, or faking night with noon light.

- *The Revenant* (2015): Cool night light contrasted with the campfire.
- *The Witch* (2015): The dark forest rendered with directional cool light.
- *Suspiria* (1977): Interiors in saturated colored light, showcasing an expressive gel vocabulary.
- *Mandy* (2018): Extreme colored light intensifies the psychological mood.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night scene lit by a single high cool-blue key from above acting as moonlight, carving thin silver rim light along the figure's shoulders and hair. The surrounding forest falls into deep underexposed shadow, a warm cabin window glows in the distance; no flat fill, no daylight cues.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Even blue fill with no directional key or rim light → establish a single high cool source that carves a silver edge.
- Surroundings exposed too bright → underexpose the surrounding space deeply; a warm practical can be added for contrast.
