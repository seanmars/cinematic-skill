# Filmic Faded

Summary: Blacks are lifted to a soft dark grey and highlights roll off smoothly, giving the romantic texture of a film print.

## Narrative Function

- Soft, "airy" blacks and creamy highlights create a romantic, memory-like mood.
- Forgiving of flaws while retaining modern color rendition.

## How It Works

- Lift the toe so blacks become low grey rather than pure black.
- Compress highlights instead of hard-clipping to white.
- Keep moderate saturation; a print-emulation LUT can be a starting point, while watching skin tones.

## When to Use

- Use: Summer memories, stories close to period pieces, images wanting a film-print feel.
- Avoid: Scenes that need crushed blacks, or when the fading is really meant as a prop-like expired-chemistry look.

## Film Examples

- *The Master* (2012): Print-like contrast and airy blacks.
- *Call Me by Your Name* (2017): Summer highlights roll off softly instead of clipping.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Filmic faded print look: lifted blacks that sit as soft charcoal grey with visible shadow detail, creamy highlights that roll off gently instead of clipping. Moderate natural saturation and a gentle photochemical contrast curve, not milky or brown.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with bleach bypass → blacks should be lifted, not made denser.
- Turning into a milky brownish haze, or blacks getting crushed instead → make sure the toe is lifted, highlights compressed without clipping, and hues preserved.
