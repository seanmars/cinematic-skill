# Cross Process

Summary: Primary color relationships are skewed, with cyan-green shadows and yellow-magenta highlights, like film developed in the wrong chemistry.

## Narrative Function

- Deliberately imitates a "kept processing error"; popular in 1990s fashion films and music videos.
- Colors shift unnaturally, conveying an unreliable, dreamlike color world.

## How It Works

- Push shadows toward cyan / green and highlights toward yellow / magenta / red.
- Raise contrast.
- Don't protect skin tones; let them follow the chemical color cast.

## When to Use

- Use: Fashion films, music videos, narratives needing unreliable or dreamlike color.
- Avoid: When accurate hues are needed (even highly saturated ones).
- Avoid: When you want a controlled complementary palette such as teal-orange.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Cross-processed film look: primaries skewed to the wrong neighbors, cyan-green contaminating the shadows, yellow-magenta highlights and slightly jaundiced skin. Punchy high contrast, 1990s fashion music-video aesthetic, not simply brighter colors.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Ending up with merely louder correct colors (hyper-saturation) or teal-orange → shift hues toward the wrong neighboring colors.
- Contrast too flat, skin tones protected → keep high contrast and give up commercial-style skin protection.
