# Teal and Orange

Summary: Skin tones stay warm orange while shadows and sky are pushed toward teal, creating strong complementary contrast.

## Narrative Function

- Uses complementary colors to make characters pop from the background, keeping faces clearly readable.
- Creates the bright, punchy texture of contemporary commercial blockbusters.
- Unsuitable for stories needing a depleted, metallic, ruthless mood.

## How It Works

- Push shadows and non-skin midtones toward cyan-teal.
- Isolate skin tones with a qualifier and keep them warm.
- Push highlights and in-frame practical sources toward amber / orange; unless motivated by a source, don't split a face into two colors.

## When to Use

- Use: Contemporary action films, warm skin against cool night sky or atmosphere.
- Avoid: War / crime worlds that need a silver-grey weariness.
- Avoid: Overall warm interiors, or cold institutional scenes.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Bleach Bypass](bleach-bypass.md) | Lowers saturation and adds density instead of warm/cool complementary separation |
| [Split Toning](split-tone.md) | A broader highlight / shadow color tool, not limited to teal-orange |
| [Warm Amber](warm-amber.md) | The whole world leans warm, with no warm/cool separation |
| [Blockbuster Gloss](../genre-looks/blockbuster-gloss.md) | A broader blockbuster look, often including teal-orange as one component |

## Film Examples

- *Transformers* (2007): Made this grade the default style of Hollywood action films.
- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): A more extreme version, with fire and chrome orange against a teal desert sky.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Teal and orange grade: warm peachy skin tones held in the highlights while shadows, sky and background push toward cyan-teal. Strong complementary contrast, faces stay natural and readable, no overall blue wash and no gritty bleach-bypass texture.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The whole frame washed cool blue, faces turning teal or grey-green → protect the warmth of skin tones.
- No orange in the highlights → push highlights and sources warm, shadows cool, for a clear complementary split.
