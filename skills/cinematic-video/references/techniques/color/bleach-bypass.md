# Bleach Bypass

Summary: Color is drained but not gone; blacks are dense, highlights harsh, with a metallic silver-grey texture.

## Narrative Function

- Builds a harsh, grimy, exhausted world; suits battlefields, crime and cold cities.
- Skin tones shift toward brass or grey, blacks deepen, and the image feels gritty.
- It is a coordinated tonal system, not just removing color.

## How It Works

- Derives from the lab practice of retaining silver in the print, hence the metallic density.
- Contrast, saturation and highlight roll-off are adjusted separately.
- Keep a little hue so it doesn't become pure black and white; keep checking skin tones throughout, and keep blacks solid.

## When to Use

- Use: Brutal scenes such as war, crime, interrogations in the rain, manhunts.
- Avoid: Blockbuster looks that need bright complementary colors.
- Avoid: Treating it as a synonym for ordinary desaturation.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Desaturation](desaturation.md) | Only lowers saturation, without adding density or contrast |
| [Teal and Orange](teal-orange.md) | Complementary warm skin and cool sky, bright tonality |
| [Monochrome](monochrome.md) | Removes hue entirely; bleach bypass still keeps crushed color |

## Film Examples

- *Saving Private Ryan* (1998): High-contrast, low-saturation brutal texture in the combat scenes.
- *Se7en* (1995): Heavy shadows and restrained color in interiors and the city.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Bleach bypass look: drained but not absent color, metallic silver midtones, harsh separated highlights and dense packed blacks that still hold detail. Gritty high-contrast texture with brassy, greyish skin tones, not a pure black-and-white conversion.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with teal-orange or mild desaturation → emphasize dense blacks, harsh highlights and metallic midtones.
- Color disappears completely or blacks crush to solid black → keep residual hue and give the blacks gradation.
