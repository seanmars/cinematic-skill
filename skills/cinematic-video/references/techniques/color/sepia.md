# Sepia

Summary: The grey scale is replaced with a brown-gold scale, like a yellowed old photograph.

## Narrative Function

- Makes events look "already over, belonging to the past".
- The audience instinctively associates it with old photographs and memory sequences.
- Unsuitable for present-tense narratives that need full color.

## How It Works

- Map the grey scale to brown-gold tonality: whites lean toward faded paper, blacks toward deep brown.
- Keep grain texture in the midtones.
- Don't just lay a brown filter over a saturated modern image.

## When to Use

- Use: Flashback chapters, found old photos, prologues.
- Avoid: Modern warm interiors, or images needing a full modern color gamut.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather Part II* (1974): The early-years sequences use a warm, aged tonality.
- *O Brother, Where Art Thou?* (2000): A digitally graded dusty yellow-brown period feel.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Sepia-toned image like an aged photograph: the whole grey scale remapped into brown and gold, creamy faded-paper highlights and deep umber shadows. Fine grain in the midtones, no residual greens or blues, no modern saturated color.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with warm amber, green leaves and blue clothes still clearly distinguishable under a brown filter → fully replace the original colors with a brown-gold scale, leaving almost no modern saturated color.
- Highlights too white and too clean → let highlights fade to simulate an old photograph.
