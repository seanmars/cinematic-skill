# Day for Night

Summary: Footage shot in daylight is treated through exposure and sky handling to look like night while the terrain stays clearly visible.

## Narrative Function

- Used when real night isn't available, or when a wide landscape must stay legible at night (called Nuit américaine in French).
- The core is exposure control and sky handling, not simply tinting blue.
- Not suitable when moonlight can actually be lit, or when daytime cues can't be hidden.

## How It Works

- Control the sun's direction; shoot at a low angle or backlit.
- Deliberately underexpose.
- Remove daytime features: bright zenith, visible sun, lit clouds; then simulate moonlight with a cool tonality.
- Advanced approaches combine footage shot at different sensitivity settings.

## When to Use

- Use: Desert chases and landscape scenes that need a night mood while keeping the terrain visible.
- Avoid: Slapping a blue LUT directly on broad-daylight footage where the sun is visible.

## Film Examples

- *Nope* (2022): The ranch landscape reads as night while the surroundings remain clearly legible.
- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): Stylized desert night sequences.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Day-for-night shot: daylight footage deeply underexposed and graded cool to read as moonlit night, sun hidden, sky darkened to deep navy with no bright clouds. The desert terrain stays faintly legible under a soft, low-level blue light, no harsh noon shadows.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Laying blue directly over a white sky and noon shadows → hide the sun, lower exposure, cool the key light, eliminate daytime sky features.
- Shadow direction and hardness give away daytime → shoot at a low-angle or backlit time of day.
