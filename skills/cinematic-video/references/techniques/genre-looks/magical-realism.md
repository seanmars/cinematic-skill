# Magical Realism

Summary: Impossible events happen in an ordinary world yet are shot with entirely everyday camera grammar, so miracles feel as natural as weather.

## Narrative Function

- Treats miracles as ordinary events, not spectacle.
- Extraordinary moments (a monster appearing, snow falling indoors) use the same visual grammar as everyday events.
- Does not rely on dramatic lighting changes or camera tricks to signal "wonder."

## How It Works

- Motivated natural light: window light, overcast sky, household sources.
- Normal focal lengths of 35mm or 50mm, eye-level framing and ordinary coverage.
- Impossible elements are presented as casually as ordinary objects; no underlighting, Dutch angles or volumetric light for fantastical moments.

## When to Use

- Use: When the miracle must resist becoming spectacle.
- Avoid: Psychedelic sequences, liminal spaces.
- Avoid: VFX showcase scenes that need camera attention to signal wonder.

## Film Examples

- *The Spirit of the Beehive* (1973): A child's strange encounter is treated as an ordinary afternoon.
- *Beasts of the Southern Wild* (2012): Wonders blend into humid daytime realism.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Magical realism: an ordinary family eats breakfast at a worn kitchen table while small goldfish swim slowly through the air above their plates, nobody reacting. Soft overcast window light, 50mm lens at eye level, plain naturalistic color, no special lighting or camera move for the impossible element.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Adding volumetric light, liminal hallways or lighting changes at the impossible moment → Keep a consistent key light and camera height.
- The camera deliberately emphasizes the miracle → Present the impossible as naturally as weather.
