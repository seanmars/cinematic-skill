# Photography

Summary: Treats every shot as a photograph you could frame and hang on a wall: locked-off camera, posed subjects, shaped by a single light source.

## Narrative Function

- Treats film as still prints, emphasizing carefully composed decisive moments over continuous movement.
- The camera acts like a shutter, capturing a single artistic frame that can stand on its own.

## How It Works

- Locked off on a tripod; subjects are posed or the camera waits for stillness.
- Light like a still photographer: window light, candlelight or a single key, no TV-style fill.
- 35-85mm focal lengths, occasionally using the zoom as a compositional crop; every frame needs "printable" visual grammar.

## When to Use

- Use: When a moment is a visual statement rather than a narrative transition.
- Avoid: Coverage scenes that need many cut points, or sequences that need empty duration.
- Avoid: Handheld vérité or searching cameras.

## Film Examples

- *Barry Lyndon* (1975): Candlelit tableau stills, with zooms cropping like a painter.
- *The New World* (2005): Natural light; frames look like exposed photographic negatives.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Photographic still-frame style: a woman in a dark wool coat sits perfectly still on a wooden chair beside a tall window, a single soft window key sculpting half her face against a deep shadowed wall. Locked tripod, 85mm lens, deliberate print-ready composition, no camera movement.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with arthouse emptiness, or adding searching camera moves → Lock the tripod.
- Flat light, casual composition → Compose deliberately to print standards and sculpt with a single light source.
