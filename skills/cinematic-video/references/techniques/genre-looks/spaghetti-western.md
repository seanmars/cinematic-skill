# Spaghetti Western

Summary: Dust, harsh noon sun and ochre tones, cutting violently between extreme wide wilderness vistas and extreme close-ups of eyes.

## Narrative Function

- Builds a mythic feel from harsh environmental conditions.
- The sun becomes a narrative tool.
- Long holds on faces reveal inner conflict before action happens.

## How It Works

- 24-28mm wide angles for vast landscapes, 75-135mm telephoto for intense facial framing.
- Unfiltered hard noon daylight, with almost no fill.
- Post-production emphasizes burnt ochre and bleached skies; dust acts as natural diffusion.

## When to Use

- Use: Standoffs that need gritty authenticity and dramatic tension.
- Avoid: Polished studio productions, mist-filled wuxia narratives, golden hour aesthetics.

## Film Examples

- *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* (1966): Wide plaza compositions against bleached eye close-ups.
- *Once Upon a Time in the West* (1968): Operatic pauses on the protagonist's face; the face is the landscape.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Spaghetti western standoff: an extreme wide shot of three gunmen in a sun-bleached desert plaza, hard cut to a 135mm extreme close-up of squinting eyes beaded with sweat. Harsh noon sun with no fill, drifting dust, burnt ochre palette and washed-out sky, 2.35:1 frame.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting it as a glossy adventure western, with filled-in faces and a teal-orange grade → Remove the fill and desaturate the sky.
- Shot size changes too gradual → Amplify the dust and cut straight from landscape vistas to extreme facial close-ups.
