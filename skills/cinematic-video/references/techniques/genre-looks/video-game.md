# Video Game

Summary: The camera runs on game-engine logic (third-person follow, isometric overhead, first-person), treating space as an explorable level.

## Narrative Function

- Builds the world as an interactive, navigable space.
- The camera acts like a game engine's follow-cam, isometric view or FPS view.
- Space is treated as a level, with an optional HUD.

## How It Works

- Choose a camera logic: third-person follow (2-3 meter boom length with collision), 45-degree isometric orthographic, or FPS.
- Keep the path clearly readable.
- Use HUD sparingly and only in gameplay moments, never in dramatic scenes; camera moves use analog-stick-style easing, not the acceleration of cinema dolly tracks.

## When to Use

- Use: Depicting playable spaces or game-like interaction.
- Avoid: Using it merely as an overlay on ordinary scene coverage.
- Avoid: When the world is not level-based or has no game-camera constraints.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Pixel Art](pixel-art.md) | A sampling method, not engine logic |
| [FPV Drone](../camera-movement/fpv-drone.md) | A real or simulated FPV drone flying through physical space, not game mechanics |
| [Animation](animation.md) | Can render games but is not itself playable |

## Film Examples

- *Free Guy* (2021): Embeds follow-cam and HUD logic in live action.
- *Scott Pilgrim vs. the World* (2010): Blends fighting-arena space and UI grammar into narrative cinematography.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Video game third-person camera: follow-cam locked about 2.5 meters behind and slightly above a hooded explorer running through ancient temple corridors, the camera lagging and easing like analog-stick input and nudging forward when it brushes a pillar. Clear level-like pathway ahead, minimal health bar HUD in one corner.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with Pixel Art, or just a regular tracking shot with a crosshair overlay → Lock the boom length and height, or the isometric angle.
- Space has no sense of a level → Structure the space as a level and allow engine-style collision and lag.
