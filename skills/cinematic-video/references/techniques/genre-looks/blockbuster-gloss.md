# Blockbuster Gloss

Summary: Strong rim light, volumetric shafts, clean skies and expensive contrast make every frame ready to print as a movie poster.

## Narrative Function

- Makes the image itself promotional material: adventure frozen into poster-grade stills.
- Emphasizes heroic composition and big-budget aesthetics over intimate vulnerability.

## How It Works

- Key light paired with strong backlit rim.
- Volumetric haze creates god rays; skies are kept clean or deliberately designed.
- 24-40mm for scale, long lenses for faces; color grading aims for expensive contrast, often teal-orange separation and bright highlights.

## When to Use

- Use: Adventure, action and large-scale stories that need billboard-level readability.
- Avoid: Intimate interiors, observational interviews.
- Avoid: Run-down spaces better served by restraint.

## Film Examples

- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): Desert contrast optimized for the big screen.
- *Dune* (2021): Monumental wide shots, volumetric shafts and controlled haze.
- *Skyfall* (2012): Clear action geography and an expensive night grade.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Blockbuster gloss hero shot: an armored pilot walks toward camera across a vast desert airfield at sunset, strong backlight carving a bright rim around her silhouette, volumetric god rays through drifting haze. Clean sky gradient, 28mm wide for scale, rich teal-orange contrast, poster-ready composition.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Missing rim light, blemished sky → Add backlit edge light and clean up sky clutter.
- Frame cannot stand alone as a still → Raise contrast until the silhouette stays clear when scaled down to poster size.
