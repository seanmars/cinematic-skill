# Cinéma Vérité

Summary: A documentary grammar using only available light and zooms that search for the subject; the filmmaker's presence is visible, and the camera is a catalyst rather than an invisible observer.

## Narrative Function

- Captures real moments with available light and zoom-searching composition.
- The camera intervenes as a catalyst rather than a hidden bystander.
- Most effective when stylization would feel dishonest.

## How It Works

- Use only natural interior light, at most a small on-camera bounce.
- 16mm or a long zoom, with focus actively searching; handheld or monopod at eye level.
- Leave mixed color temperatures uncorrected; deliberately add no key, rim or backlight.

## When to Use

- Use: When the filmmaker's presence matters and aesthetic honesty is the top priority.
- Avoid: Polished productions, found-footage horror stories.
- Avoid: Scripted scenes that depend on three-point lighting.

## Film Examples

- *Primary* (1960): The camera follows candidates through crowds, zooming to find its subject.
- *Dont Look Back* (1967): The camera is like one of the people in the room, recording the tour backstage.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Cinéma vérité documentary style on 16mm: a handheld camera at eye level in a cramped backstage room, the long zoom drifting to find a musician arguing with his manager. Only window daylight and a tungsten practical, mixed color temperatures left uncorrected, grainy and unglamorous.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Adding timestamps or panic aesthetics → That is Found Footage; remove these elements.
- Interviews with clean backlight and key light → Remove artificial lighting, let the zoom search naturally, with the filmmaker as witness.
