# Maximalism

Summary: Every inch of the frame is packed with clashing patterns, colors and costumes, refusing empty space and making abundance itself the worldview.

## Narrative Function

- Builds theatrical, crammed, inherently decorative worlds.
- Clashing patterns, colors and costumes suggest excess rather than restraint.
- The frame refuses emptiness and faithfully presents the environment it depicts.

## How It Works

- 24-40mm short to medium focal lengths, fitting in as many elements as possible.
- High saturation, with multiple colored practicals.
- Enough depth of field to keep patterns legible; symmetrical or centered composition often organizes the chaos; lighting favors clarity over mood.

## When to Use

- Use: Lavish spaces (hotels, theaters, maximalist apartments).
- Avoid: Empty interiors, film noir aesthetics, minimalist environments.
- Avoid: Just applying a color grade to an empty room.

## Film Examples

- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Centered, dense composition; wallpaper becomes an architectural element.
- *Moulin Rouge!* (2001): Theatrical color and pattern-covered interiors.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Maximalist interior: a centered symmetrical view of an opulent hotel lounge crammed with clashing floral wallpaper, striped velvet sofas, stacked gilded frames and a woman in a patterned emerald gown. Multiple colored lamps, 28mm lens, deep focus so every pattern stays sharp, bright clear lighting.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with Giallo, lighting a bare wall with a single violent color → Fill every zone with competing patterns.
- Background out of focus, patterns blurred → Keep the whole frame sharp while using multiple hues.
