# German Expressionism

Summary: Distorted architecture, painted shadows and skewed angles literally "build" a character's psychological state into the set.

## Narrative Function

- Presents inner psychology through a constructed environment rather than merely suggesting it.
- The set itself is a visual diagnosis of the character's mental state.

## How It Works

- Build impossible architecture: converging walls, oversized windows, illogical staircases.
- Use hard light without fill to form graphic, sharp shadow edges.
- Default to a slight Dutch angle; high-contrast black and white or orthochromatic film texture; 35mm spherical lens, locked-off camera and theatrical blocking.

## When to Use

- Use: When the set must represent a psychological or emotional state.
- Avoid: Using a Dutch angle only as decoration, or just adding a contrast grade to a square room.
- Avoid: Depicting realistic contemporary spaces.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dutch Angle](../camera-angles/dutch-angle.md) | Only tilts the horizon, rather than distorting the whole set |
| [Hard Light](../lighting/hard-light.md) | Only a quality of light, without architectural distortion |
| [Film Noir](film-noir.md) | Distorts real streets with light; expressionism rebuilds the architecture itself |

## Film Examples

- *The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari* (1920): Painted shadows and a tilted town become psychological space.
- *Metropolis* (1927): Geometric scale and shafts become a diagram of social structure.
- *Nosferatu* (1922): The body merges with its shadow on the wall.

## Prompt Examples

```text
German Expressionist style, high-contrast black and white: a gaunt man climbs a crooked staircase between walls that lean inward at impossible angles, jagged shadows painted directly onto the set. Hard unfilled light, slight Dutch tilt, locked theatrical framing on a 35mm lens.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with Film Noir, or adding a Dutch angle and contrast to an ordinary room → Make the set's perspective itself crooked.
- Shadows come only from light → Paint graphic shadows, use hard light and align the camera with the set geometry.
