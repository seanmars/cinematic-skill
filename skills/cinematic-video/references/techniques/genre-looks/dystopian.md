# Dystopian

Summary: The future is not shiny but bureaucratic, polluted, cold and massive; architecture, smog and decayed surfaces make "the system" the villain.

## Narrative Function

- Depicts an authoritarian future through architecture, pollution and decay rather than high-tech aesthetics.
- Establishes hierarchy through visual markers such as costumes, queues and signage.
- The villain is the entire civic system, not a single bad actor.

## How It Works

- Approach 1: 35mm handheld, mixed available light, showing social collapse.
- Approach 2: Wide angles stress monumental scale, paired with smog and desaturated color.
- Emphasize weathered surfaces, broken infrastructure and visual hierarchy; no heroic lighting.

## When to Use

- Use: When systemic oppression, not individual wrongdoing, drives the story.
- Avoid: Pursuing neon crime aesthetics or glossy sci-fi adventure.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Tech Noir](tech-noir.md) | Crime investigation in a mechanized city at night; dystopian is used, civic decay |
| [Bleach Bypass](../color/bleach-bypass.md) | Only a desaturation treatment, not structural design |
| [Film Noir](film-noir.md) | 1940s crime grammar, not a collapsed future system |

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): Handheld through run-down near-future streets.
- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Monumental haze and orange-toned scale of hierarchy.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Dystopian near-future: a long queue of citizens in identical grey coats waits at a concrete checkpoint beneath a colossal ministry building, faded propaganda signage on stained walls. Wide 24mm lens, thick smog haze, desaturated cold palette, grime on every surface, no heroic rim light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Drifting into Tech Noir or Blockbuster Gloss (hard black shadows, clean rim light) → Keep surface grime, visible signage and smog.
- Unclear approach → Choose between handheld collapse and monumental bureaucracy.
