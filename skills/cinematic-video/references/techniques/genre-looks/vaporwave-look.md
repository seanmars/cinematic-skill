# Vaporwave

Summary: Soft pink and cyan gradients, marble and Greek statues, and VHS noise long for an 1980s shopping-mall future that never arrived.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys melancholy nostalgia for vanished consumer spaces.
- Evokes the mood of abandoned malls and obsolete technology, not crime or investigation.

## How It Works

- Soft, even, fluorescent-like light tinted pink or cyan; no hard key light.
- Centered composition, 35mm spherical lens, moderate depth of field.
- VHS artifacts: chroma bleed, tracking errors, reduced sharpness; production design uses marble, palm trees, Greek busts, geometric grids and gradient sunsets.

## When to Use

- Use: Depicting an unused future preserved on degraded media.
- Avoid: Crime narratives, hard-edged neon noir styles, investigative moods.

## Film Examples

- *Drive* (2011): Similar color grammar, but structurally still neo-noir.
- True vaporwave appears mainly in music videos and internet imagery.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Vaporwave aesthetic: an empty 1980s shopping mall atrium with a marble fountain, potted palms and a white Greek bust, bathed in soft even pink and cyan fluorescent light. Centered symmetrical framing, pastel gradient sunset on a wall mural, low contrast, VHS chroma bleed and tracking lines.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with Neo-Noir (wet detective alleys, unfilled shadows) → Flatten contrast and switch to centered composition.
- Missing signature elements → Add sculptural elements and emphasize videotape degradation.
