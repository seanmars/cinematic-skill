# Film Noir

Summary: High-contrast black-and-white low-key lighting, hard light, venetian blind shadows and wet streets let the shadows themselves speak of guilt and moral ambiguity.

## Narrative Function

- Deliberately withholds visual information to create psychological tension.
- Half-shadowed faces suggest guilt and moral complexity.
- Silver-toned contrast replaces color as the main emotional tool.

## How It Works

- Hard key light (Fresnel or open-face fixture) 45-90 degrees off-axis, with almost no fill (about 8:1).
- Venetian blind or tree-branch gobos cast shadow geometry on surfaces; wet ground reflects the key as bounce.
- Practicals such as desk lamps and signs motivate the light; 35-50mm lenses at eye level, moderate aperture so the background falls off.

## When to Use

- Use: Crime scenes, moral confrontations, moments that need visual mystery.
- Avoid: Colorful fashion contexts or futuristic scenes.
- Avoid: When facial detail must read clearly, or when going for a neon contemporary style (use Neo-Noir instead).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Neo-Noir](neo-noir.md) | Keeps the guilty geometry but adds colored practicals and gelled sources |
| [Tech Noir](tech-noir.md) | Uses digital screens as the key light source |

## Film Examples

- *The Third Man* (1949): Wet Vienna streets, raking key light, doorway framing.
- *Double Indemnity* (1944): Venetian blind gobos create a confessional-like atmosphere.
- *The Big Combo* (1955): A spotlight in fog becomes the spatial landmark of a murder sequence.

## Prompt Examples

```text
1940s film noir, black and white: a detective in a fedora stands in a dim office, a hard key light from 70 degrees off-axis casting sharp venetian blind stripes across his half-shadowed face, no fill. Outside the window, rain-slick pavement reflects a single streetlamp; deep silver contrast, 40mm lens at eye level.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with generic low-key or neo-noir (soft underexposure, neon color, filled-in shadows) → Remove the fill.
- Missing shadow patterns and reflections → Add sharp gobo patterns, wet down the ground, and keep the ungelled silver tone.
