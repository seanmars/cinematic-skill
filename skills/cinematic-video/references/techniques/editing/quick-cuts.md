# Quick Cuts

Summary: Short shots from multiple angles cut at an accelerating pace with progressively tighter shot sizes, using speed itself to build tension.

## Narrative Function

- Rhythm is meaning: ever-shorter shots and ever-tighter framing stack up pressure.
- Placing a pause within the quick cutting lets the climax land.

## How It Works

- Design a gradually shortening length curve, paired with a progression in shot size (wide → close, hands → face).
- Change angle on every cut, bringing a new viewpoint rather than a gap within the same view.
- Selectively align with musical or action accents; not every cut needs to hit the beat.

## When to Use

- Use: duels, chases, performances, moments where tension needs to tighten.
- Avoid: dialogue that needs breathing room.
- Avoid: pacing with no pause before the close, or the climax cannot land.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Jump Cut](jump-cut.md) | Jumps time within similar setups; quick cuts keep changing angle |
| [Crash Cut](crash-cut.md) | A single violent cut, not an accelerating pattern |
| [Montage](montage.md) | Mainly compresses time or collides meaning, not driven by cutting rate |
| [Fragments](fragments.md) | Hides the complete action; quick cuts show action through many short, complete views |

## Film Examples

- *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* (1966): the three-way standoff tightens step by step from bodies to faces and eyes.
- *Whiplash* (2014): performance is tightly bound to rhythm and pressure.
- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): short shots drive forward like a pulse.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Quick-cut sequence building to a duel: wide shot of two cowboys on a dusty street, then progressively shorter cuts from new angles, hands near holsters, boots, squinting eyes, a twitching finger, ending in one held silent close-up before the draw. Distinct angles every cut, no morphing.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turns into jump cuts (same framing, different time) → change viewpoint on every cut.
- Shapeless chaos → design a gradually shortening curve and end on one resolving image.
