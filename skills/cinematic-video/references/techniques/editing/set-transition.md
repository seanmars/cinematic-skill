# Set Transition

Summary: The camera keeps moving while the surrounding walls and set transform into another location within the shot, instead of changing scenes with a cut.

## Narrative Function

- Connects spaces to one another, letting "geography itself" edit the story.
- Keeps space continuous around the subject, showing shifts between contrasting locations such as rehearsal → performance or memory → street.

## How It Works

- Build two or more complete spaces that can physically or digitally replace each other along the same camera path.
- Methods include sliding flats, rotating walls, doorframe seams or digital set extensions.
- Maintain consistent lighting motivation throughout.

## When to Use

- Use: when spaces should feel interconnected and no cut should be acknowledged.
- Avoid: when a hard cut or wipe tells the story more efficiently.
- Avoid: when the second space lacks narrative weight.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Invisible Cut](invisible-cut.md) | Hides the cut; the set may not change |
| [Morphing](../effects/morphing.md) | Interpolated image deformation, not architectural rewriting |
| [Pass Through](../camera-movement/pass-through.md) | The camera passes through a surface as a portal; in a set transition the walls move around the character |
| [Match Motion Cut](match-motion.md) | Changes worlds through the body's motion vector; the walls don't move |

## Film Examples

- *Birdman* (2014): the theater and the city become one connected set.
- *1917* (2019): hidden seams keep the geography continuous from trenches to ruins.
- *The Matrix* (1999): the lobby sequence rewrites space through action and spatial continuity.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Continuous set transition: the camera follows an actress walking forward as the backstage walls slide apart around her and reveal a glowing theater stage, then the stage flats rotate into a rainy night street. She stays stable in frame, lighting remains motivated, no cut and no morphing of her body.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The actor morphs, or an obvious cut appears at the threshold → keep the subject stable and let the walls and extended set do the rewriting.
- Inconsistent lighting between spaces → match lighting and hide the seams well.
