# Iris

Summary: A circular (or other shaped) mask opens from black or closes down onto a detail, marking the focal point like a stage spotlight.

## Narrative Function

- Isolates a comic detail or serves as theatrical punctuation for a sequence.
- Like "a wink plus a chapter ending", it points to a specific subject while keeping a theatrical feel.

## How It Works

- Build the mask in post, controlling center point, shape and timing.
- The mask can follow the subject or stay fixed while action continues beneath it.
- An iris-in opens from black; an iris-out closes to black.

## When to Use

- Use: emphasizing a ring, a glance, a gag, or ending a scene theatrically.
- Avoid: using it as a substitute for a push-in.
- Avoid: when the whole frame needs to darken evenly; use a Fade instead.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Fade](fade.md) | Empties the whole frame evenly rather than isolating a center |
| [Wipe](wipe.md) | Uses a straight or clock edge rather than a central hole |
| [Vignette](../effects/vignette.md) | Only darkens the corners; not a transition |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Links two complete images rather than deliberately masking the frame |

## Film Examples

- *Neighbors* (1920): a circular reveal changes the scale and situation of a wedding scene.
- *The Artist* (2011): iris-like framing as silent-era grammatical punctuation.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Iris-out transition: a static wide shot of a silent-era comedian winking at camera, a circular black mask slowly closes in centered on his face until only the wink remains, then goes fully black. Image outside the shrinking circle stays sharp and fully exposed until masked.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Made into a zoom, fade or heavy vignette → keep the camera still and use a circular mask with a clear center.
- The image outside the mask gradually darkens → keep full exposure outside the mask; only the mask edge moves.
