# Jump Cut

Summary: Cut out a stretch of time within nearly identical framing, making the subject "jump" in frame so the break in time is plainly visible.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys impatience, unease, comic ellipsis or deliberately exposed editing.
- Intentionally lets the audience see the skipped time instead of hiding it.
- The obvious gap creates psychological discomfort.

## How It Works

- Cut time out of a single camera setup, or join very similar views of the same subject.
- Keep lens, angle and subject size similar so the break is recognizable at a glance.
- Sound can run continuously to smooth the jump, or break in sync to emphasize it.

## When to Use

- Use: waiting, long-winded conversations, long drives, low-mood sequences.
- Avoid: when invisible continuity is needed; use Match on Action or an Invisible Cut instead.
- Avoid: using it to cover for missing coverage.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Smash Cut](smash-cut.md) | Cuts to a completely different image; a jump cut stays within similar framing |
| [Montage](montage.md) | Many shots from different setups; a jump cut is a time gap within one setup |
| [Axial Cut](axial-cut.md) | Emphasizes a change in distance rather than lost time |
| [Quick Cuts](quick-cuts.md) | Rhythm comes from constant angle changes, not time jumps |

## Film Examples

- *Breathless* (1960): driving and dialogue keep jumping forward within similar framing.
- *Dancer in the Dark* (2000): discontinuous editing disrupts smooth viewing.
- *Requiem for a Dream* (2000): repeated ritual actions shown in jump cuts convey the rhythm of craving.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Jump cut sequence from one locked-off camera: a man waits on a bench, and between each hard cut his posture, the wall clock and the crowd behind him change abruptly. Framing never moves, no morphing between poses, time visibly skipped.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Changing angle every time, turning it into Quick Cuts → keep the framing unchanged.
- The subject morphs smoothly between poses → keep nearly identical framing and cut time out cleanly.
