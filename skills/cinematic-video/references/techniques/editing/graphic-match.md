# Graphic Match

Summary: Two consecutive images share the same dominant shape, line or color block in the same position, forming a visual rhyme on a hard cut.

## Narrative Function

- Links two images through visual form rather than narrative logic.
- The audience still "feels" continuity even when time or place changes.

## How It Works

- Have both images share a recognizable outline: a silhouette, a circle, a diagonal or a horizon height.
- Place the same shape at the same position and size in frame.
- Use a hard cut to emphasize precision; a dissolve works but weakens the formal impact.

## When to Use

- Use: when you want form itself to carry meaning across time and space (clock face → dome, mouth → tunnel).
- Avoid: images without a clear corresponding outline.
- Avoid: using it as filler between unrelated shots, or relying only on the same actor or costume for continuity.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): a thrown bone cuts to a spacecraft in orbit.
- *Women on the Verge of a Nervous Breakdown* (1988): a series of compositional shape rhymes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Graphic match: close-up of a round clock face filling the center of frame, hard cut to an overhead view of a circular stadium roof at identical size and position. Two different objects rhyming through one shared circle, no interpolation between them.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Made into a morph → the two objects must differ and share only the shape.
- Relying on the same actor alone to carry the link → lock a single shape and its placement.
