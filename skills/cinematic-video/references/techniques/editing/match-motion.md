# Match Motion Cut

Summary: The subject's direction and speed of motion stay consistent across the cut, but the world around it has completely changed.

## Narrative Function

- Makes impossible transitions work: the environment is replaced entirely while the motion vector continues.
- Uses movement to carry the audience through world changes, such as dream and reality, or train and grassland.

## How It Works

- At the cut, align the subject's pose, frame position, camera angle, direction of motion and speed.
- Cut mid-action, not at rest.
- The two worlds can contradict each other; only the motion vector must match.

## When to Use

- Use: dream sequences, chases across locations, dances spanning different eras.
- Avoid: ordinary coverage of a single action in the same space (that is Match on Action).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Match on Action](match-action.md) | Same event, same world, two camera angles |
| [Whip Pan](../camera-movement/whip-pan.md) | Hides the cut with camera blur rather than subject motion |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Can be a visual similarity between static subjects |

## Film Examples

- *The Graduate* (1967): Benjamin jumps into the pool and lands falling onto a bed.
- *Paprika* (2006): dream movements link incompatible spaces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Match motion cut: a girl sprints left to right down a crowded city sidewalk, and mid-stride the shot hard cuts to her running at the same speed, pose and screen position across a misty mountain ridge. Only the world changes; direction and velocity continue, no morphing body.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The subject resets speed or changes direction after the cut → preserve the phase, direction and speed of the action.
- The subject is static on both sides → cut mid-action and let the geography change at the cut.
