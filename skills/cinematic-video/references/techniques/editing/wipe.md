# Wipe

Summary: A visible moving edge sweeps across the frame, "pushing" the previous image out for the next.

## Narrative Function

- Creates brisk, storybook-like scene changes that openly acknowledge the editing.
- Like turning a page, it marks progress in geography or plot.
- Suits adventure films and serial-style pacing.

## How It Works

- Choose the edge form: vertical, horizontal, diagonal, clock hand, barn door or a custom shape.
- Sweep the edge across two clean shots.
- A soft or hard edge is a stylistic choice, but it must be a graphic element and must not let the scenes melt into each other.

## When to Use

- Use: jumping between locations, chapters or scenes at a serial pace.
- Avoid: when seamless, invisible continuity is needed.
- Avoid: using it to cover editing mistakes.

## Film Examples

- *Star Wars* (1977): directional wipes link adventure scenes and different locations.
- *Seven Samurai* (1954): a visible page-turning feel marks plot progress.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Horizontal wipe transition: a hard-edged vertical line travels left to right across the frame, replacing a desert canyon chase with a bustling port town at dusk. Both shots stay complete and unaltered on each side of the moving edge, no blending.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The edge smears or turns into a dissolve → use a clean moving matte.
- A passing actor takes over the wipe's job (becoming an invisible cut) → the edge must be a clear graphic element.
