# Match Split

Summary: In a split screen, a line, eyeline or motion continues across the seam to the other side, joining two worlds into one whole.

## Narrative Function

- Uses a shared graphic boundary to visually unify contrasting spaces or moments.
- Presents duality (expectation vs reality, twins, parallel timelines) as one organism rather than separate boxes.

## How It Works

- Let lines, eyelines, horizons or motion vectors continue across the seam.
- Align scale and proportion across multiple versions of the same subject.
- Light each side by its own world's logic, unless a visual gag needs both sides to match.

## When to Use

- Use: twins, double lives, contrasts across time, connected spaces.
- Avoid: when the two spaces should stay graphically separate, or share no common outline.
- Avoid: ordinary phone conversations with no visual rhyme (a Split Screen is enough).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Split Screen](split-screen.md) | Side by side at once, but with no graphic continuation |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Replaces one full image with another over time |
| [Graphic Match](graphic-match.md) | A shape continues across a hard cut, not a simultaneous seam |
| [Frame within Frame](../composition/frame-in-frame.md) | Doors or windows framing within the scene, not a split composition |

## Film Examples

- *The Parent Trap* (1961): two versions of the same girl sit in a shared composition, the split line defining their relationship.
- *(500) Days of Summer* (2009): a party is divided into expectation and reality along a readable seam.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Match split frame: left half shows a young man at a dinner table in 1970, right half shows the same man aged 70 at the same table today; the table edge and his eyeline continue unbroken across a clean vertical seam. Both halves visible at once, no morphing across the divide.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treated as an ordinary split screen with misaligned eyelines → design the seam deliberately and continue the same outline.
- Cutting to a new full image → keep both worlds visible at the same time.
