# Axial Cut

Summary: Keep the angle and hard cut along the same lens axis to a closer (or wider) shot size, so the focal point "jumps" toward the viewer.

## Narrative Function

- Delivers emphasis through impact rather than gradual progression.
- Closes in step by step along a single viewpoint, building staged recognition.

## How It Works

- Shoot matching compositions at different distances from exactly the same angle.
- Keep the subject's pose and background alignment consistent.
- Join them with hard cuts, with no animated zoom.

## When to Use

- Use: details that need to land hard (timestamps, facial features, key objects).
- Avoid: when the camera should move naturally through space, or continuous optical movement is needed.
- Avoid: replacing a motivated push-in within a long take; it fails if the angle drifts more than a few degrees.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Crash Zoom In](../camera-movement/crash-zoom-in.md) | A continuous focal-length change, not a cut |
| [Jump Cut](jump-cut.md) | Jumps time at a similar shot size; an axial cut jumps distance on the same axis |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Links through visual similarity; an axial cut isolates scale on the same axis |

## Film Examples

- *The Birds* (1963): successively closer shots of the dead farmer.
- *The Shining* (1980): hard axial cuts for emphasis on rooms and faces.
- *Goodfellas* (1990): punch-ins to signal recognition instead of smooth zooms.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Axial cut in three hard steps along the same lens axis: wide shot of an old man seated at a kitchen table, cut to medium, then cut to an extreme close-up of his trembling eye. Same angle and pose in every frame, no zoom animation, scale jumps only on the cuts.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Faking it with an animated zoom or digital crop → it must be two matching compositions, hard cut on the same axis.
- Subject moves or deforms between cuts → lock the pose and change only the shot size.
