# Fade In / Fade Out

Summary: The image gradually sinks into a solid color (usually black) or emerges from it, like punctuation for a sequence.

## Narrative Function

- Marks chapter breaks, shifts in consciousness or emotional closure.
- Leaves a "blank" between two scenes, implying absence, sleep or death, rather than blending them.

## How It Works

- Gradually bring the image down to a single color field, or reverse it to rise from the field.
- Black is the most neutral; other colors can carry emotion.
- Decide whether sound fades with the image or carries over the blank color field.

## When to Use

- Use: openings, between acts, a character losing consciousness, thematic chapter breaks.
- Avoid: frequent use between ordinary dialogue scenes, unless deliberately building a formal rhythm.
- Avoid: when the two images need to overlap; use a Dissolve instead.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Dissolve](dissolve.md) | The two scenes overlap directly; a fade out empties the frame first |
| [Iris](iris.md) | Uses a moving mask to point at a local detail, darkening the rest |
| [Wipe](wipe.md) | Both images coexist, divided by a moving edge |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Links thematically similar images with a hard cut |

## Film Examples

- *Cries and Whispers* (1972): red color fields serve as emotional punctuation between scenes.
- *1917* (2019): a black screen represents the protagonist losing consciousness before waking again.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Slow fade out: a soldier collapses in a moonlit field and the whole image evenly darkens into pure black over three seconds, holding on black before the next scene fades in. Clean uniform field, no smoke or fog doing the work.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Made into a dissolve with two overlapping images → it must pass through a solid color field.
- Faking the fade with smoke or fog → use a clean, uniform color field transition.
