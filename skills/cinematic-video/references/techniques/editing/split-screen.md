# Split Screen

Summary: Two or more image panels side by side in one frame, letting different spaces coexist in the same moment.

## Narrative Function

- Shows multiple spaces at the same moment, making it easy to compare dual identities, parallel timelines or multiple witnesses.
- A visual grammar of "coexistence" rather than sequential reveal.

## How It Works

- Align eyelines, horizons or directions of motion across panels for cohesion; phone conversations can be kept deliberately separate.
- Establish a clear boundary: a hard straight line, architectural structure or an organic shape.
- Keep character styling consistent unless the story contrasts two versions.

## When to Use

- Use: simultaneous phone calls, twin narratives, expectation vs reality, multi-panel parallel sequences.
- Avoid: decorative layering of a single conversation.
- Avoid: when surprise matters more than simultaneity; use Cross-Cutting instead.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Frame within Frame](../composition/frame-in-frame.md) | Uses doors and windows within the scene, not editorial panels |
| [Cross-Cutting](cross-cut.md) | Alternates sequentially; a split screen stacks simultaneously |
| [Match Split](match-split.md) | The split line continues across both worlds |
| [Montage](montage.md) | Images arranged in time; a split screen shows them all at once |

## Film Examples

- *Pillow Talk* (1959): both sides of a phone conversation side by side in one frame.
- *Timecode* (2000): four simultaneous long takes in quadrants.
- *(500) Days of Summer* (2009): expectation and reality of a party side by side, left and right.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Vertical split screen with a hard white dividing line: on the left, a woman in a sunny apartment answers her phone; on the right, a man in a dark office speaks into his receiver. Both scenes play in real time, eyelines angled toward the center line.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Made into doorway or window framing (frame within frame) → it must be two independently staged, fully formed spaces.
- Made into sequential cutting → divide graphically and keep both sides synchronized in time.
