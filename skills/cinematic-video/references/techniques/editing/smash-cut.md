# Smash Cut

Summary: At a quiet or seemingly concluding moment, hard cut abruptly to an image completely opposite in brightness, volume and shot size, creating sharp contrast.

## Narrative Function

- First establishes a calm, finished or sacred state, then breaks it with a sudden shift.
- The contrast itself is the gag or emotional impact.
- It only works if the previous state is truly "established".

## How It Works

- Hold the outgoing shot long enough for its tone and scale to register.
- Hard cut to an image radically different in brightness, volume, composition or subject.
- Hit with sound and picture together, or delay the sound for a second jolt.

## When to Use

- Use: false endings, sudden interruptions, reveals that call for a physical reaction.
- Avoid: using it as a default scene transition.
- Avoid: when the two shots are similar in brightness, scale and tone; without contrast there is no effect.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Jump Cut](jump-cut.md) | Jumps time within similar framing; a smash cut leaves the original space entirely |
| [Crash Cut](crash-cut.md) | Launches action with a sense of collision; a smash cut is contrast after calm |
| [Match Cut](match-cut.md) | Links through visual rhyme; a smash cut usually refuses correspondence |
| [Graphic Match](graphic-match.md) | Links time through shape; a smash cut breaks continuity |
| [Cross-Cutting](cross-cut.md) | Alternates between two storylines; a smash cut is a single violent cut |

## Film Examples

- *Cat People* (1942): a tense night walk is interrupted by a suddenly arriving bus.
- *Carrie* (1976): a shock after a seeming conclusion.
- *Alien* (1979): a threatening sound followed by a harmless truth.
- *Oppenheimer* (2023): the explosion image appears first, and the blast sound arrives late.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Smash cut: a long, silent shot of a candlelit bedroom at night with a sleeping child, held still; hard cut to a blinding daylight close-up of a roaring jet engine. Darkness to glare, silence to noise, intimate scale to massive, no transition effect.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using a dissolve (too gentle), or the two shots are too close in tone → establish a clear state in the first shot.
- Changing only one parameter → change at least two at once (sound + scale, tone + time) and execute with a hard cut.
