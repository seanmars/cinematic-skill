# J-Cut / L-Cut

Summary: Sound and picture cut at different points, so the audio of one space extends over the picture of another.

## Narrative Function

- Carries thought across locations through sound, with audio leading or trailing its visual source.
- Holds on a listener or an empty room while sound from another space continues, sustaining continuity across spaces.

## How It Works

- J-cut: the next scene's audio starts under the current picture.
- L-cut: the previous scene's audio carries over the next picture.
- The receiving image must hold attention (reaction shot, doorway, walking) so the sound-picture offset reads as deliberate, not a mistake.

## When to Use

- Use: hearing dialogue before revealing the room; holding on a listener's reaction while the other person speaks offscreen; linking unrelated images with continuous sound.
- Avoid: when both spaces need to be seen at the same time.
- Avoid: when a simple silent reaction shot is enough.

## Film Examples

- *The Social Network* (2010): a speaker's voice carries over the listener's face.
- *Heat* (1995): diner dialogue overlaps with reaction shots.
- *Yi Yi* (2000): piano music sounds first, and only later is its source seen.
- *Reservoir Dogs* (1992): upbeat music contrasts with disturbing images.

## Prompt Examples

```text
L-cut: a detective's calm voice keeps talking offscreen as the picture cuts to a close-up of a young suspect in an interrogation room, silently listening, jaw tightening. Audio from the previous office scene carries over the new image; hold on the listening face.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The reaction shot's sound and picture cut at the same time → let the first image hold until the second audio track starts, or offset in reverse.
- The receiving image has nothing to look at → choose a listening face or a transitional space that can carry offscreen sound.
