# Fisheye

Summary: Curvilinear projection bends straight lines outward, wrapping the world around the subject like a dome.

## Narrative Function

- Produces an enveloping, dome-like perspective that distorts the world around the subject.
- Subject centered → feels protectively wrapped at the center; off-center → emphasizes stretching and unreality.

## How It Works

- Uses curvilinear projection; straight lines bend, more so toward the edges.
- Can render as a circular fisheye (a round image inside a black frame) or a diagonal fisheye (filling the rectangular frame).
- Unless distortion is intended, keep faces centered; distortion should occur systematically around a stable center.

## When to Use

- Use: Mental distortion, subjective pressure, comedy, surreal spaces.
- Avoid: Ordinary wide shots, or when straight-line geometry must be preserved.
- Avoid: Confusing it with rectilinear ultra-wide lenses that keep lines straight.

## Film Examples

- *The Favourite* (2018): Palace interiors bend under a wide curvilinear view.
- *Poor Things* (2023): Distorted wide angles continually create strangeness.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on a full-frame fisheye lens: a nervous courtier stands at the center of an ornate palace hallway, the walls, ceiling and floor bowing outward in strong barrel distortion so the room wraps around him like a dome. Curved horizon, systematic distortion, surreal and unsettling.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a rectilinear ultra-wide as a fisheye, or adding random wavy warping → apply systematic barrel distortion around a clear center, with a curved horizon.
