# Vintage Cine Glass

Summary: The optical character of old cinema lenses makes highlights bloom softly, lowers contrast and adds a slight swirl at the edges; the texture comes from the lens, not the grade.

## Narrative Function

- Produces a softer, less clinically cold image through optics rather than post.
- Highlights bloom gently, contrast drops, and edge aberrations may create a swirling feel.
- Builds atmosphere through the camera itself rather than grading.

## How It Works

- Choose genuine vintage or vintage-style spherical cine primes (character glass such as Cooke or K35).
- Shoot at wider apertures to reveal optical aberrations.
- Keep highlights warm and let them bloom naturally instead of hard-clipping like digital images.

## When to Use

- Use: Memory sequences, period pieces, portraits needing organic softness.
- Avoid: VFX work needing sharp plates.
- Avoid: When a simple faded grade on a modern lens is enough.

## Film Examples

- *The Master* (2012): Faces shot with gentler lenses, lower contrast, without digital sharp edges.
- *Phantom Thread* (2017): Likewise renders characters with soft optics.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on vintage spherical cine primes wide open: a 1950s seamstress by a window, highlights gently blooming with soft warm halation, low contrast, slightly swirling soft edges, organic optical softness rather than a digital filter. Intimate, nostalgic portrait.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a sharp modern lens plus a brownish LUT as vintage → prioritize optical softness and highlight bloom rather than relying on grading alone.
