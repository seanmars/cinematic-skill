# 85mm Portrait

Summary: Shooting from farther away compresses the background into a soft secondary plane, making the face the only landscape in frame.

## Narrative Function

- Isolates the character as the primary subject; the background recedes into a soft secondary plane rather than readable space.
- The face is the "landscape" of the whole frame.

## How It Works

- The camera stands farther back than normal conversational distance, then uses 85mm to bring the subject back to its original size.
- The more distant viewpoint makes facial proportions more flattering, while background objects are relatively enlarged and compressed.
- Isolation comes from viewpoint and compression, not just shallow depth of field.

## When to Use

- Use: When the face or the character's state of mind is the focus of the scene.
- Avoid: Master shots, group compositions, environmental portraits.
- Avoid: Sequences that need architectural context as a narrative partner.

## Film Examples

- *Her* (2013): Faces isolated against soft cityscapes, making the person the subject of the frame.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on an 85mm portrait lens from a distance: head-and-shoulders close-up of a young man lost in thought, the city lights behind him compressed into a soft, creamy wash. Flattering facial proportions, rapid focus falloff, the face as the entire landscape.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Standing too close with a shorter focal length, or using 50mm at conversational distance, resulting in an enlarged nose and a clearly readable room behind → move back and switch to 85mm, making sure the face is still large enough and the background stacks into a secondary plane.
