# Anamorphic

Summary: Squeezed horizontally during capture and restored to widescreen on projection, yielding oval bokeh and horizontal streak flares.

## Narrative Function

- Achieves a wide frame with a moderate focal length.
- Oval bokeh, horizontal flares and distinctive edge characteristics declare "cinema" on their own, without adding letterbox bars.

## How It Works

- Cylindrical elements squeeze one dimension of the image during capture (usually 2x), which is desqueezed on projection.
- Produces oval out-of-focus highlights and horizontal highlight streaks.
- Focus breathing behaves differently from spherical lenses.

## When to Use

- Use: When the anamorphic lens itself should be part of the visual language, such as night scenes with oval practical lights, or portraits in a vast city.
- Avoid: When clinical sharpness and round bokeh are needed.
- Avoid: Using it only to solve aspect ratio.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Widescreen night interiors and city imagery.
- *Inception* (2010): Most of the live-action footage shot with anamorphic lenses.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on 2x anamorphic lenses: a detective walks through a rainy neon street at night, out-of-focus streetlights rendered as tall oval bokeh, a blue horizontal streak flare crossing the frame from a passing car's headlights. Widescreen 2.39:1, correct desqueezed proportions.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a letterboxed spherical image as anamorphic, or just overlaying a flare effect without oval highlights → ensure correct desqueezed geometry, with real oval bokeh and horizontal streak behavior.
