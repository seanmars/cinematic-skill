# Tilt-Shift

Summary: Rotating the focal plane leaves only a narrow band of sharpness, making real scenes look like miniature models.

## Narrative Function

- Rotating the focal plane creates selective sharpness; shot from high above, the real world can look like a toy model.
- The shift function adjusts perspective without rotating the camera.

## How It Works

- Tilt the lens so the focal plane cuts through the subject like a thin band.
- Blur should vary with depth, not be one even sheet of blur.
- Use shift separately when perspective distortion needs correcting.

## When to Use

- Use: Isolating focus through a crowd on a tilted focal plane, turning cityscapes into a diorama-like look.
- Avoid: Using it as a substitute for ordinary portrait shallow depth of field.
- Avoid: Arbitrarily adding horizontal blur bars to a flat image.

## Film Examples

- *The Social Network* (2010): The Henley regatta sequence uses a high angle with an extremely thin focal plane, making real boats look like toys.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Tilt-shift miniature effect: a high-angle view of a busy town square with tiny people and cars, only a thin horizontal band across the middle in sharp focus, blur increasing gradually toward the top and bottom following the scene's depth. Saturated colors, toy-like diorama look.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using ordinary portrait bokeh or flat blur bars that ignore depth → use a high angle, subjects at real human scale, and a focal plane that varies with depth, so the blur graduates with perspective.
