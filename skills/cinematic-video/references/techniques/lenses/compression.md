# Telephoto Compression

Summary: Shot with a long lens from far away, depth between foreground and background flattens, and distant objects seem pressed right behind the subject.

## Narrative Function

- Depth planes are compressed; distant objects seem pressed onto the subject.
- Movement appears slower, and escape routes seem to vanish.
- Creates a crowded feeling even in wide-open locations.

## How It Works

- Move the camera away from the subject until near and far elements look similar in size, then frame tightly with a telephoto (135-200mm).
- The effect comes from the distant viewpoint, not just the lens itself.
- Atmospheric rendering (haze, heat waves) must match the shooting distance.

## When to Use

- Use: When surrounding terrain, crowds or architecture should feel oppressive and inescapable.
- Avoid: When space needs to recede naturally.
- Avoid: Mistaking a close-range wide-angle crop or a simple zoom for compression.

## Film Examples

- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): Sherif Ali approaches through desert heat waves, distance compressed.
- *Payback* (1999): Characters and cars seem locked together in depth.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Telephoto compression from a distant camera position: a runner on a city street appears pressed against a wall of skyscrapers and traffic behind him, near and far elements nearly the same size. 200mm lens, stacked flattened planes, hazy air, claustrophobic crowded feeling.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Labeling a close-range image as telephoto, or the frame still having obvious receding space → move farther back, use a long lens, and make elements at different depths look similar in size.
