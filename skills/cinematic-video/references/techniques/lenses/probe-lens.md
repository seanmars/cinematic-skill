# Probe Lens

Summary: A tiny front element on a long, slender barrel reaches between ice cubes, machinery or food, capturing extreme close viewpoints an ordinary camera can't reach.

## Narrative Function

- Takes the camera into spaces the body can't reach, such as ice cubes, machinery, food, miniature sets.
- Captures extreme close-range images from "inside" the subject while the camera body stays outside.

## How It Works

- Mount a slender barrel with a tiny front element, and carefully plan the barrel's movement path in advance.
- Light from outside, or illuminate from within using fiber optics.
- Stay stable: tiny movements are magnified into large displacements on screen.
- Apertures are usually small, yet shallow depth of field still needs handling.

## When to Use

- Use: Shots that need to pass through tight spaces ordinary gear can't enter.
- Avoid: Using it as a substitute for overhead tabletop macro.
- Avoid: Facial portraits.

## Film Examples

- Food and product commercials: Consistently use probe lenses to weave between bubbles and garnishes.
- *The Hobbit* (2012) series: Miniature set sequences showcase a tabletop-world shot grammar.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Probe lens shot: a tiny wide-angle lens glides between towering ice cubes and rising bubbles inside a glass of soda, the lemon slice looming like a giant wall. Impossible proximity, smooth barrel-path movement, backlit sparkling liquid, miniature-world scale.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistaking an ordinary macro or interior wide-angle lens for a probe lens → make sure the viewpoint originates inside a tight space, moves along the barrel path, and carries a wide-angle sense of scale.
