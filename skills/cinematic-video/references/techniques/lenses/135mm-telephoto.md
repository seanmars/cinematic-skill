# 135mm Telephoto

Summary: Shot with a long lens from far away, the background is compressed against the subject, and the isolation carries a sense of eavesdropping and surveillance.

## Narrative Function

- Compresses distance, crowding the background against the subject for a surveillance or eavesdropping effect.
- The isolation leans voyeuristic rather than intimate.
- Unsuitable for cramped interiors or tender dialogue moments.

## How It Works

- The camera stands far away and uses a long focal length to crop tightly on the distant view.
- Compression comes from viewpoint distance, not the glass itself.
- Depth of field is very shallow; atmospheric haze becomes visible over long distances.

## When to Use

- Use: Sporting events, rooftop surveillance, layered stacked city scenes.
- Avoid: Dinner-table coverage, intimate dialogue, establishing geography that needs a sense of receding depth.

## Film Examples

- *The Conversation* (1974): The long lens places the protagonist within the crowd yet isolated from it.
- Heat makes Los Angeles street scenes stack up in layers under the long lens.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on a 135mm telephoto lens from across a busy plaza: a woman in a red coat on a phone, crowds and building facades behind her compressed tightly against her figure. Thin depth of field, slight atmospheric haze, the feeling of being watched from far away.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with 85mm (standing too close, effect too gentle), or a wide-angle image mislabeled as telephoto → put as much distance as possible between camera and subject so the background presses tightly against the figure.
