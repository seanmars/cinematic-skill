# 24mm Wide

Summary: An environmental portrait where person and setting matter equally; space reads clearly, with only moderate perspective stretch.

## Narrative Function

- Produces environmental portraits that give subject and setting equal status.
- Suits reportage-style interior and street coverage; space is a narrative partner, not an effect.
- Avoids extreme distortion while keeping human scale.

## How It Works

- The camera steps back a few paces, usually at eye level.
- On full frame or Super 35, it gives moderate perspective stretch and a wide field of view.
- Foreground elements such as hands and furniture look prominent and need deliberate placement.

## When to Use

- Use: When characters and environment must meaningfully coexist.
- Avoid: When space should overwhelm the subject, or the subject needs isolating.
- Avoid: Extreme environmental immersion, or tight portraits.

## Film Examples

- *Children of Men* (2006): Threatened interiors and streets, with geography and character action both clearly readable.
- *The Hurt Locker* (2008): Likewise uses this focal length so the dangerous environment and characters coexist.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Shot on a 24mm wide lens at eye level from several feet away: a journalist stands full-body in a cluttered, war-damaged apartment, the room's architecture clearly readable around her. Moderate perspective stretch, handheld reportage feel, natural light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistakenly rendered as 14mm (exaggerated swelling foreground, cartoonish edges) or 35mm (framing too tight) → keep a few paces of distance, maintain full-body framing and readable architecture, and keep distortion moderate.
