# Tableau

Summary: Characters carefully arranged within a fixed, wide, frontal frame, like a moving painting, letting the audience read the relationships at their own pace.

## Narrative Function

- Meaning unfolds inside a fixed frame; the audience reads who stands where at their own pace.
- Subtle gestures become significant because the frame is still.
- Suits comedy, ritual and scenes that build tension through restraint; not suited to action or moments that expose deception.

## How It Works

- Design positions, spacing and eyelines in advance.
- Coordinate the timing of each character's action within the same frame.
- Depth of field must keep every focal point legible.
- Usually locked on a tripod; slow movement is optional but changes the effect.

## When to Use

- Use: ceremonies, ensemble comedy, processions, scenes where the audience should discover the whole picture themselves.
- Avoid: lingering on hollow or purely decorative arrangements whose elements have no relationship.

## Film Examples

- *PlayTime* (1967): Gags scattered across wide compositions held for a long time.
- *The Color of Pomegranates* (1969): Frontal, ritualized arrangements of figures and objects.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Tableau composition: a locked-off wide frontal shot of a family dinner, twelve relatives arranged along a long table with clear spacing, each frozen in a distinct readable pose like a living painting, only the grandmother slowly raising her glass. No camera movement, deep focus.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating it as a master shot to be broken up right away, or a posed group with no relationships → give everyone purposeful action and let the long hold itself complete the coverage, not editing.
