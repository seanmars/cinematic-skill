# Triangular Composition

Summary: Three visual masses form a pyramid; the apex represents power or focus, the base represents support or threat.

## Narrative Function

- Uses geometry to establish hierarchy: the apex is usually the seat of power, desire or judgment.
- The two base points can be supporters or potential threats.
- Derives from the compositional tradition of Renaissance painting and altarpieces.

## How It Works

- Arrange three distinct masses so the three vertices are clearly readable.
- Use a moderately wide lens (about 27-40mm), or a long lens from a distance, so all three points read clearly.
- Depth of field must keep the intended focal point sharp.
- Use different heights (sitting, standing, leaning in a doorway) to make a true triangle.

## When to Use

- Use: groups with inherent hierarchy, such as leadership scenes, conflicted families, negotiations.
- Avoid: forcing in a third element just to complete the triangle.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): Sitting, standing and waiting by the door in the office form a pyramid of power.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Triangular composition: a crime boss sits in a high-backed chair at the apex in the back of a dim office, two lieutenants stand lower-left and lower-right in the foreground, forming a clear pyramid of power. 35mm lens, warm low-key light, Renaissance-style blocking.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Three people at the same height, with no visible triangle → create height differences by sitting, standing or leaning in a doorway to make a true pyramid.
