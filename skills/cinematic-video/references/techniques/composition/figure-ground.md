# Figure-Ground

Summary: Separate the subject from the background with value, color or focal plane so the audience spots the point at a glance.

## Narrative Function

- Ensures the subject is recognizable at first glance.
- When subject and background share similar values, the character "disappears," forcing the audience to search.
- Deliberate blending (camouflage, hiding) is the exception.

## How It Works

- Place the subject against a simpler or clearly contrasting background, separating with value, color or focal plane.
- Backlight outlines the silhouette; shallow depth of field softens the background.
- Changing wardrobe is often cheaper than altering the set.

## When to Use

- Use: as the default for general coverage where the subject must read instantly.
- Avoid: when the story deliberately wants the character to blend in, e.g. an office worker swallowed by the office, a dancer lost in a crowd.

## Film Examples

- Classical Hollywood: Rim light, backlight and wardrobe contrast ensured theater audiences could see clearly.
- Westerns placed figures against the sky; film noir placed them against reflections on wet streets.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Strong figure-ground separation: a man in a dark suit stands in front of a pale, evenly lit concrete wall, a thin warm rim light tracing his shoulders and hair. Shallow depth of field softens the background, making the subject instantly readable. 85mm lens.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Clothes the same color as the wall, subject invisible → add a rim light, change background value, swap wardrobe, or use shallow depth of field.
