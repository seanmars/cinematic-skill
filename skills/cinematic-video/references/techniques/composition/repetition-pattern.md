# Repetition and Pattern

Summary: Identical units repeat neatly across the frame, then one "exception" is placed; that exception is the story.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes a visual system (army, hotel, factory) so the audience expects consistency.
- The point that breaks the pattern becomes the focus without a close-up.
- Can also work across time, with repeated scenes forming structural echoes.

## How It Works

- Find a repeating unit and adjust the camera position so the arrangement is clearly countable.
- Place the anomaly (a gap, a different color, a motionless person) in a strong compositional position.

## When to Use

- Use: institutional, highly replicated environments.
- Avoid: forcing patterns onto irregular, private spaces, unless the character is trying to impose order.

## Film Examples

- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Repeated doors and uniforms make small differences stand out.
- *The Matrix* (1999): Replicated bodies are themselves a philosophical pattern.
- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): The same route walked again, its meaning now changed.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Repetition and pattern: a long row of identical gray office cubicles with workers in white shirts, all hunched over monitors, except one woman in a yellow coat standing still and staring at the camera on the right third. Countable linear tiling, 35mm, flat fluorescent light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Cluttered production design with no countable repeating unit → choose one motif, adjust the camera position so the arrangement is clear, and isolate just one inconsistent point.
