# Negative Space

Summary: A small subject surrounded by vast emptiness (sky, walls, snow, sea), where the emptiness itself carries the weight of the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Uses "emptiness" to carry emotion, expressing loneliness, unease and waiting.
- The empty area acts like a character, sometimes outweighing the person.
- The emptiness must be deliberately designed, not underexposure or accidental space.

## How It Works

- Make the subject small and place it in a corner or along the bottom of a large, simple area.
- Deliberately control gaze direction, headroom and background detail to keep the emptiness clean.
- A long lens isolates the person; a wide lens makes the space expand endlessly.
- Hold the imbalance; don't instinctively "correct" it.

## When to Use

- Use: moments of isolation, waiting, or when the landscape should overwhelm the character.
- Avoid: mistaking a dull background for designed emptiness.
- Avoid: information-dense scenes that need to establish many narrative elements.

## Film Examples

- *Ida* (2013): Faces pressed to the bottom edge beneath large expanses of architecture and sky.
- *Mr. Turner* (2014): Figures submit to vast atmospheric space.
- *L'Avventura* (1960): The island and empty coastline outweigh the characters searching for someone.
- *Her* (2013): Pale interiors and city air become compositional partners.
- *No Country for Old Men* (2007): The barren landscape refuses the characters any comfort.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Negative space composition: a tiny figure in a dark coat stands at the bottom-right corner of the frame, beneath a vast, flat overcast sky that fills most of the image. Minimal detail, muted gray palette, long lens isolation, a feeling of quiet loneliness.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a cluttered extreme wide shot as negative space, with a busy horizon competing with the character → simplify the empty area; if the complexity is intentional, think in terms of Architexture instead.
- The empty area accidentally underexposed, looking like a mistake → give the emptiness a deliberately designed texture and brightness.
