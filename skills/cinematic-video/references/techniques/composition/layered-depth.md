# Layered Depth

Summary: Foreground, midground and background each have their own action, so one shot presents several relationships at once.

## Narrative Function

- Reveals social relationships and power structures through spatial placement, without dialogue.
- Who occupies which layer shows who controls the situation and what matters most.

## How It Works

- Distribute meaningful characters or objects across planes at different distances, with clear gaps between layers.
- Use a wider lens and greater camera distance to create deep space naturally.
- When several layers must be sharp, stop down and add light.

## When to Use

- Use: when two or more relationships must be visible in the same shot without cutting.
- Avoid: when only one layer truly carries the story and the others are decoration.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Deep Focus](../effects/deep-focus.md) | Optical, front-to-back sharpness; layered depth is spatial staging and can pair with selective focus |
| [Foreground Interest](foreground-interest.md) | Requires only a near layer; layered depth requires the far layers to work too |
| [Rule of Thirds](rule-of-thirds.md) | Provides a dynamically balanced base framework |

## Film Examples

- *Citizen Kane* (1941): Adults decide the boy's future indoors while the child keeps playing in the snow outside the window.
- *The Best Years of Our Lives* (1946): The bar is split into two dramatic focal points, the piano and the phone booth.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Layered depth staging in one shot: in the foreground a mother signs a document at a table, in the midground a lawyer waits by the fireplace, and through the back window a child plays alone in the snow. Wide 28mm lens, deep staging, all three planes readable.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistaking a "deep-focus background" for layered depth: everything is sharp but there is only one action → add a second storyline on another layer, or simplify to a single-plane portrait.
