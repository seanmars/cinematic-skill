# Clean Frame

Summary: No accidental intrusions on any of the four edges; the frame belongs entirely to the subject, crisp as a studio poster.

## Narrative Function

- Ownership of the space belongs solely to the subject.
- Achieves isolation through compositional discipline (not shallow depth of field), conveying control and intent.
- Not suited to a voyeuristic feel or documentary aesthetics.

## How It Works

- Watch all four edges and exclude any stray objects.
- Clear the frame with set dressing and flags, not just by cropping clutter with a long lens.
- Staying clean on a wide lens shows more compositional control than compressing the frame.

## When to Use

- Use: product shots, ceremonial scenes, portraits, storytelling in controlled environments.
- Avoid: depicting stolen moments, or crowded documentary scenes that need realism.

## Film Examples

- *Drive* (2011): Isolates characters in the night with poster-like discipline.
- Product photography: Clears the surroundings so objects look monumental.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Clean frame: a single man in a satin jacket leans against a car under a streetlight, all four edges of the frame completely empty with no stray objects or foreground occluders. 32mm lens, crisp studio-poster clarity, controlled night lighting.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Calling an uncleared street scene poster-like, or mistaking shadows for cleanliness → deliberately clear the edges, or simply go with Dirty Frame.
