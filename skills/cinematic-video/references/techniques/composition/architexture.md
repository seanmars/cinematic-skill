# Architexture

Summary: Building facades and materials fill the frame as the protagonist, with people as small details that provide scale.

## Narrative Function

- Lets architecture replace people as the subject.
- Shrinks people into small compositional elements that establish scale.
- Shows people swallowed by institutions, cities or workspaces.

## How It Works

- Expose for the material surface; use raking or flat light to emphasize texture and graphic quality.
- A long lens compresses building planes into layers of texture; or put a wide lens close to the wall so people appear tiny.
- Stop down to keep material detail sharp.

## When to Use

- Use: when the place matters more than the character, e.g. arriving in a city, labor scenes, documenting monuments.
- Avoid: as the only coverage for an intimate dialogue scene; expressions still need tighter shot sizes.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [One-Point Perspective](one-point-perspective.md) | Forms a vanishing-point tunnel; architexture treats material as a flat, tactile plane |

## Film Examples

- *Playtime* (1967): Glass grids and waiting rooms dominate the composition; visitors become texture within modernist geometry.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Architexture composition: a massive brutalist concrete facade with a grid of identical windows fills the entire frame, raking late-afternoon light revealing its rough texture, while a single tiny office worker walks along the base for scale. Telephoto compression, deep focus.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Extreme close-up of a face with a famous building blurred in the background → pull back or lengthen the focal length until the body becomes one module in the architecture; otherwise admit it is a portrait.
