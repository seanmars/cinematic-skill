# Rule of Thirds

Summary: Place the subject on an intersection of the 3x3 grid, leaving the larger side for the gaze, landscape or threat.

## Narrative Function

- Allocates frame "ownership": the subject takes one cell, the rest goes to gaze direction, environment or potential danger.
- Off-center placement lets the frame breathe, less formal and solemn than centering.
- It is a default, not a law; centered, symmetrical or oppressive compositions often break it deliberately.

## How It Works

- Overlay a 3x3 grid on the monitor; place eyes, head or landmarks on intersections, not in the middle of cells.
- Put the horizon on the upper or lower third line, not across the middle of the frame.
- Focal length affects the result: ultra-wide exaggerates the outer thirds, long lenses compress them into bands; 35-50mm renders them most faithfully.
- Keep the camera level unless the story requires otherwise.

## When to Use

- Use: landscapes, single shots looking off-screen, establishing shots that need placement without being too formal.
- Avoid: applying it to every close-up, which drains the composition of meaning.
- Avoid: ceremonial scenes, claustrophobic oppressive compositions, or shots that need poster-like symmetry.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Golden Ratio](golden-ratio.md) | Divisions at about 38/62%, more refined, with a spiral eye path |
| [Lead Room](lead-room.md) | Specifically horizontal space ahead of movement or gaze, often a natural result of the rule of thirds |

## Film Examples

- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): Desert horizons on the third lines let the landscape dominate.
- *Days of Heaven* (1978): Wheat fields and sky divided in thirds.
- *There Will Be Blood* (2007): Characters pushed to one side so the land outweighs them.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Rule-of-thirds composition: a lone traveler stands on the lower-left intersection of the frame, gazing right into open space. The horizon sits on the upper third line, with the wide empty landscape filling the remaining two-thirds. Natural 40mm perspective, level camera.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Looks like thirds but is actually a centered portrait (equal space above and on both sides) → move the eyes to an intersection and enlarge the space in the gaze direction, or explicitly switch to Central Framing.
- Horizon cutting through the middle of the frame → place it on the upper or lower third line depending on emphasis.
