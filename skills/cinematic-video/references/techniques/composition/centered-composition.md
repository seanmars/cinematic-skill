# Centered Composition

Summary: The camera squarely faces the back wall and the whole frame is organized along the central axis, with the subject as the focal point of an altar.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys control, ritual, ceremony and a sense of fate.
- The subject is "enshrined" at the center by the whole space; the frame feels designed.
- Not suited to chases, eavesdropping or dialogue that needs natural off-screen looks.

## How It Works

- The camera is perpendicular to the back wall, shooting frontally.
- Left and right masses are balanced; deliberate imbalance can serve as a comedic beat.
- Use about 27-40mm so the order of the whole room is visible; a long lens crops out spatial context.

## When to Use

- Use: characters in seats of power, ceremonial entrances, stylized comedy, moments that need a "designed" feel.
- Avoid: chases, whispered conversations that need voyeuristic intimacy.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Central Framing](central-framing.md) | Only requires the subject on the crosshair; centered composition requires order across the whole frame |
| [Symmetry](symmetry.md) | Requires left-right mirroring; centered composition allows an asymmetrical room |
| [One-Point Perspective](one-point-perspective.md) | Adds a tunnel converging into depth; centered composition keeps a flat altar feel |
| [Tableau](tableau.md) | Fixes the whole ensemble as if frozen |

## Film Examples

- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Frontal, altar-like interiors where any departure from the central axis stands out.
- *Moonrise Kingdom* (2012): Likewise uses frontal, centered interiors to create order and deadpan humor.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Centered composition: the camera faces the back wall squarely, a stern headmaster sits at a desk on the exact center axis, bookshelves and lamps balanced on both sides. 32mm lens showing the whole orderly room, pastel palette, ceremonial stillness.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Face dead center with a chaotic background (bullseye headshot + random background) → square up the camera, balance the masses on both sides, or honestly switch to Central Framing.
- Camera not perpendicular to the wall, lines skewed → correct the angle so architectural lines are level and plumb.
