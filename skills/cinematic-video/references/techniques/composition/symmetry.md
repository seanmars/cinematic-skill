# Symmetry

Summary: The frame mirrors left and right around a central axis, with architecture, furniture and lines matching each other to form near-obsessive order.

## Narrative Function

- Mirrored masses create a sense of ritual and controlled space.
- Within perfect order, any small disruption is magnified into an event.
- Not suited to improvised, natural moments unless artifice is intended.

## How It Works

- Place the camera on the scene's centerline, shooting frontally.
- Align architectural elements, furniture and verticals on both sides; check with a level.
- Stop down to keep both sides sharp.

## When to Use

- Use: inherently ordered environments such as hotels, temples, spaceships, courtrooms, controlled interiors.
- Avoid: forcing it onto naturally asymmetrical scenes without narrative reason.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Centered Composition](centered-composition.md) | Focuses on subject position; symmetry focuses on the mirrored structure of the whole frame |
| [One-Point Perspective](one-point-perspective.md) | Relies on vanishing-point depth; symmetry also works in flat compositions |
| [Rule of Thirds](rule-of-thirds.md) | Dynamic balance, contrasting with symmetry's deliberate order |
| [Repetition and Pattern](repetition-pattern.md) | Repeated units suffice; no mirror axis required |
| [Asymmetry](asymmetry.md) | Deliberately rejects mirroring |

## Film Examples

- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Symmetrical hotel interiors like blueprints.
- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Left-right balanced order represents futuristic design intent.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Perfect bilateral symmetry: the camera sits on the exact centerline of a long marble courtroom, matching columns, benches and hanging lamps mirrored on both sides. True vertical lines, level horizon, deep focus, an atmosphere of rigid, obsessive order.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Actor centered but the two sides of the room don't match, creating a sense of disconnect → align the camera with the architectural axis, or switch to an asymmetrical composition with a clear counterweight.
- Lens slightly tilted, verticals skewed → use a level and correct perspective.
