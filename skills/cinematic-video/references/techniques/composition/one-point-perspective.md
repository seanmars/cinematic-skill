# One-Point Perspective

Summary: The camera squarely faces the architectural axis and all parallel lines converge to a single vanishing point, creating tunnel-like depth and ceremonial order.

## Narrative Function

- Creates tunnel-like depth or ceremonial order, implying fate, design and control.
- The structure itself demands symmetry, so anyone off the central axis stands out.

## How It Works

- Align the lens perpendicular to the building's centerline, shooting frontally.
- Use a tilt-shift lens or adjust the camera position to keep verticals straight.
- Place the vanishing point on or behind the subject.
- A moderately wide lens (24-35mm) best shows the funnel-like convergence.

## When to Use

- Use: designed, fate-sealed interiors such as hotels, spaceships, churches, bunkers.
- Avoid: forcing it onto irregular spaces such as forests.
- Avoid: mistaking a centered person in a flat room for one-point perspective.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Leading Lines](leading-lines.md) | Lines can converge but need no complete axial structure |
| [Centered Composition](centered-composition.md) | Subject on the central axis, but without tunnel depth |
| [Vanishing Point](vanishing-point.md) | The convergence point itself; in two-point perspective it can sit off-axis |
| [Symmetry](symmetry.md) | Often accompanies one-point perspective but isn't required |
| [Architexture](architexture.md) | Flattens facades into material rather than directional depth |

## Film Examples

- *The Shining* (1980): The Overlook Hotel's corridors point straight to fate.
- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Axially symmetrical futuristic interiors.
- *Léon* (1994) and *The Grand Budapest Hotel* (2014): Squared-up corridors establish order.

## Prompt Examples

```text
One-point perspective: the camera is perfectly squared to the centerline of a long, empty hotel corridor, walls, ceiling lights and carpet pattern all converging to a single vanishing point behind a small child standing at the far end. 24mm lens, true verticals, eerie symmetrical depth.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting a centered person in a corridor from a corner, producing two vanishing points → move onto the central axis until both sides converge equally, or explicitly adopt two-point perspective.
