# Atmospheric Haze

Summary: Air scattering fades distant planes layer by layer and lowers contrast, using aerial perspective to convey vast distance.

## Narrative Function

- Makes successive distant planes progressively brighter and lower in contrast, conveying immense distance.
- Treats depth as "weather," not just physical space.

## How It Works

- Keep the foreground sharp and rich in detail.
- Background planes rise in brightness and lose contrast, usually shifting toward the sky's color.
- Can come from real dust or humidity; can also be simulated in the grade if the physics stays consistent.

## When to Use

- Use: Wide shots that need layered recession (deserts, cities, valleys, seascapes).
- Avoid: Close-range obscuring effects (use fog or smoke instead).

## Film Examples

- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): The desert fades into the distance; marching troops look like part of the weather.
- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Stacked orange, fading planes make the city's kilometers of distance perceptible.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A lone rider on a ridge in the foreground, crisp and high-contrast, overlooking a vast desert valley. Behind him, successive mountain ranges fade step by step into paler, bluer, lower-contrast silhouettes until the farthest peaks nearly merge with the sky.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The foreground is milky too → Keep near subjects sharp and high in contrast.
- All planes graded the same → Make each receding plane noticeably paler; particles should be too small to see.
