# Sparks and Embers

Summary: Self-luminous, short-lived sparks and embers streak through darkness, bringing danger, heat and an industrial feel.

## Narrative Function

- Builds an industrial or apocalyptic atmosphere with glowing particles.
- Suggests danger, heat and labor (welding, fire, cigarettes).
- Particles glow on their own and are short-lived, unlike cold dust.

## How It Works

- First establish a heat source (welding torch, grinder, bonfire, cigarette).
- Let gravity and physics set trajectories: sparks fall and bounce, embers can rise and drift.
- Expose for both particles and subject detail, or accept a silhouette.
- Keep the surroundings dark so particles stand out.

## When to Use

- Use: Factories, night fires, ruined cities, the moment a match is struck.
- Avoid: Random orange specks in pastoral scenes with no plausible heat source.
- Avoid: Cold, slow particles, particles that accumulate, or scenes without fire.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Orange particles become industrial weather.
- *The Dark Knight Rises* (2012): Flames and debris fly through the air in night action scenes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
In a dark factory at night, a welder crouches over a steel beam and a fountain of bright orange sparks sprays from the torch, arcing down under gravity and bouncing off the concrete floor. The glowing particles light up his mask from below while the surrounding shop stays in deep shadow.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Orange-tinted dust or snow with no heat source → Establish a heat source and make particles self-luminous.
- Particles hover indefinitely → Apply gravity so sparks are short-lived and fall along trajectories; darken the surroundings.
