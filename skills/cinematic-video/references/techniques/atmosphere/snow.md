# Snow

Summary: Large backlit flakes drift down and accumulate, footprints leave traces, and the whole world goes quiet.

## Narrative Function

- Shows visible winter silence through falling and accumulating ice crystals.
- Records passage through footprints; lowered contrast makes the world muffled.
- Backlit flakes against sodium lamps or sunlight add emotional weight.

## How It Works

- The air needs large, distinct flakes (live action or digital).
- Light from behind or the side to separate flakes from the background.
- Let snow accumulate on surfaces (wool, hair, railings, ground).
- Control slanted or vertical fall through changes in wind direction.

## When to Use

- Use: Waiting on train platforms, hunts, small-town crime, quiet city scenes.
- Avoid: When you only need pale air without accumulation (use mist instead).
- Avoid: When what falls is liquid (use rain instead).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Rain](rain.md) | Forms streaks and mirror reflections; snow covers and leaves a record |
| [Mist](mist.md) | Hangs without accumulating; snow lands and stays |
| [Blue Hour](../lighting/blue-hour.md) | A sky condition often paired with snow; not weather itself |
| [Dust Motes](dust-motes.md) | Indoor particles; snow is outdoor accumulation |

## Film Examples

- *Fargo* (1996): Snow is at once ground, air and isolation.
- *Let the Right One In* (2008): Falling flakes and a muffled suburb.
- *The Revenant* (2015): Backlit characters amid falling and accumulated winter snow.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A small-town train platform at night during heavy snowfall: large distinct flakes drift down, lit from behind by an orange sodium lamp so each one glows against the dark. Snow piles on the bench, the railings and a waiting woman's wool coat, a single line of footprints trailing behind her in the muffled, low-contrast scene.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using white-tinted rain or noise grain instead of snow → Use large backlit flakes.
- Snow doesn't accumulate → Show visible snow buildup and footprints, and lower overall contrast.
