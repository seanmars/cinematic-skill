# Fog

Summary: Dense fog turns lamps into glowing orbs, shrinks the visible world to a few meters, and lets figures slowly emerge from the white.

## Narrative Function

- Turns lights into orbs and hides spatial geography.
- Delays a character's appearance, creating suspense or horror.
- Narrows the visible world to the immediate surroundings, like a flashlight beam.

## How It Works

- Fill the space with dense droplets using a fog machine or real weather.
- Contrast drops rapidly with distance; things become unreadable within a short range.
- Backlight / side light creates halos around lamps; frontal light flattens the fog.
- Practical lights must be placed in the fog, not rely solely on lens filters.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments when a character should slowly emerge, or the space should suddenly "disappear."
- Use: Horror, docks, mysterious scenes.
- Avoid: Wide shots that need clear depth or distant landscape detail.

## Film Examples

- *The Exorcist* (1973): Fog shrouds the house, streetlights and figures.
- *The Others* (2001): Dense fog swallows the estate, cutting it off from the outside world.
- *Sicario* (2015): A dusty, fog-like veil erases the desert's boundaries.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A deserted harbor at night swallowed by thick fog: visibility ends a few meters away, and dock lamps become soft glowing globes hanging in the gray. A lone figure slowly emerges from the murk, only gaining shape and detail as she comes close to the camera.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Rendered as atmospheric haze or just a desaturated grade → Force a short visibility distance.
- Buildings several blocks away still clear, lamps without halos, faces sharp at 30 meters → Add backlit lamp halos and delay the character's appearance.
