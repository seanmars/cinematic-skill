# Dust Storm

Summary: Wind-whipped dust turns the environment into an active enemy; the sun becomes a dull coin and characters are reduced to silhouettes.

## Narrative Function

- Portrays the environment as an active adversary rather than a backdrop.
- Creates silhouettes and a hostile environment, with visibility dropping to near opacity.
- Suits sieges, border crossings and convoys; not calm desert portraits or indoor dust motes.

## How It Works

- Fill the foreground and midground air with wind-driven particles (fuller's earth, real sand or digital sand).
- Use strong directional wind; backlight or sunlight through the dust silhouettes the subject.
- Let sand cling to highlights on skin and vehicles; reduce contrast within working distance.

## When to Use

- Use: Ambushes, border crossings, narrow escapes on the road.
- Avoid: Merely applying a warm grade to still dunes.
- When used, flags must whip and pores must be caked with sand.

## Film Examples

- *Sicario* (2015): Desert particles erase the border, making daylight hostile.
- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): Walls of sand rush in; the sun looks like a coin.
- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): Wind-blown sand reads as an attack, not distant haze.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A military convoy pushes through a violent desert sandstorm: wind drives thick horizontal streams of grit across the frame, and the sun is only a dull pale disk behind the brown haze. Soldiers appear as dark silhouettes, their scarves whipping, sand clinging to sweaty skin and the dirty highlights of the trucks.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Clean pores, limp flags, a bright white sun and a clear horizon (like haze or just a brown grade) → Add strong wind and airborne sand close to camera.
- No silhouettes or traces of dust → Make the sun a dull disk and coat highlights with sand.
