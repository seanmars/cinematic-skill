# Mist

Summary: Light moisture divides the space into successively paler planes while the subject stays clearly visible.

## Narrative Function

- Builds layered atmospheric depth while keeping the subject recognizable.
- Conveys lyrical mystery, like the breath of early morning or the mountains, rather than concealment.

## How It Works

- Use low-density droplets or a natural temperature inversion.
- Backlight separates the planes in the air.
- Near subjects keep their contrast; distant planes rise in brightness and drop in saturation.

## When to Use

- Use: Dawn hunts, mountain passes, damp gardens.
- Avoid: Horror-style "pea soup," or filling an interior (unless a window faces a cold dawn).
- Avoid: When the story needs spatial geography to vanish completely (use fog instead).

## Film Examples

- *The Revenant* (2015): Early-morning mountain moisture stacks into pale layers around the characters.
- *Raise the Red Lantern* (1991): The courtyard air is milky, but the architecture stays clearly visible.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Dawn in a misty pine valley: a hunter stands sharp and clear in the foreground while three soft layers of pale mist recede behind him, each farther ridge lighter and less saturated. Low backlight from the rising sun glows through the moisture, which drifts slowly without ever whiting out the scene.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Density too high, the subject disappears and it becomes fog → Keep the subject clearly readable.
- Only the horizon fades, becoming haze → Stack 2-3 progressively paler receding planes and backlight the moisture.
