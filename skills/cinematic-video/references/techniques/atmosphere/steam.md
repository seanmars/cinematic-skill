# Steam

Summary: A heat source meeting cold air releases white water vapor that billows upward and is blown apart by wind.

## Narrative Function

- Shows white vapor created where heat meets cold (trains, manhole covers, showers).
- Conveys an industrial feel or an intimate temperature event.
- Clearly distinct from smoke (combustion) and fog (ambient weather).

## How It Works

- Put the heat source in frame (chimney, drain grate, kettle, showerhead).
- Steam rises and is dispersed by wind shear.
- Backlight renders the steam translucent bright white with visible condensation.
- Emphasize wetness and glow.

## When to Use

- Use: Winter train platforms, street grates, bathrooms, factories.
- Avoid: Generic alley atmosphere with no heat source.
- Avoid: When the scene is burning (use smoke instead), or is only cold and wet without heat (use fog / rain instead).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Smoke](smoke.md) | Combustion particles, gray or colored; sinks or leaves stains |
| [Fog](fog.md) | Ambient water droplets with no heat source |
| [Atmospheric Haze](haze.md) | Long-distance scattering, unrelated to a local heat source |

## Film Examples

- *The Untouchables* (1987): Locomotive steam forms a violent white veil.
- *Drive* (2011): Steam rising from street grates at night.
- *Spirited Away* (2001): The bathhouse steam becomes interior weather.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A freezing winter night on a city street: thick white steam billows up from a manhole grate, glowing bright and translucent in the backlight of a streetlamp. A gust shears the plume sideways as a man in a long coat walks through it, droplets of condensation glistening in the air.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Wrong color or source, looking like smoke → Establish a clear heat source and show white, translucent upward motion.
- No rising heat, looking like fog → Add backlight and wind shear so the steam rises and disperses.
