## Framing and Shot Size (25)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Extreme Close-Up](framing/extreme-close-up.md) | The frame holds only a single part of a face or object (eye, mouth, fingertip); the detail itself is the event of the scene. |
| [Choker Shot](framing/choker.md) | Framed from hairline to chin, the face fills the frame with no shoulders visible, trapping the character inside their own face. |
| [Close-Up](framing/close-up.md) | Frames the head and shoulders, making subtle facial expressions the focus of the image. |
| [Medium Close-Up](framing/medium-close-up.md) | Framed from the chest up, keeping dialogue and expressions clear while retaining body language such as gestures. |
| [Medium Shot](framing/medium-shot.md) | Framed from the waist up, facial expressions and gestures are equally clear while the background only establishes the basic setting. |
| [Cowboy Shot](framing/cowboy-shot.md) | Framed from mid-thigh up, with the hands and any weapon at the waist in frame and the feet out of frame. |
| [Full Body](framing/full-body.md) | Head to toe fully in frame, the figure fills the frame height and the environment is just a backdrop. |
| [Long Shot](framing/long-shot.md) | The figure's full body is recognizable but noticeably small, with the surrounding landscape or architecture dominating the frame. |
| [Extreme Long Shot](framing/extreme-long-shot.md) | The environment overwhelms everything; the figure is just a tiny scale marker in a vast space. |
| [Establishing Shot](framing/establishing-shot.md) | Before detailed coverage begins, tells the audience "where this is and when it is". |
| [Master Shot](framing/master-shot.md) | A single camera position captures the whole scene's space and blocking, serving as the skeleton for later editing. |
| [Two-Shot](framing/two-shot.md) | Two main characters share the frame; the distance between them is itself the narrative. |
| [Three-Shot](framing/three-shot.md) | Three main characters share the frame in a triangular composition, with alliances, hierarchy and exclusion written directly into the blocking. |
| [Group Shot](framing/group-shot.md) | Frames a small group as a single subject while still revealing its internal hierarchy. |
| [Insert Shot](framing/insert.md) | A close shot of a story-critical object, hand, text or screen, conveying facts a wide shot can't show clearly. |
| [Cutaway](framing/cutaway.md) | Briefly leaves the main action to cut to a related detail, onlooker or environment, then returns to the main line. |
| [Reaction Shot](framing/reaction-shot.md) | Shows how a character "receives" an off-screen event or line; meaning comes from juxtaposition with the previous shot. |
| [Over-the-Shoulder Frame](framing/over-the-shoulder-frame.md) | Shoots past the blurred shoulder of one person in the foreground to the sharp face of the person opposite, keeping their relationship in frame throughout. |
| [Single](framing/single.md) | The frame belongs to one main character only; even if the other person is present, they are erased by the composition. |
| [Cut-In](framing/cut-ins.md) | Tightens from a wide shot onto a detail already present in the frame, without changing spatial orientation. |
| [Gesture](framing/gesture.md) | Tightly frames a subtle body movement, making "the movement itself" the narrative subject. |
| [Interview](framing/interview.md) | The subject answers while looking at an unseen questioner beside the camera, making the audience a witness to testimony. |
| [Product](framing/product.md) | A commercial portrait that treats the object as the star, with designed lighting making its form, material and reflections clearly readable. |
| [Video Portraits](framing/video-portraits.md) | A long gaze at one person in a fixed shot size, where blinking and breathing are the entire content. |
| [Wide Shot](framing/wide-shot.md) | The place is the subject of the frame; figures are visible but secondary, and the spatial geography reads clearly. |

## Camera Angles (19)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Eye Level](camera-angles/eye-level.md) | The camera sits at the subject's eye height with a level line of sight and straight horizon, presenting an equal, neutral view. |
| [High Angle](camera-angles/high-angle.md) | The camera sits above eye level looking obliquely down; the character shrinks but the face stays clear as the floor and spatial layout emerge. |
| [Low Angle](camera-angles/low-angle.md) | The camera sits below eye level looking up, so the character towers like a building, bringing authority or menace. |
| [Bird's-Eye View](camera-angles/birds-eye.md) | Looking almost straight down from high above, turning people and objects into symbols and patterns on a map. |
| [Worm's-Eye View](camera-angles/worms-eye.md) | The lens sits on or below the ground tilting sharply up, verticals converging toward the zenith as the subject becomes a towering structure. |
| [Overhead Top-Down](camera-angles/overhead.md) | The lens points straight down at 90 degrees, flattening the scene into a floor plan with people as graphic elements in the composition. |
| [Ground Level](camera-angles/ground-level.md) | The lens sits on the ground, making boots, hems, tires and pavement the subject as the world rises sharply from the ground plane. |
| [Dutch Angle](camera-angles/dutch-angle.md) | The camera rotates around the lens axis, tilting the horizon and verticals so the world loses its balance. |
| [Point of View](camera-angles/pov.md) | The camera is the character's eyes; the audience "becomes" them and sees what they see. |
| [Profile](camera-angles/profile.md) | The camera sits at 90 degrees to the face, showing only one eye and one ear, with the bridge of the nose defining the outline. |
| [Three-Quarter Angle](camera-angles/three-quarter.md) | The face turns about 45 degrees; both eyes are visible, the far cheek recedes, and the head shows three-dimensional volume. |
| [Reverse Angle](camera-angles/reverse-angle.md) | Moves the camera to the other side of the axis so the audience sees whom the character is looking at or talking to. |
| [Trunk Shot](camera-angles/trunk-shot.md) | The camera hides in a car trunk or box looking up as people lean over the opening; the audience becomes the hidden thing. |
| [Hip Level](camera-angles/hip-level.md) | The lens sits at a standing character's belt / holster height, making hands and weapons as prominent as faces. |
| [Shoulder Level](camera-angles/shoulder-level.md) | The camera sits slightly below the eyes, around shoulder height, like a companion walking alongside. |
| [First-Person](camera-angles/first-person.md) | The camera is the character's body, with hands, breath, tools and footsteps in frame, sustained over time. |
| [Fourth Wall](camera-angles/fourth-wall.md) | A character looks directly into the lens, acknowledging the audience and pulling the onlooker into the scene as an accomplice. |
| [Object POV](camera-angles/object-pov.md) | The camera is rigidly mounted to an object, so a tool, vehicle or device "sees" while the world moves around it. |
| [Incline](camera-angles/incline.md) | The horizon tilts only a few degrees, quietly hinting at unease rather than loudly announcing it like a Dutch angle. |

## Lighting (41)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Three-Point Lighting](lighting/three-point-lighting.md) | The key shapes, the fill opens the shadows, and the backlight traces the hair edge, making the face dimensional and separated from the background. |
| [Key Light](lighting/key-light.md) | The dominant source that sets the subject's lighting direction, shadow shape and exposure baseline. |
| [Fill Light](lighting/fill-light.md) | Lifts the shadows opposite the key, determining how much detail remains visible in the dark areas. |
| [Backlight](lighting/backlight.md) | A source behind the subject, facing the camera, outlines the edges and separates the subject from the background. |
| [Rim Light](lighting/rim-light.md) | A narrow edge highlight traces the subject's shape while the face itself stays dark. |
| [Kicker](lighting/kicker.md) | A hard highlight from the rear side that skims the edge of the far jaw or cheek. |
| [Hair Light](lighting/hair-light.md) | A small light from high behind on the crown of the hair, keeping dark hair from merging into a dark background. |
| [Eye Light](lighting/eye-light.md) | A faint, small frontal light that leaves only a tiny catchlight in the pupils, keeping a dark face alive. |
| [High-Key Lighting](lighting/high-key.md) | Bright, low-contrast and shallow shadows, keeping the whole frame clean and open. |
| [Low-Key Lighting](lighting/low-key.md) | High contrast with shadows dominating the frame; only a few areas are lit. |
| [Chiaroscuro](lighting/chiaroscuro.md) | A single source sculpts the figure's volume out of darkness; the strong contrast of light and dark carries moral weight. |
| [Rembrandt Lighting](lighting/rembrandt.md) | A high key at 45 degrees leaves a small triangle of light under the eye on the shadow-side cheek. |
| [Butterfly Lighting](lighting/butterfly-lighting.md) | The key shines down from directly above the camera, forming a small butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose for a symmetrical, glamorous face. |
| [Loop Lighting](lighting/loop-lighting.md) | The key sits slightly off-axis; the nose shadow forms a small loop angled toward the mouth corner, not connecting with the cheek shadow. |
| [Split Lighting](lighting/split-lighting.md) | The key comes from 90 degrees to the side, leaving the face half lit and half dark along the midline. |
| [Short Lighting](lighting/short-lighting.md) | The key lights the cheek turned away from the camera, while the near side facing the camera falls into shadow. |
| [Broad Lighting](lighting/broad-lighting.md) | The key lights the near cheek facing the camera, making the face look open, bright and readable. |
| [Silhouette](lighting/silhouette.md) | Exposing for a bright background turns the subject into a fully black shape that tells the story through outline alone. |
| [Motivated Lighting](lighting/motivated-lighting.md) | Every light appears to come from a plausible source in the story world, hiding the traces of lighting. |
| [Practical Lighting](lighting/practical-lighting.md) | Lamps, neon, candles and screens visible in frame actually light the characters. |
| [Available Light](lighting/available-light.md) | No added lights; only the sun, sky and practical fixtures already present at the location. |
| [Naturalistic Ambient](lighting/naturalistic-ambient.md) | Soft interior or sky light with plausible direction; even with added lights, no deliberate lighting is visible. |
| [Bounce Light](lighting/bounce-light.md) | A light or sunlight is aimed at a reflective surface, which then lights the subject, softening shadows while keeping direction. |
| [Hard Light](lighting/hard-light.md) | A small, distant source produces sharp shadow edges, amplifying texture and contrast. |
| [Soft Light](lighting/soft-light.md) | A large source wraps the subject with softly graduated shadow edges while keeping direction. |
| [Side Lighting](lighting/side-lighting.md) | Light from about 90 degrees to the side leaves half the form bright and half in shadow, bringing out texture and volume. |
| [Top Light](lighting/top-light.md) | The source sits directly overhead; forehead and shoulders catch light while the eye sockets sink into shadow. |
| [Under-Light](lighting/under-light.md) | A source below the chin shines upward, so shadows climb in reverse onto cheekbones and eye sockets, looking eerie. |
| [Cross Lighting](lighting/cross-lighting.md) | Two opposing keys hit the subject from both sides, producing edge light on each side and charged stage energy. |
| [Window Light](lighting/window-light.md) | Large, directional light enters through a window and gradually falls off toward the depth of the room. |
| [Candlelight](lighting/candlelight.md) | A small, warm flame source with very fast falloff and natural flicker; faces are carved by firelight while the surroundings sink into darkness. |
| [Neon Practicals](lighting/neon-practical.md) | Neon tubes in frame actually serve as the key or strong accent, staining figures with their own magenta and cyan. |
| [Golden Hour](lighting/golden-hour.md) | Low-angle warm sun around sunrise or sunset, casting long shadows with amber directional light. |
| [Blue Hour](lighting/blue-hour.md) | Cool twilight after the sun has dropped below the horizon while the sky still glows, paired with freshly lit warm artificial lights. |
| [Dappled Light](lighting/dappled-light.md) | Hard light passes through irregular occluders such as leaves, water or blinds, scattering broken patches of light across the subject. |
| [Gobo Lighting](lighting/gobo-lighting.md) | Blinds, bars or cut-out patterns placed in the beam project graphic shadows onto figures and spaces. |
| [Volumetric Light](lighting/volumetric-light.md) | Beams pass through haze, dust or rain, making the light's path through the air visible. |
| [Cameo Lighting](lighting/cameo-lighting.md) | Only the figure is lit; the environment sinks into pure black, so the figure seems to emerge from a void. |
| [Epiphany Lighting](lighting/epiphany.md) | At the moment of a character's realization, a motivated light suddenly finds their face, making the inner shift visible. |
| [Spotlight](lighting/spotlight.md) | A theatrical fixture throws a hard-edged circular pool, isolating the subject within it, surrounded by darkness. |
| [Glam](lighting/glam.md) | A large soft source near the lens makes skin refined and eyes bright, presenting the face as a star product. |

## Composition (32)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Rule of Thirds](composition/rule-of-thirds.md) | Place the subject on an intersection of the 3x3 grid, leaving the larger side for the gaze, landscape or threat. |
| [Golden Ratio](composition/golden-ratio.md) | Arrange the frame by a ratio of about 1:1.618 and a spiral path so the eye flows naturally along the curve to the subject. |
| [Central Framing](composition/central-framing.md) | The subject faces the lens dead on the optical axis with equal space on both sides, as if placed on a display stand. |
| [Centered Composition](composition/centered-composition.md) | The camera squarely faces the back wall and the whole frame is organized along the central axis, with the subject as the focal point of an altar. |
| [Symmetry](composition/symmetry.md) | The frame mirrors left and right around a central axis, with architecture, furniture and lines matching each other to form near-obsessive order. |
| [Asymmetry](composition/asymmetry.md) | The subject sits to one side while color blocks, light sources or objects counterweight the other, giving a balanced frame with tension. |
| [Leading Lines](composition/leading-lines.md) | Use real lines such as rails, roads, corridors or shadows to lead the eye to (or through) the subject. |
| [Frame Within a Frame](composition/frame-in-frame.md) | Doors, windows, arches or mirrors form a second frame inside the frame, defining whether the subject is watched, trapped or displayed. |
| [Negative Space](composition/negative-space.md) | A small subject surrounded by vast emptiness (sky, walls, snow, sea), where the emptiness itself carries the weight of the frame. |
| [Foreground Interest](composition/foreground-interest.md) | Place an object close to the lens between camera and subject to create depth and put the audience "inside" the scene. |
| [Layered Depth](composition/layered-depth.md) | Foreground, midground and background each have their own action, so one shot presents several relationships at once. |
| [Diagonal Composition](composition/diagonal-composition.md) | The camera stays level while diagonals in the scene (stairs, shadows, crowds) cut across the frame, creating momentum and unease. |
| [Triangular Composition](composition/triangular-composition.md) | Three visual masses form a pyramid; the apex represents power or focus, the base represents support or threat. |
| [Headroom](composition/headroom.md) | Control the distance from the top of the head to the top edge of the frame; tight creates pressure, loose lets the space above take over. |
| [Lead Room](composition/lead-room.md) | Leave more space ahead of the subject's gaze or direction of travel so the frame points to "what comes next." |
| [Short Siding](composition/short-siding.md) | The face is pushed to the frame edge it looks toward, with a large space left behind the head, so the character seems to have nowhere to go. |
| [Visual Weight](composition/visual-weight.md) | Brightness, color, sharpness, faces and motion decide what is seen first in the frame; the pull on attention doesn't equal physical size. |
| [Repetition and Pattern](composition/repetition-pattern.md) | Identical units repeat neatly across the frame, then one "exception" is placed; that exception is the story. |
| [Figure-Ground](composition/figure-ground.md) | Separate the subject from the background with value, color or focal plane so the audience spots the point at a glance. |
| [Tonal Contrast](composition/contrast-composition.md) | The brightest and darkest masses confront each other head-on, giving the frame poster-like graphic impact so the subject cannot be missed. |
| [One-Point Perspective](composition/one-point-perspective.md) | The camera squarely faces the architectural axis and all parallel lines converge to a single vanishing point, creating tunnel-like depth and ceremonial order. |
| [Vanishing Point](composition/vanishing-point.md) | Parallel lines converge to a point in the distance, turning "distance" and "destination" into the meaning of the frame. |
| [Dirty Frame](composition/dirty-frame.md) | The frame edges are occupied by out-of-focus shoulders, glass, foliage or crowds, like a scene glimpsed in secret. |
| [Clean Frame](composition/clean-frame.md) | No accidental intrusions on any of the four edges; the frame belongs entirely to the subject, crisp as a studio poster. |
| [Look Space](composition/look-space.md) | Leave space in the direction of the character's gaze so the unseen off-screen target commands attention. |
| [Architexture](composition/architexture.md) | Building facades and materials fill the frame as the protagonist, with people as small details that provide scale. |
| [Reflections](composition/reflections.md) | Through mirrors, glass or water, the watcher and the watched, two spaces, appear in one frame at once. |
| [Screen in Screen](composition/screen-in-screen.md) | A screen or display is embedded in the frame; the audience watches "the person watching," or an interface takes over the whole frame. |
| [Tableau](composition/tableau.md) | Characters carefully arranged within a fixed, wide, frontal frame, like a moving painting, letting the audience read the relationships at their own pace. |
| [Void](composition/void.md) | The environment is erased entirely, isolating the character in pure black or pure white nothingness. |
| [Voyeur](composition/voyeur.md) | The audience peeks from behind cover while the subject is completely unaware, giving the frame a transgressive tension. |
| [Windows](composition/windows.md) | A grid of windows on a building facade shows many slices of life at once, so a single shot works like a montage. |

## Time and Motion (21)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Slow Motion](time-and-motion/slow-motion.md) | Shot at a high frame rate and played back at normal speed, stretching a brief instant so normally invisible physical detail emerges. |
| [Fast Motion](time-and-motion/fast-motion.md) | Shot at a low frame rate or retimed in post so continuous action plays back faster than real time. |
| [Speed Ramp](time-and-motion/speed-ramp.md) | Within one continuous shot, speed slows from normal into a key instant, then accelerates back to normal. |
| [Freeze Frame](time-and-motion/freeze-frame.md) | Time stops on a single frame; image and grain go completely still while sound can continue. |

| [Step Printing](time-and-motion/step-printing.md) | Shot at a low frame rate with a long shutter, then frames are repeated, so motion smears within each frame and stutters forward between frames. |
| [Reverse Motion](time-and-motion/reverse-motion.md) | Recorded action plays backward, so effect precedes cause and the laws of physics visibly invert. |
| [Bullet Time](time-and-motion/bullet-time.md) | The action freezes or nearly freezes while the camera moves around it, turning an instant into an explorable space. |
| [Long Take](time-and-motion/long-take.md) | A continuous, uncut long shot that preserves the full performance and duration. |
| [Oner](time-and-motion/one-er.md) | Looks like a single continuous shot, flowing through multiple connected spaces and actions; hidden stitches are acceptable. |
| [Motion Blur](time-and-motion/motion-blur.md) | The subject or camera moves during exposure, smearing the image and turning light sources into streaks. |
| [Stutter / Stop-Stutter](time-and-motion/stutter.md) | Frames are irregularly dropped or repeated so time advances in jumps, creating an anxious rhythm. |
| [Boomerang](time-and-motion/boomerang.md) | A short action plays forward, then retraces its path in reverse, forming a toy-like back-and-forth loop. |
| [Infinite Loop](time-and-motion/infinite-loop.md) | The last frame joins seamlessly back to the first, so the action always moves forward yet never ends. |
| [Stop Motion](time-and-motion/stop-motion.md) | A physical puppet or model is adjusted slightly for each frame shot, building up motion frame by frame. |
| [Low Shutter](time-and-motion/low-shutter.md) | Extremely short exposure per frame makes motion crisp and staccato rather than blurred and fluid. |
| [Timelapse Glam](time-and-motion/timelapse-glam.md) | On a locked-off camera, the face stays stable while hair and wardrobe change rapidly in the stylists' hands. |
| [Timelapse Human](time-and-motion/timelapse-human.md) | The protagonist stands still in normal time while the surrounding crowd is sped up into a flowing blur. |
| [Timelapse Landscape](time-and-motion/timelapse-landscape.md) | The terrain is the protagonist; clouds, light and shadows sweep over stable ground at a non-human pace. |
| [Frozen in Motion](time-and-motion/frozen-in-motion.md) | The environment and bystanders are entirely still; only designated characters or the camera can move through the frozen world. |
| [Moonwalk](time-and-motion/moonwalk.md) | The performer glides backward in a dance move, appearing to walk forward while moving backward, as the world itself runs forward normally. |

## Editing and Transitions (23)

| Technique | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| [Match Cut](editing/match-cut.md) | Two self-contained shots joined by a hard cut through correspondence in shape, position, motion or sound, letting the audience "see" their conceptual link at the cut. |
| [Graphic Match](editing/graphic-match.md) | Two consecutive images share the same dominant shape, line or color block in the same position, forming a visual rhyme on a hard cut. |
| [Match on Action](editing/match-action.md) | An action starts in one shot and finishes in the next, hiding the cut in the motion so the audience follows the action without noticing the change. |
| [Jump Cut](editing/jump-cut.md) | Cut out a stretch of time within nearly identical framing, making the subject "jump" in frame so the break in time is plainly visible. |
| [Smash Cut](editing/smash-cut.md) | At a quiet or seemingly concluding moment, hard cut abruptly to an image completely opposite in brightness, volume and shot size, creating sharp contrast. |
| [Dissolve](editing/dissolve.md) | The outgoing shot gradually fades out while the next fades in, the two images briefly overlapping for a soft transition. |
| [Fade In / Fade Out](editing/fade.md) | The image gradually sinks into a solid color (usually black) or emerges from it, like punctuation for a sequence. |
| [Wipe](editing/wipe.md) | A visible moving edge sweeps across the frame, "pushing" the previous image out for the next. |
| [Iris](editing/iris.md) | A circular (or other shaped) mask opens from black or closes down onto a detail, marking the focal point like a stage spotlight. |
| [Cross-Cutting](editing/cross-cut.md) | Cut back and forth between two or more locations or storylines, implying they happen simultaneously and are converging. |
| [Split Screen](editing/split-screen.md) | Two or more image panels side by side in one frame, letting different spaces coexist in the same moment. |
| [Montage](editing/montage.md) | Create meaning through the order of a series of different shots, so inference comes from the collision between shots rather than any single image. |
| [Axial Cut](editing/axial-cut.md) | Keep the angle and hard cut along the same lens axis to a closer (or wider) shot size, so the focal point "jumps" toward the viewer. |
| [Invisible Cut](editing/invisible-cut.md) | Hide the cut in motion, darkness, occlusion, blur or a digital seam, making multiple shots look like a single continuous take. |
| [J-Cut / L-Cut](editing/l-cut-visual.md) | Sound and picture cut at different points, so the audio of one space extends over the picture of another. |
| [Crash Cut](editing/crash-cut.md) | Hard cut straight into an event or its aftermath with no setup, opening on a sense of collision. |
| [Flash Cut](editing/flash-cut.md) | Insert a few frames of a different image into an ongoing scene, then immediately hard cut back to the present. |
| [Match Motion Cut](editing/match-motion.md) | The subject's direction and speed of motion stay consistent across the cut, but the world around it has completely changed. |
| [Match Split](editing/match-split.md) | In a split screen, a line, eyeline or motion continues across the seam to the other side, joining two worlds into one whole. |
| [Quick Cuts](editing/quick-cuts.md) | Short shots from multiple angles cut at an accelerating pace with progressively tighter shot sizes, using speed itself to build tension. |
| [Set Transition](editing/set-transition.md) | The camera keeps moving while the surrounding walls and set transform into another location within the shot, instead of changing scenes with a cut. |
| [Transitions](editing/transitions.md) | Design the cut itself as a visible gesture, making "how we leave this scene" part of the narrative. |
| [Fragments](editing/fragments.md) | Break an event into many partial shots without ever showing the whole, letting the audience assemble the action in their heads. |
