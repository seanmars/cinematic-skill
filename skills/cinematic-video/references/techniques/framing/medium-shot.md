# Medium Shot

Summary: Framed from the waist up, facial expressions and gestures are equally clear while the background only establishes the basic setting.

## Narrative Function

- The standard workhorse shot size for dialogue and hand-action scenes, also called a waist shot.
- Balances expression and body language, touching on environmental information only lightly.
- Not suited to emphasizing movement, full costume, or moments needing very close facial detail.

## How It Works

- Bottom edge cuts roughly at the waist / belt line.
- Usually 40-50mm, shot from conversational distance with the camera between chest and eye height.
- Background is enough to establish the place without competing with the subject.

## When to Use

- Use: Overlapping dialogue, dinner-table scenes, or when props in hand or gestures matter.
- Avoid: When the focus is below the hips or on the full-body costume.
- Avoid: When micro-expressions need to be read (use a tighter shot size).

## Film Examples

- *His Girl Friday* (1940): Gestures and setting stay visible during rapid-fire dialogue.
- *Lost in Translation* (2003): Conversation scenes use medium shots to keep posture and space.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Medium shot of a bartender framed from the waist up, both hands visible polishing a glass, the bar shelves softly readable behind him. 50mm lens at chest height, balanced weight between his expression and his gestures.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Framing down to the thighs (becomes a cowboy shot) or cutting at the chest (becomes an MCU) → Make sure the waist falls at the bottom edge of the frame.
