# Long Shot

Summary: The figure's full body is recognizable but noticeably small, with the surrounding landscape or architecture dominating the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Places the character in a world not centered on them, conveying isolation or scale.
- The person is still recognizable but diminished within the geography.

## How It Works

- The subject stands far enough back that the full body is visible, with landscape / architecture occupying most of the frame.
- Usually 24-35mm.
- Depth of field must be sufficient for the scene to read clearly.

## When to Use

- Use: Placing a character within a vast location.
- Avoid: When facial detail is needed or the figure should read as a portrait.
- Avoid: When the figure is too small to recognize (that is an extreme long shot).

## Film Examples

- *Seven Samurai* (1954): Figures in the village space, recognizable but surrounded by the environment.
- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): Riders in the desert, still clear but overwhelmed by the scale of the terrain.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Long shot of a lone hiker standing on a rocky ridge, whole body clearly readable but small, the mountain valley dominating two-thirds of the frame. 28mm lens with deep focus so both the figure and the distant peaks stay sharp.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting a full-body portrait (figure too large) or an extreme long shot dot (figure too small) → The figure must be recognizable, but the world dominates visually.
