# Full Body

Summary: Head to toe fully in frame, the figure fills the frame height and the environment is just a backdrop.

## Narrative Function

- Emphasizes costume, movement and the body's position in space.
- The person remains the subject; the environment only provides context without stealing focus.

## How It Works

- Both head and feet must be in frame, with only a little floor and ceiling space above and below.
- The figure occupies most of the frame height.
- Shoot on 35-50mm from a suitable distance; avoid 24mm up close, which exaggerates and distorts the feet.

## When to Use

- Use: Costume reveals, characters crossing a space, or when overall body language must be seen.
- Avoid: When the scale of the environment is the point (use a long shot or wider).
- Avoid: When thigh-up is enough to tell it (use a cowboy shot).

## Film Examples

- *The Searchers* (1956): A full figure in the doorframe shows the costume before the wilderness takes over.
- *Drive* (2011): Night walks hold a full-body composition, highlighting the jacket and gait.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Full body shot of a dancer standing center frame, head to toe fully visible with only a sliver of floor and ceiling, her figure filling most of the frame height. 40mm lens from a comfortable distance, the rehearsal studio readable around her silhouette.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Figure too small (becomes a long shot) or feet cut off (becomes a cowboy shot) → Keep head and feet in frame, with the figure taking up most of the frame height.
