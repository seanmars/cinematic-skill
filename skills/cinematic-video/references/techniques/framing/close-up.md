# Close-Up

Summary: Frames the head and shoulders, making subtle facial expressions the focus of the image.

## Narrative Function

- Lets the audience read a character's inner decision, hesitation or realization.
- Keeping a bit of shoulder keeps the subject a "person" rather than a cut-up face.

## How It Works

- Spans roughly from the collarbone / upper chest to just above the hairline, with moderate headroom.
- Usually 65-85mm, shot from conversational distance.
- Shallow depth of field can downplay the background, but don't rely on blur alone to carry the shot.

## When to Use

- Use: Key lines and moments of hesitation or realization where micro-expressions must be read.
- Avoid: Using it on every line, turning it into unfocused "wallpaper".
- Avoid: When the important action happens below the chest (hands, holster, doorknob).

## Film Examples

- *The Passion of Joan of Arc* (1928): Head-and-shoulder close-ups build the pressure of the trial.
- *The Silence of the Lambs* (1991): Interrogation dialogue uses close-ups to show thought while keeping a human presence.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Close-up of a man's head and upper shoulders, collar just visible at the bottom edge, modest headroom. 75mm lens, shallow depth of field softening the room behind him as a flicker of doubt crosses his face.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Framing too loose so it becomes a medium close-up, or too tight so it becomes a choker → Keep "head + upper shoulders" and make sure the collar or shoulder line is visible.
