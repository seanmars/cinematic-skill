# Establishing Shot

Summary: Before detailed coverage begins, tells the audience "where this is and when it is".

## Narrative Function

- Conveys location, geography, time and social context.
- Reorients the audience after a time jump or scene change.
- Unlike decorative scenic shots, its placement and length serve the story.

## How It Works

- Choose a viewpoint that reveals spatial relationships: how areas connect, orientation, lighting conditions.
- Usually a wide or extreme long shot, but architectural details, aerials and empty shots also work.
- Hold long enough for the audience to read the time of day, and keep the ambient sound.

## When to Use

- Use: At the start of a scene, or after a shift in time / space.
- Avoid: Inserting one at every cut when the geography hasn't changed.
- Avoid: Confusing it with a master shot that must carry the dramatic action.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Master Shot](master-shot.md) | Carries the whole scene's blocking and action; an establishing shot only conveys time and place |
| [Extreme Long Shot](extreme-long-shot.md) | A shot size, often used to perform the establishing function |
| [Wide Shot](wide-shot.md) | Environment-led coverage within the scene; not the same as an establishing shot |

## Film Examples

- *Late Spring* (1949): Still-life-like images interrupt the characters' exchanges and reorient the scene.
- *Tokyo Story* (1953): Empty rooms and streets act as punctuation between sequences.
- *The Shining* (1980): The drive to the Overlook Hotel establishes the geography first.
- *Blade Runner* (1982): The opening cityscape establishes the era and social order.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Establishing shot at dawn of a small fishing harbor, boats moored along the pier and the village climbing the hillside behind, clearly showing how the dock connects to the main street. Slow drifting fog and gulls as the only motion, holding long enough to read the early morning light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting it as a master that plays the whole scene, or as scenery unrelated to the story → Make sure the shot answers "where and when" before coverage begins.
