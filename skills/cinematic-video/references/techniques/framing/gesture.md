# Gesture

Summary: Tightly frames a subtle body movement, making "the movement itself" the narrative subject.

## Narrative Function

- Treats small movements such as a moving hand, a glance or a shift of weight as the main storytelling element.
- Meaning comes from the movement, not from an object or the setting.

## How It Works

- Frame the moving body part tightly, but keep context for where the movement starts (wrist, shoulder).
- Light so the fingers separate from the background.
- Keep the camera still and let the movement drive the frame.

## When to Use

- Use: When a subtle body movement carries an entire emotional beat.
- Avoid: When the focus is actually a static prop (that is an insert).
- Avoid: Forcing a wide shot when the movement would be lost in a wide master.

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *In the Mood for Love* (2000): Hands that almost touch and glances build affection.
- *Drive* (2011): Movements of gloves and steering wheel convey tension.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Tight gesture shot of a man's hand reaching toward a woman's hand on a cafe table, stopping a finger's width away, then slowly withdrawing. Frame includes the wrist and cuff for context, fingers rim-lit against a dark background, camera completely still.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Framing too wide so the hand movement becomes noise → Size the frame to the moving body part.
- Treating a static prop shot as a gesture → Give the movement a clear start and finish, and let it play out completely.
