# Three-Shot

Summary: Three main characters share the frame in a triangular composition, with alliances, hierarchy and exclusion written directly into the blocking.

## Narrative Function

- Shows the dynamics among three people: who sides with whom, who is on top, who is left out.
- Reveals "the one left out" through spatial blocking rather than editing.

## How It Works

- Arrange the three bodies in a triangle across depth or width.
- All three must be clearly recognizable so the audience can compare them.
- Blocking takes priority over formal composition rules.

## When to Use

- Use: When a three-way relationship drives the story, such as standoffs, verdicts, tense love triangles.
- Avoid: When there are only two people or a large crowd.
- Avoid: When the scene needs everyone to have exactly equal visual weight.

## Film Examples

- *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* (1966): In the graveyard climax duel, the three gunmen share the frame in triangular geometry with similar weight.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Three-shot in a dim office: two partners stand shoulder to shoulder in the mid-ground while a third colleague sits alone in the foreground, forming a clear triangle. All three faces readable, the seated figure isolated in a separate pool of lamplight.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting a two-shot plus a blurry third person, or a very wide group shot → All three leads must be clearly readable, with deliberate triangular blocking.
