# Extreme Long Shot

Summary: The environment overwhelms everything; the figure is just a tiny scale marker in a vast space.

## Narrative Function

- Turns scale into emotion: awe, loneliness, insignificance.
- The environment is the protagonist; the figure only provides a scale reference.

## How It Works

- Must keep a perceptible scale marker, made visible through contrast, motion or placement.
- Use deep focus to preserve spatial layers.
- Without depth cues, the image easily looks like a miniature model.

## When to Use

- Use: When scale itself is the emotional message.
- Avoid: Dialogue scenes or when character detail is needed.
- Avoid: Shooting an empty landscape with no measurable reference.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Long Shot](long-shot.md) | The figure's full body is still recognizable |
| [Wide Shot](wide-shot.md) | Environment dominates, but the body is still visible, not a dot |
| [Establishing Shot](establishing-shot.md) | A function, not a shot size; often uses this scale but can also show buildings or signs |

## Film Examples

- *Lawrence of Arabia* (1962): Riders shrink to a few marks in the desert.
- *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968): Spacecraft and people are swallowed by the cosmic environment.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Extreme long shot of a vast salt flat under a stormy sky, a single tiny figure walking near the horizon as the only human-scale reference. Deep focus with layered foreground cracks, mid-ground haze and distant mountains, the landscape is the true subject.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The figure's posture is still clearly readable (actually a long shot) → Shrink the figure to a merely perceptible mark.
- An empty landscape with no reference looks like a tabletop model → Add a human or object scale reference and spatial depth cues.
