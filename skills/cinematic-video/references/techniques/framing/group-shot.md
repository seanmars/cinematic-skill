# Group Shot

Summary: Frames a small group as a single subject while still revealing its internal hierarchy.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes group belonging and dominance through blocking and lighting (family, team, jury).
- Even lighting flattens hierarchy; strategic light placement reveals who controls the scene.

## How It Works

- Spread members across different depths and heights.
- Use a moderate wide-angle (35-50mm) so the group is clear without feeling distant.
- Position, height and where the key light falls together express internal power relations.

## When to Use

- Use: Presenting a cohesive group as a single subject.
- Avoid: When there are only two or three leads, or when action coverage matters more than the ensemble.
- Avoid: Large crowd scenes.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): Seating, light and standing positions mark status within the family.
- *Parasite* (2019): Group blocking shows class and master-servant relations.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Group shot of a five-person heist crew gathered around a planning table, the leader standing at the head in the only hard key light while the others sit or lean at varying depths. 40mm lens, every face readable, the hierarchy obvious from position and light.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a three-person frame as a group shot → Use a three-shot for three people.
- Framing too wide so faces can't be read → Keep a small, clearly layered group with every face recognizable.
