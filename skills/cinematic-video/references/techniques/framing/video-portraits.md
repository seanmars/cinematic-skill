# Video Portraits

Summary: A long gaze at one person in a fixed shot size, where blinking and breathing are the entire content.

## Narrative Function

- Time itself is the mechanism for creating intimacy.
- Subtle movements such as blinking, breathing and swallowing carry the content in place of dialogue and camera movement.
- The person's presence is itself a complete event.

## How It Works

- Camera locked off, framed as a close-up or MCU.
- Key light simple and stable, unchanged throughout.
- Hold for at least several seconds, barely moving the camera.

## When to Use

- Use: Portrait studies where the person alone is enough to be the subject, or near-still moments that emphasize duration.
- Avoid: Dialogue coverage needing reverses, story inserts, spatial tours.
- Avoid: Effects where a single element loops while everything else freezes (that is a cinemagraph).

## Film Examples

- Andy Warhol *Screen Tests* (1964-1966): The classic template for this approach.
- *The Irishman* (2019): Patient, long-held close-ups let faces persist through duration.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Video portrait of an elderly fisherman in a close-up, camera locked off for a long uninterrupted hold. Soft, constant window light; the only movement is his slow breathing, an occasional blink and a faint shift of the eyes.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating it as dialogue coverage, adding camera movement or cutting on lines → Lock the camera, preserve the subtle life of the face and extend the hold.
