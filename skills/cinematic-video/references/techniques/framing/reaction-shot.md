# Reaction Shot

Summary: Shows how a character "receives" an off-screen event or line; meaning comes from juxtaposition with the previous shot.

## Narrative Function

- The point is reception, not expression.
- Meaning arises from editorial juxtaposition (Kuleshov effect); the audience reads the emotion from context.
- Doesn't rely on exaggerated acting.

## How It Works

- The character's eyeline points toward the source of the stimulus.
- Eyeline direction and look space must match the previous shot.
- Keep the performance restrained and let the edit carry the meaning; no mugging.

## When to Use

- Use: When "seeing / hearing" is itself the key action.
- Avoid: Stacking a reaction shot after every line, or overplaying the expression.
- Avoid: When the speaker's delivery still needs to be seen.

## Film Examples

- *Rear Window* (1954): The same expression on Jeff takes on different meanings when cut against different courtyard images.
- *The Godfather* (1972): Moments of "reception" carry emotional weight.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Reaction shot of a young soldier listening to news from someone off-screen left, eyes fixed on that direction with matching look space on the left of frame. Nearly still, only a slow swallow and a slight tightening of the jaw, no exaggerated expression.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting it as an ordinary close-up of the speaker, or overdoing the expression → Emphasize the receiver's stillness and a clear eyeline direction rather than displaying emotion.
