# Medium Close-Up

Summary: Framed from the chest up, keeping dialogue and expressions clear while retaining body language such as gestures.

## Narrative Function

- The workhorse shot size for dialogue scenes, balancing facial expression and hand movement.
- Conveys a character's state without needing the full body.
- Not suited to passages emphasizing body movement, footwork or architectural space.

## How It Works

- Bottom edge cuts near the chest / sternum.
- Usually 50-75mm, shot from conversational distance.
- Keep a visible strip of background behind the shoulders, with standard headroom.

## When to Use

- Use: Dinner-table conversations, phone calls, interview-style coverage and other situations where gestures matter.
- Avoid: When the key action is at waist height (such as a holster).
- Avoid: When extreme facial detail is needed.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |

## Film Examples

- *Heat* (1995): The diner standoff presents both leads at this distance.
- *The Sopranos* (1999): Much of the dialogue is handled in medium close-ups, keeping spatial information to a minimum.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Medium close-up of two-person dialogue coverage: a woman framed from mid-chest up, shoulders and gesturing hands readable at the lower edge, a thin slice of the kitchen visible behind her. 70mm lens at conversational distance, standard headroom.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Framing so tight it becomes a floating head (actually a close-up) → Bring the chest line back into the composition.
- Belt / waist visible (actually a medium shot) → Tighten to the sternum.
