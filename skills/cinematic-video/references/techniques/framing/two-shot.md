# Two-Shot

Summary: Two main characters share the frame; the distance between them is itself the narrative.

## Narrative Function

- Lets the audience compare the two people's distance, posture and relationship at once.
- Subtle movements such as leaning in or pulling back are visible without a cut.
- Not suited to moments needing one person to dominate the frame, or when a third important character is present.

## How It Works

- The two head sizes can be balanced or deliberately unbalanced.
- Usually 40-50mm, shot at conversational or walking-together distance.
- Decide which face gets focus or lighting priority.

## When to Use

- Use: When the relationship itself is the story, such as diner conversations, walking side by side, shared silence.
- Avoid: Using it as an indecisive wide, leaving neither performance readable.
- Avoid: When one person needs to dominate, or a third important character is in the scene.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Over-the-Shoulder Frame](over-the-shoulder-frame.md) | Favors one face; the other is only a foreground shoulder, not a shared composition |
| [Single](single.md) | Removes the other person entirely |
| [Three-Shot](three-shot.md) | Adds a third main character, changing the power dynamics |

## Film Examples

- *The Apartment* (1960): Two people in the same frame, with spatial relationship as an emotional element.
- *Persona* (1966): Two faces juxtaposed in a rigid shared composition.
- *Before Sunrise* (1995): The pair talking as they walk stay in the same frame throughout.
- *Heat* (1995): Two adversaries facing each other across a diner table; the gap is made visible before cutting to singles.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Two-shot of an elderly couple seated on a park bench in profile, both faces at similar scale, a noticeable gap between them on the bench. 45mm lens at eye level, locked frame; midway through she slowly slides closer without any cut.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating an OTS (one sharp face + blurred shoulder) or separate singles as a two-shot → Keep both leads at similar scale, with the space between them clearly visible.
