# Master Shot

Summary: A single camera position captures the whole scene's space and blocking, serving as the skeleton for later editing.

## Narrative Function

- Covers the complete spatial geography and blocking in one take, protecting the whole scene.
- As the "skeleton", it lets closer shots cut in later while keeping spatial continuity.

## How It Works

- The camera position must cover all key blocking elements: seats, doors, tables, movement paths.
- Faces and props must still be recognizable; don't shoot too far away.
- Usually a wide or a looser two-shot.

## When to Use

- Use: When scene geography and blocking must be clear and continuous.
- Avoid: Treating it as a rigid safety shot while the real performances sit in singles that don't match.
- Avoid: When only the location needs conveying, with no action (that is an establishing shot).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Establishing Shot](establishing-shot.md) | Only conveys time and place; a master shows what happens in the space |
| [Wide Shot](wide-shot.md) | Only describes shot size, not a coverage function |
| [Two-Shot](two-shot.md) | Can serve as a master only when the two-person interaction is fully in frame |
| [Group Shot](group-shot.md) | Counts as a master only when the whole scene's action happens in frame |

## Film Examples

- *12 Angry Men* (1957): A loose master establishes the jury room's seating, giving the singles a stable spatial basis.
- *Nashville* (1975): Multi-character scenes use loose masters to clarify spatial arrangement.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Master shot of a cramped family dinner: the whole table, the kitchen doorway and the front door all in frame, every family member's face readable. Wide static frame on a 32mm lens, the argument plays out continuously as the father stands and walks toward the door.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting an exterior with no action (actually an establishing shot) → A master must carry the whole scene.
- Framing too tight and cutting off essential elements such as doors → Make sure all important bodies and movement paths are in frame, so the whole scene can play out in this one image.
