# Cowboy Shot

Summary: Framed from mid-thigh up, with the hands and any weapon at the waist in frame and the feet out of frame.

## Narrative Function

- Highlights hand position, stance and upper-body action; especially suited to standoffs and weapon scenes.
- When footwork and shoes don't matter, avoids wasting frame space on the lower body.
- Also called an American shot.

## How It Works

- Bottom edge of the frame cuts at mid-thigh.
- Usually 35-50mm, shot from a few steps back.
- Often paired with a hip-level camera position, placing the lens near the subject's belt height.

## When to Use

- Use: Standing standoffs, visible weapons and scenes where gestures carry meaning.
- Avoid: When full-body movement or the complete costume silhouette is needed.
- Avoid: Dialogue scenes seated at a table.

## Film Examples

- *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* (1966): Establishes both gun position and facial expression before cutting to tighter shots.
- *Django Unchained* (2012): Keeps hands and weapons visible throughout the standoff.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Cowboy shot of a gunslinger framed from mid-thigh up, right hand hovering beside the holster on his hip, feet out of frame. Camera at hip level on a 40mm lens, dusty street stretching behind him.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistaking a waist-up or full-body composition for a cowboy shot → Bottom edge cuts at the thigh, hands must be visible, feet must stay out of frame.
