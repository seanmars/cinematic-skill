# Interview

Summary: The subject answers while looking at an unseen questioner beside the camera, making the audience a witness to testimony.

## Narrative Function

- Establishes the social geometry of "answering an invisible questioner"; the audience watches the testimony from the side.
- Unlike direct address to camera or dramatic dialogue, it is a "question and answer" contract.

## How It Works

- Usually an eye-level MCU or a looser close-up.
- Eyeline slightly off-lens, toward the off-screen interviewer; look space opens toward the listener's side.
- Stable composition, simple motivated key light, catchlights in the eyes.

## When to Use

- Use: Documentary testimony, mockumentary confessionals, talking-head passages in fiction.
- Avoid: Two-person dramatic scenes that need over-the-shoulder coverage.
- Avoid: Letting the eyeline accidentally meet the lens (unless deliberately breaking the fourth wall).

## Film Examples

- *The Fog of War* (2003): Stable talking-head framing turns policy discussion into personal testimony.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Documentary interview framing: a retired engineer in a medium close-up at eye level, gaze slightly camera-left toward an unseen interviewer, open look space on the left. Locked-off frame, soft window key with a clear catchlight, hands mostly still.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Eyes accidentally looking straight into the lens → Move the eyeline to the questioner's position beside the lens.
- Using it in a fictional two-person dialogue scene → Make sure there is an implied listener context.
