# Choker Shot

Summary: Framed from hairline to chin, the face fills the frame with no shoulders visible, trapping the character inside their own face.

## Narrative Function

- Strips away body language and any escape; emotion can only come through the eyes and mouth.
- Suits confessions, breakdowns and unavoidable stares.
- Also called a Big Close-Up (BCU).

## How It Works

- Top edge cuts at the hairline, bottom edge at the chin, with almost no headroom.
- Shoot on 75-100mm from portrait distance to avoid facial distortion.
- Camera usually at eye level unless showing a power dynamic; focus on the eye nearer the lens.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments where emotion has nowhere to hide and no upper-body action is needed.
- Avoid: When you need to see shoulders heave, a swallow or similar movement.
- Avoid: When the story point is a single facial feature (that is an extreme close-up).

## Film Examples

- *Persona* (1966): Oppressive facial framing where the actors have nowhere to retreat from the camera.
- *Black Swan* (2010): Tight on the face to convey the character's mental strain.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Big close-up of a woman's face cropped from the hairline to the chin, no shoulders or neck in frame, almost zero headroom. Shot on an 85mm lens at eye level, focus on the nearer eye, the framing stays locked as her lips tremble.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with an extreme close-up (a single facial feature only) or a close-up (shoulders and collar visible) → Strictly bound it from hairline to chin, with everything below the chin out of frame.
