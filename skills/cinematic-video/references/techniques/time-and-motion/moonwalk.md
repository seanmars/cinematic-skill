# Moonwalk

Summary: The performer glides backward in a dance move, appearing to walk forward while moving backward, as the world itself runs forward normally.

## Narrative Function

- Use when the gait itself is the spectacle: dance sequences, supernatural characters defying physics, frictionless ice-like ground.
- Not suited to chases on rough ground, or to expressing emotional "restoration".

## How It Works

- The performer keeps the body upright, one foot slides back while the other pushes, heels flat, with no walking motion.
- The camera can be locked off or track laterally.
- This is a real performance, not reversed footage.

## When to Use

- Use: Moments where the way of moving is itself visually striking, such as wet, glossy asphalt at night or stage performances.
- Avoid: Reversing footage of normal walking to fake it.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Reverse Motion](reverse-motion.md) | The whole environment is reversed; in a moonwalk the world still runs forward |
| [Double Dolly](../camera-movement/double-dolly.md) | Performer and camera move together; a moonwalk relies purely on the footwork illusion |
| [Boomerang](boomerang.md) | Back-and-forth loop; a moonwalk is a continuous glide |

## Film Examples

- *Moonwalker* (1988): Michael Jackson established this vocabulary through music videos and film, with many later works paying homage.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Moonwalk glide: a dancer in a loose white shirt slides smoothly backward across wet night asphalt, torso upright, feet staying flat as one foot pushes while the other slips back. His shirt trails with the motion, the city and passing cars move forward normally, no reversed footage, no floating or spinning.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Actually normal backward walking or reversed footage, with heels lifting, arms swinging and the environment reversed → glide with soles flat and keep the world running forward.
- Turns into floating or spinning → keep feet on the ground and the body from rotating.
