# Stutter / Stop-Stutter

Summary: Frames are irregularly dropped or repeated so time advances in jumps, creating an anxious rhythm.

## Narrative Function

- Expresses a damaged mind: obsession, panic, a disordered sense of time.
- Cuts time into fractured jumps rather than smooth transitions; the rhythm itself carries meaning.

## How It Works

- On clean footage, drop or duplicate frames at irregular intervals.
- Alternatively, lower the in-camera sampling rate directly.
- Space and character identity must stay continuous so the audience perceives a "time glitch" rather than a spatial cut.

## When to Use

- Use: Sequences of psychological stress, anxiety attacks or altered states of consciousness.
- Avoid: When aiming for romantic smearing, geographic discontinuity, or just a stylistic texture.
- Avoid: Using it to imitate Wong Kar-wai-style smearing or Godard-style cutting.

## Film Examples

- *Pi* (1998): Staccato, high-contrast frame skips linking anguish to numerical rhythm.
- Music videos often make motion stutter on the beat.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Stutter effect: a man paces his cramped apartment and his motion skips forward in irregular jerks, some frames repeating, others dropping out. Locked camera and fixed framing, crisp images with no dreamy blur, an anxious broken rhythm without digital compression glitches.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with step printing or a jump cut, with motion still smooth or positions jumping → keep the space consistent.
- Random stutter rhythm with no rule → plan the dropped and repeated frames deliberately.
