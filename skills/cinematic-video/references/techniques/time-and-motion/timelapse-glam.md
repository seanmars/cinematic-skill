# Timelapse Glam

Summary: On a locked-off camera, the face stays stable while hair and wardrobe change rapidly in the stylists' hands.

## Narrative Function

- Presents transformation as "labor in progress" rather than an instant costume change.
- The stylists and wardrobe activity are the moving elements; the face is the fixed anchor.
- The clock runs on dressing-room time, not the outside world's time.

## How It Works

- The camera is locked in a fixed position.
- Character identity, eyeline and composition stay unchanged throughout.
- Wardrobe and hair change step by step at clearly readable intervals; stylists' hands may cross the frame, but facial features must stay stable.

## When to Use

- Use: Styling scenes before a public appearance, or when the transformation process itself carries narrative meaning.
- Avoid: Skyline time-lapses, fast-running crowds or frozen-world situations.

## Film Examples

- Originated in Vogue transformation shorts and fashion week coverage.
- A common device in music videos for a star's rapid outfit changes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Glam time-lapse from a locked-off camera: a singer sits perfectly still facing the mirror, her face and eyeline unchanged, while stylists' hands blur around her and her hair and outfit transform through several distinct looks. Interval-sampled change, no cuts and no morphing of her facial features.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Facial structure drifting, shooting only the city, or using an edited costume-change montage → keep face shape and eyeline consistent and use interval sampling.
- The transformation process isn't visible → make the stylists' hand activity clearly visible.
