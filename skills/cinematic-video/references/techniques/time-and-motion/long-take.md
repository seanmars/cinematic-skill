# Long Take

Summary: A continuous, uncut long shot that preserves the full performance and duration.

## Narrative Function

- Builds tension through sustained duration, letting contradictory situations coexist in the same space (e.g. danger and the everyday happening at once).
- The audience is anchored in spatial geography rather than jumping between isolated fragments.

## How It Works

- Foreground, midground and background action must be aligned on the same timeline.
- The camera moves along a planned path (Steadicam, crane, handheld, dolly), always keeping a character as the visual center.
- Requires extensive rehearsal of blocking, eyelines and background action.

## When to Use

- Use: When narrative tension would drain away the moment the audience leaves the space.
- Avoid: Showy corridor walks with no action in depth.
- Avoid: When close-up isolation would serve the scene better.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Oner](one-er.md) | Designed to look uncut and may hide stitches; a long take is genuinely continuous |
| [Steadicam](../camera-movement/steadicam.md) | Only a camera-movement tool; doesn't guarantee duration |
| [Master Shot](../framing/master-shot.md) | A wide shot that provides editing flexibility; a long take is often the whole scene |

## Film Examples

- *Atonement* (2007): The Dunkirk beach sequence moves through soldiers and wreckage.
- *The Player* (1992): The studio-lot opening, one conversation after another.
- *Touch of Evil* (1958): The car-bomb sequence, with bystanders oblivious.
- *Russian Ark* (2002): A tour of the Winter Palace in a single take for the entire film.
- *Victoria* (2015): One night shot continuously for the entire film.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Single unbroken long take: the camera follows a nurse through a crowded field hospital, soldiers being treated in the foreground, stretchers carried through the midground and an explosion flashing far in the background, all timed together. Steady tracking path, she stays the visual anchor, no cuts at all.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a stitched oner as truly uncut; or a crowded wide frame with no timed action → genuinely uncut, with action layered in depth.
- Unclear camera path, no character anchor → plan a clear camera path and carry the whole shot with one character.
