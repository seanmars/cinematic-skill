# Step Printing

Summary: Shot at a low frame rate with a long shutter, then frames are repeated, so motion smears within each frame and stutters forward between frames.

## Narrative Function

- Uses repeated frames plus in-frame motion blur to convey longing and temporal instability.
- Separates a lonely character from the surrounding bustle, e.g. one person in a city crowd.
- Carries a memory-like instability, without the feel of a digital glitch.

## How It Works

- Shoot at a frame rate far below 24 fps, and lengthen the shutter time to create blur.
- Duplicate frames to fill the output frame rate.
- Result: smearing within each frame, jumpy advance from frame to frame.

## When to Use

- Use: Walks in the rain, night scenes, chases that feel remembered.
- Use: When emphasizing a character's alienation from urban clamor.
- Avoid: When impacts must be clearly analyzed, for mechanical fight choreography, or when digital-feeling anxiety should be shown.

## Film Examples

- *Chungking Express* (1994): Blurred, stuttering crowd movement and chase sequences.
- *Fallen Angels* (1995): An irregular urban motion texture, used for expressive sequences.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Step-printed look: shot at a very low frame rate with a long shutter, each frame repeated so motion advances in visible, countable steps. A man in a raincoat stays legible in the foreground while neon-lit crowds smear into streaks around him, lyrical and dreamlike, not smooth slow motion.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turns into clean, smooth high-frame-rate slow motion → explicitly ask for a low frame rate, long shutter, visible repeated frames and a countable rhythm.
- Generic digital noise appears → rely on in-frame smearing and repeated frames, avoiding glitch effects.
