# Speed Ramp

Summary: Within one continuous shot, speed slows from normal into a key instant, then accelerates back to normal.

## Narrative Function

- Acts like a magnifying glass for time: energy holds on entry and exit while a motivated instant in between (an impact, a glance, a reaction) is stretched.
- Makes the delayed beat feel worth watching closely.

## How It Works

- Shoot at a high frame rate so both fast and slow sections have enough detail.
- Identify the key event, design the acceleration/deceleration curve and pair it with matching motion blur.
- Maintain the mechanical continuity of the action through speed transitions.

## When to Use

- Use: A single key instant within an action sequence, such as a jump, a catch or a glance.
- Avoid: Overuse that causes emphasis fatigue.
- Avoid: When the shot should stay slow throughout, or only needs magnification through focal length.

## Film Examples

- *300* (2006): Combat switches between fast and slow, keeping the action readable.
- *Sherlock Holmes* (2009): Anticipated fight details are slowed first, then return to normal speed.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Speed ramp in one unbroken shot: a skateboarder rolls in at real-time speed, time slows dramatically at the peak of the jump as the board spins beneath him, then snaps back to full speed on the landing. Continuous body motion, natural physics and motion blur that matches each speed.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Entire passage in slow motion with no return to real time → design a three-part structure: entry, emphasis, release.
- Poses jump at speed transitions → keep body motion continuous across the whole speed curve.
