# Timelapse Human

Summary: The protagonist stands still in normal time while the surrounding crowd is sped up into a flowing blur.

## Narrative Function

- The subject is a still axis; the flow of the crowd represents the passage of time.
- Creates isolation through temporal contrast.
- Usually shot on a locked-off camera to keep the contrast clear.

## How It Works

- Both the protagonist's position and the camera position must be fixed.
- Sample background pedestrians at longer intervals.
- Alternatively, composite the protagonist's continuous performance over time-lapse crowd footage; unless there is a narrative reason, don't let shadows sweep across the protagonist.

## When to Use

- Use: When the protagonist must stay in place while the environment rushes by around them.
- Avoid: The protagonist moving through a frozen crowd (that is frozen in motion).
- Avoid: Weather-driven sequences (that is timelapse landscape).

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Time-Lapse](time-lapse.md) | The whole frame is sped up, with no isolated subject |
| [Frozen in Motion](frozen-in-motion.md) | Crowd frozen; the protagonist or camera can move |
| [Bullet Time](bullet-time.md) | Orbits a nearly frozen instant |
| [Fast Motion](fast-motion.md) | The protagonist is sped up too; the wrong tool |

## Film Examples

- *Koyaanisqatsi* (1982): Uses time-lapse to present crowds as a visual system.
- *The Matrix* (1999) lobby sequence is often mistaken for this technique; it is actually bullet time.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Human time-lapse on a locked camera: a woman in a red coat stands perfectly still and sharp in the middle of a busy train station, while hundreds of commuters stream around her as translucent, smoke-like blurs. Steady lighting, she never moves, the crowd is pure flowing motion.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Bystanders stopping while the protagonist walks, or the camera orbiting → keep the protagonist in place and the camera locked.
- The crowd rendered as distinct individuals → present the crowd as flowing motion.
