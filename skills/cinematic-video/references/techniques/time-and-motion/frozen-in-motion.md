# Frozen in Motion

Summary: The environment and bystanders are entirely still; only designated characters or the camera can move through the frozen world.

## Narrative Function

- Separates the protagonist's experience from the world's timeline, e.g. superhuman perception or a mental escape.
- Creates a subjective moment no one else can enter.
- Key: hair, weather, shadows and debris must all obey the same freeze rule.

## How It Works

- Define the freeze rule clearly: who / what can move, and what stops.
- Use posed extras with physical rigs for simple setups; VFX / 3D for complex suspension.
- Describe the structure: still environment + suspended particles + clear movement permissions.

## When to Use

- Use: A single character moving through a frozen environment; crowds suspended in a kitchen or street.
- Avoid: Full-frame freezes, orbiting moves, or shots with fast-moving crowds.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |

## Film Examples

- *X-Men: Days of Future Past* (2014): Quicksilver acts within a frozen moment in the Pentagon kitchen.
- *The Worst Person in the World* (2021): Julie runs through motionless Oslo pedestrians.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Frozen-in-motion moment: a young man walks calmly through a cafe where time has stopped, coffee splashing mid-air, waiters and customers locked in mid-gesture, steam hanging motionless. Only he and the tracking camera move; the frozen crowd does not breathe or drift.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with freeze frame or bullet time, or bystanders still moving → write an explicit freeze rule.
- Frozen people breathing, particles drifting → keep extras fully suspended and allow only the designated subject and camera to move.
