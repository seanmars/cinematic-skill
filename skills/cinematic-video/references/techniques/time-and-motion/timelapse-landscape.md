# Timelapse Landscape

Summary: The terrain is the protagonist; clouds, light and shadows sweep over stable ground at a non-human pace.

## Narrative Function

- Geography and terrain are the main subject; changes in weather and light are the action.
- The camera is static or moves slowly, without competing with the sun's path.
- People serve only as scale, not as the subject of the action.

## How It Works

- Set the interval by subject (clouds change faster than shadows).
- Lock the camera or use a motion-control pan.
- For day to night, adjust exposure gradually and keep the sun's path continuous.

## When to Use

- Use: Light moving across ridgelines, plazas or buildings; stories centered on a place's weather or climate.
- Avoid: Crowd sequences, still characters, undercranked performances, walking camera moves.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Time-Lapse](time-lapse.md) | A broader method, not limited to geography |

## Film Examples

- *Koyaanisqatsi* (1982) and *Baraka* (1992): Clouds and shadows moving at a non-human pace established the vocabulary of landscape time-lapse.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Landscape time-lapse from a fixed tripod: clouds roll quickly over a jagged mountain ridge while long shadows sweep steadily across the valley as the sun arcs toward sunset. Solid, unchanging terrain, a tiny hiker only as scale reference, exposure easing smoothly into dusk.
```

## Common Mistakes

- People racing but the sun not moving, or mountains melting and warping → let shadows move and keep the ground solid.
- Camera movement upstaging the scene → keep the camera static or limit it to slow motion-control moves.
