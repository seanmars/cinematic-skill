# Slow Motion

Summary: Shot at a high frame rate and played back at normal speed, stretching a brief instant so normally invisible physical detail emerges.

## Narrative Function

- Stretches a fleeting moment to reveal compressed physical detail.
- Conveys the emotional weight of fear, grief and the like through extended time.
- Sound design is key: ambient sound stretched in sync feels heavy, while keeping sound in real time suggests memory or unreliability.

## How It Works

- Shoot at 120-240 fps and play back at 24 fps (overcranking).
- Exposure time shortens, so more light is needed (usually a 180° shutter).
- Gravity, fabric sway and body movement must be coherent and plausible, avoiding interpolation artifacts.

## When to Use

- Use: Key moments whose meaning would be missed at normal speed, such as an object leaving a hand or an impact.
- Avoid: Using slow motion for an entire sequence; selective use preserves its impact.
- Avoid: When comic timing or a quick scan of the space is needed.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Speed Ramp](speed-ramp.md) | Changes playback speed mid-shot; slow motion is a fixed slowdown |
| [Step Printing](step-printing.md) | Low frame rate with repeated frames, creating a stuttering texture; slow motion is continuous sampling |
| [Bullet Time](bullet-time.md) | Camera orbits a nearly frozen subject; in slow motion the camera is static or moves simply |
| [Freeze Frame](freeze-frame.md) | Stops time completely |
| [Fast Motion](fast-motion.md) / [Time-Lapse](time-lapse.md) | Compresses time rather than stretching it |

## Film Examples

- *Seven Samurai* (1954): The moment the kidnapper falls is shown in slow motion.
- *The Wild Bunch* (1969): Violence is broken into multi-angle slow-motion fragments.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Smooth slow motion as if shot at 200 fps: a glass slips from a hand and shatters, water droplets and shards hanging and drifting through the air, the sleeve fabric reacting with a heavy delay. Locked-off camera, the action keeps moving continuously, no frozen pose and no orbiting camera.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with bullet time, ending up with a frozen subject and an orbiting camera → the subject must keep moving, with the camera static or moving simply.
- Incoherent motion, interpolation artifacts → explicitly specify a 120-240 fps look and ensure plausible physics.
