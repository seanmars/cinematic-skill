# Boomerang

Summary: A short action plays forward, then retraces its path in reverse, forming a toy-like back-and-forth loop.

## Narrative Function

- Turns small actions such as a hair flip, a water splash or a jump into a toy-like back-and-forth loop.
- Both the forward and reverse directions must read clearly.
- Not suited to sad beats, reversed-physics storytelling or seamless one-way loops.

## How It Works

- Shoot a short, clean action with clear contact points.
- Play it forward, then reverse the same frames.
- Trim the join at a near-still apex or matching pose; usually shot on a locked-off camera.

## When to Use

- Use: Light, playful physical actions made into a loop.
- Avoid: One-way impact sequences, or dialogue that cannot be reversed.

## Film Examples

- Originated on platforms such as Instagram / Vine.
- Music videos often use it as a motion hook; feature films rarely use it, mostly only as small gags on phone screens or title cards.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Boomerang loop: a girl on a locked-off camera tosses confetti into the air, the pieces burst outward, then the exact same motion plays backward as the confetti falls back into her hands. Forward then reverse on the same frames, with a seamless join at the matching start pose.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with one-way reversal or a forward loop → explicitly specify that the same footage plays forward, then in reverse.
- Jumpy join → join at a matching pose or near-still apex.
