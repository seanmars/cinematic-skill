# Low Shutter

Summary: Extremely short exposure per frame makes motion crisp and staccato rather than blurred and fluid.

## Narrative Function

- Chops motion into distinct, sharp fragments to convey impact and stress.
- Suits combat and panic scenes, emphasizing gritty discontinuity over silky flow.

## How It Works

- Use a narrower shutter angle (e.g. 45° instead of the standard 180°), shortening exposure per frame.
- At 24 fps this is about 1/192s, compared with the standard 1/48s.
- Less light reaches the sensor, so add light or adjust exposure.

## When to Use

- Use: Landings, rescues, fights that need a fractured rhythm.
- Avoid: Scenes that need light trails or fluid, lyrical motion.

## Film Examples

- *Saving Private Ryan* (1998): Sharp, fractured motion on Omaha Beach.
- *Gladiator* (2000): Discontinuous treatment of the battle footage.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Narrow 45-degree shutter look at normal speed: soldiers sprint through a muddy trench under explosions, every flying clod of dirt and water droplet frozen crisp in each frame. Limbs move in sharp, choppy fragments with almost no motion blur, a harsh staccato cadence.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with motion blur (long exposure, light streaks) → explicitly ask for a narrow shutter angle, about 1/200s exposure and reduced blur.
- Dropping frames to create the staccato → keep normal playback speed; the staccato comes from short exposure.
