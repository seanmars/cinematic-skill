# Fast Motion

Summary: Shot at a low frame rate or retimed in post so continuous action plays back faster than real time.

## Narrative Function

- Compresses time to convey excessive efficiency, comedy or relentless pressure.
- Suits factory labor or crowd scenes that "can't hold up at normal speed".
- Handle sound separately so the pitch doesn't speed up and give it away.

## How It Works

- Shoot below the playback frame rate (undercranking), or retime in post.
- Longer exposure time increases motion blur.
- Verify that hand contact, object placement and footstep rhythm remain physically plausible.

## When to Use

- Use: Mechanized labor, crowded efficiency, nonstop continuous action.
- Avoid: Showing the sky turning from day to night.
- Avoid: Subjects such as weather or construction progress that require interval sampling (time-lapse).

## Film Examples

- *Modern Times* (1936): The sped-up factory pace highlights mechanized pressure.
- *Mad Max: Fury Road* (2015): Selectively alters pacing on action beats.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Undercranked fast motion: a line of factory workers packs boxes at comically accelerated speed, hands and arms smearing with motion blur while every grab and placement stays physically coherent. One continuous performance at a steady speed-up ratio, background activity racing along too.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confused with time-lapse, producing a racing sun or teleporting people → keep one continuous performance at a fixed speed-up ratio and check contact points.
- Using fast motion when hours need to be compressed → use interval shooting instead.
