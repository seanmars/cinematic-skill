# Toxic

Summary: Backlit, sickly yellow-green fumes shroud an industrial scene, making polluted air a visible threat.

## Narrative Function

- Presents polluted air as visible danger, conveying environmental threat and decay.
- The atmosphere is a physical substance, not just a decorative tint.

## How It Works

- Eye level or slightly low angle, 35mm, locked shot.
- Backlight the fumes in the air so the pollutants are visible.
- Sickly green-yellow tones, wet industrial surfaces and visible suspended particles.

## When to Use

- Use: Industrial landscapes, dystopian cities, chemical disaster scenarios.
- Avoid: Pastoral or clean scenes; on a picnic or harmless location it has no effect.

## Film Examples

- *Chernobyl* (2019): Presents the air itself as an encroaching environmental threat.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A worker in a respirator walks through a derelict chemical plant, 35mm slightly low, static camera. Thick sickly yellow-green fumes hang in the air, backlit by harsh floodlights so every drifting particle glows, wet corroded pipes and puddles reflecting the poisoned haze around him.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a green grade, with no fumes or signs of danger → Ensure pollutants are visible, using backlight and sickly tones.
- No people in frame → Place a figure in the polluted space to convey the scale of the threat.
