# Magazine

Summary: A portrait designed for layout, with clean edges and deliberate negative space reserved for a masthead, presenting the person as a product.

## Narrative Function

- Layout takes priority over the subject; the person is packaged as a product.
- Must leave "air" for the masthead; a frame that can't hold a title is just an ordinary portrait.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 85mm, locked shot.
- Beauty lighting, clean background, deliberate negative space at the top or side.
- Wardrobe must read even at thumbnail size.
- No paparazzi-style flash unless deliberately going for a trashy tabloid look.

## When to Use

- Use: Fashion features, identity-as-product imagery, debut shots.
- Avoid: War photography, cluttered frames, candid flash, compositions with crowded edges or nowhere to put a title.
- Don't generate fake headline text.

## Film Examples

- *The Devil Wears Prada* (2006): Magazine covers are presented as calculated geometry and the product of labor.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Fashion portrait of a model in a sharp emerald suit, 85mm eye-level, locked-off, flawless beauty key light against a seamless pale gray backdrop. She sits in the lower two-thirds of the frame, leaving a clean band of empty space across the top for a masthead, bold silhouette readable even as a thumbnail, no text rendered.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A pretty portrait with no negative space, or invented headlines → Reserve masthead space, keep the light clean, remove all text.
