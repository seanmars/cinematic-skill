# Casual Monster Slayer

Summary: A person in ordinary casual clothes defeats a monster deadpan, as if running a daily errand.

## Narrative Function

- The comedy comes from a clash of register: an impossible feat handled as routine.
- Replaces heroic grandeur with indifference; the character just wants to finish and go home.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 35mm; handheld or locked shot.
- Wardrobe must be mundane with realistic detail.
- The monster and the protagonist share the same light: natural street light, kitchen practicals, overcast sky.
- No stylized rim light, no dramatic treatment.

## When to Use

- Use: Horror comedy, deadpan heroes, characters who just want to clock out and leave.
- Avoid: Champion-poster feel of a dark epic, or victory-pose shots.

## Film Examples

- *Shaun of the Dead* (2004): Dispatching zombies with a cricket bat on the street in broad daylight, like doing chores.
- *Big Trouble in Little China* (1986): A truck driver casually deals with unbelievable monsters.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Eye-level 35mm handheld shot on an overcast suburban sidewalk: a man in a gray hoodie and sweatpants, holding a grocery bag in one hand, casually pins down a huge tentacled creature with a mop in the other. Flat daylight hits both him and the monster equally, his expression bored, no rim light, no heroic angle.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A warrior vibe still shows through the casual clothes, with stylish grime and low-angle rim light → Keep the wardrobe boring and the monster detailed.
- Camera language too heroic → Use commuter-flat camera moves and composition.
