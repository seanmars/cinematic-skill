# Canvas

Summary: Turns a portrait into a handmade oil painting with brushstrokes, canvas weave and impasto highlights, while keeping the face recognizable.

## Narrative Function

- Makes the subject look painted in a studio, evoking an artistic sense of memory.
- Suits formal opening/closing titles or handmade portrait sequences.

## How It Works

- 50-85mm at eye level, locked shot.
- North window light or a large soft source, simulating a painter's studio lighting.
- Visible directional brushstrokes, canvas texture, thick paint on the highlights, with facial detail still preserved underneath.

## When to Use

- Use: Handmade portraits, title sequences, moments with a sense of remembrance.
- Avoid: Smooth painting filters with no texture, unfinished sketches, wet-on-wet styles, a luxury-magazine feel.

## Film Examples

- *Loving Vincent* (2017): The entire film uses oil paintings as its imagery while keeping faces recognizable.
- *The Mill and the Cross* (2011): Turns a painting's space into a moving crowd scene.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Head-and-shoulders portrait of an elderly fisherman at eye level, 85mm, locked frame, soft north-window light from the side. Rendered as a real oil painting: visible directional bristle strokes, canvas weave showing through, thick impasto on the highlights, eyes crisp and detailed beneath the paint.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Smooth, plastic-looking filter with no visible brushstrokes → Force in bristle marks and canvas texture.
- Face smeared by brushstrokes → Impasto on the highlights, but keep the eyes and features clear.
