# Hand Paint

Summary: Wet brushstrokes are visibly being applied over a photographic base layer, as if the portrait is being painted in real time.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys the act of creation, and "memory as labor".
- Shows a portrait being made rather than a finished work, favoring process over result.

## How It Works

- Medium close-up, about 50mm, locked shot.
- The photographic image is the base layer, with directional wet brushstrokes layered on top.
- The painter's hand may enter frame, or its presence is implied by fresh brush marks.

## When to Use

- Use: Portraits in progress, explorations of memory, artistic labor.
- Avoid: Using it as a dry painting filter, or when a finished painting effect is wanted.

## Film Examples

- *Loving Vincent* (2017): The whole film treats "paint" as a verb; sequences and the opening titles keep the momentum of brushstrokes.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Medium close-up of a young man's face at 50mm, locked camera, his photographic features clear underneath while fresh wet brushstrokes sweep across his cheek and hair in real time. Glossy streaks of ochre and blue follow the brush direction, a painter's hand and brush tip entering from frame right mid-stroke.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Dry painterly grade with no sense of wetness or brush direction → Ensure strokes are wet, glossy, fresh and directional.
- Brushstrokes cover the face → Keep the face clear and readable.
