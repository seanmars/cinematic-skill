# Origami

Summary: A figure or an entire city becomes folded-paper sculpture, with crisp creases, sharp edges and flat color planes.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys myth, title-sequence feel or moments of delicate menace through geometric, origami-ized figures or environments.
- The creases themselves are compositional lines.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 35-50mm, locked shot.
- Hard studio light or sunlight.
- Sharp edges, visible fold lines, flat color blocks; no text on the paper.
- It is "the world itself folding", not a handheld origami prop.

## When to Use

- Use: Mythic subjects, title sequences, delicate origami menace imagery.
- Avoid: Muddy war scenes, paper-crane props on a craft table, or torn fibers without clear facets.

## Film Examples

- *Kubo and the Two Strings* (2016): In stop-motion, paper is a living medium, with creases used as compositional elements.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A samurai and the city behind him are made entirely of folded paper, 40mm eye-level, locked camera, hard late sun. Every roof, sleeve and blade is built from crisp geometric creases and flat facets of indigo, white and vermilion, sharp shadows along each fold, no writing on the paper, no torn edges.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Becomes a handheld origami prop, or drifts into a torn-paper aesthetic → Make the whole world fold by rule, with clear creases and flat color planes.
