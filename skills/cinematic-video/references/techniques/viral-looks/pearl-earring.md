# Pearl Earring

Summary: An image like a Dutch Golden Age portrait, with a single cool window light, a dark earthen wall and one tiny highlight on jewelry.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a quiet, contemplative character study with a painterly quality.
- The subject seems observed and gazed upon rather than acting; the more movement, the weaker the effect.

## How It Works

- A single north window light from frame left, with no studio fill at all.
- 50-85mm, eye level, locked shot.
- A dark earth-tone wall as the background; the head turns toward the camera.
- A single specular highlight on the jewelry becomes the visual focal point, with cool daylight dominant overall.

## When to Use

- Use: Intimate portraits that need stillness and a painterly feel.
- Avoid: Action sequences, nightclub scenes, studio shoots with heavy beauty dishes.
- Don't directly copy the original pose and costume of the famous painting.

## Film Examples

- *Girl with a Pearl Earring* (2003): Applies the visual grammar of window light and jewelry to the actor, a reinterpretation rather than a copy.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A young woman glances back over her shoulder in a dim room, 85mm at eye level, locked-off. One cool north window from camera-left is the only light, dark umber wall behind her, a single tiny specular glint on a silver earring, quiet and painterly, modern clothing hinting at period simplicity.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using a studio beauty dish with a headscarf costume, losing the natural interior feel → Let the room itself be the "lamp" and keep the cool window light.
- Jewelry highlight too dominant → Treat it only as a subtle catchlight-like glint.
- Re-enacting the famous painting's pose → Use a new subject and suggestive wardrobe elements.
