# Monet Muse

Summary: Depicts gardens, water and light through optical mixing of broken color dabs and strokes, like an impressionist painting rather than a soft-focus blur.

## Narrative Function

- Evokes pastoral gardens, memory and the mood of a picnic in a painting.
- Light itself is the star, as important as the figures.

## How It Works

- Garden or waterside, eye level, 35-85mm, locked shot.
- High-angle sunlight or golden hour.
- Use broken color dabs and short strokes that mix optically, rather than soft focus; the underlying form and drawing structure remain.

## When to Use

- Use: Garden idylls, memory sequences, waterscapes and impressionist scenes, when light quality matters as much as the subject.
- Avoid: Night scenes and neon (unless creating deliberate conflict); also don't use it as just a blur filter.

## Film Examples

- *Mr. Turner* (2014): Treats the light of water and sky as the object of a painter's labor.
- *The Impressionists* (2006): A period drama emphasizing broken color as a way of seeing.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A woman in a white dress holds a parasol beside a lily pond in bright midday sun, 50mm eye-level locked frame. Rendered in impressionist broken color: small dabs of lilac, green and yellow that mix optically, shimmering reflections on the water, forms still clearly drawn underneath, no blur filter, no ink outlines.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A painterly blur applied without real color dabs or form → Depict garden and water with broken color dabs, keeping optical mixing visible and form intact.
- Adding hard ink lines → Remove all hard-edged outlines.
