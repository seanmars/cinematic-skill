# 2000s Paparazzi

Summary: Direct on-camera flash from a consumer digital camera, hard shadows on the wall and a greenish white balance, like an unflattering moment caught by a hidden lens.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys scandal, youth under intense scrutiny, hounded exits and stolen moments of fame.
- It is the visual language of a paparazzi chase, not sanctioned glamour.

## How It Works

- Framed from chest to eye height, 28-50mm equivalent.
- Direct on-camera flash leaves hard shadows on the wall.
- Includes the greenish white-balance flaws of a consumer camera; set on a sidewalk or at a nightclub.
- Deliberately avoids beauty lighting; motion blur is acceptable.

## When to Use

- Use: Scandal scenes, celebrity exits, stories of young people under pressure.
- Avoid: Royal portraits, beauty magazine covers, planned fashion shoots or any posed setup.

## Film Examples

- *The Bling Ring* (2013): The small flash becomes a visual weapon; images look accidentally captured.
- *Paparazzi* (2004): Paparazzi flash and the sense of pursuit dominate the frame.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A young pop star rushes out of a nightclub onto a wet sidewalk at 2 a.m., shot at chest height with a 35mm compact camera and harsh direct on-camera flash. Hard black shadow on the brick wall behind her, slight greenish white balance, a bit of motion blur, her face caught mid-blink and unprepared.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Flash too soft, looks like fashion photography → Ensure hard shadows on the wall and camera flaws.
- Unconsciously drifting into Superstar → The expression must be completely unprepared; no posing.
