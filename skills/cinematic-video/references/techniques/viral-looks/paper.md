# Paper

Summary: The entire world is made of paper pulp, with torn edges, visible fibers and cut-paper figures.

## Narrative Function

- Builds a world where everything is made of paper.
- Emphasizes paper as both medium and the world itself.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 35-50mm, locked shot.
- Use raking light to bring out paper texture and fibers.
- Torn edges, cut-paper subjects, with a paper background.

## When to Use

- Use: Title sequences, stop-motion sequences, worlds built entirely of paper.
- Avoid: Origami-style creased forms, or portraits where paper is only a prop rather than the environmental foundation.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Origami](origami.md) | Deliberate creases and geometric folding, not organic tearing |
| [Collage](../effects/collage.md) | Pasted, glued fragments; Paper uses torn pulp as a single environment |
| [Agamemnon](agamemnon.md) | Ancient tragic aesthetic, not material texture |

## Film Examples

- *The Tale of the Princess Kaguya* (2013): Paper and pencil become a living medium.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A tiny paper fox trots through a forest built entirely from torn paper layers, 40mm eye-level, locked camera. Low raking light skims across the sheets, revealing rough fibers and ragged torn edges, cut-out tree silhouettes stacked in depth against a pulpy cream sky, no text, no neat folds.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Paper becomes a handheld prop, or neat origami → Emphasize tears and fiber texture, letting paper form the entire environment.
