# Fallen Angel

Summary: A figure bathed in a sacred shaft of light, with broken "wings" only suggested through fabric, shadow or posture, showing the state after the fall.

## Narrative Function

- Depicts the aftermath of a spiritual or physical fall; the focus is loss, not redemption.
- The face is the focal point, carrying both judgment and grace.
- Wings need not actually appear; fabric, shadow and placement are enough to suggest them.

## How It Works

- Eye level or slightly low angle, 35-50mm.
- Strong window light or a light shaft from high frame left, with overall low-key contrast.
- Matte skin texture with no beauty highlights; wardrobe looks damaged but uses no feathers.

## When to Use

- Use: Themes of grief, blasphemy or moral judgment, or portraits of spiritually struggling characters.
- Avoid: Halloween costumes, superhero styling, love stories seeking a redemptive ending, carnival-style treatment.

## Film Examples

- *Wings of Desire* (1987): Angels wear realistic overcoats and exist as observers.
- *The Prophecy* (1995): Divine beings portrayed as weary and violent.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A kneeling figure in an empty stone nave, 35mm lens at slightly low eye level, locked camera. A single hard shaft of window light falls from high camera-left across half the face, low-key contrast, matte skin, torn gray fabric draped behind the shoulders like ruined wings, no feathers.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using stock white feathered wings, a clean key light, a carnival feel → Remove literal feathers; use torn fabric and a light shaft instead.
- Face fully lit → Keep the facial expression half in light, half in shadow.
