# Akrill

Summary: A thick layer of highly saturated, plastic-sheen acrylic paint clings to people and objects, like a poster come to life.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a "poster come to life" aesthetic, with paint sitting on the surface like thick plastic.
- Connects to toys, pop art and graphic storytelling rather than traditional fine-art painting.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 35-50mm, locked shot.
- Hard or semi-hard studio key light so raised paint catches wet highlights.
- Impasto, high-chroma, graphic color blocks, while keeping three-dimensional modeling of the face.

## When to Use

- Use: Pop aesthetics, toy subjects, poster-style visuals.
- Avoid: Watercolor effects, classical oil painting, or thin washes where paint soaks in.

## Film Examples

- *Dick Tracy* (1990): Accent-color makeup applied to faces like poster paint.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A skateboarder poses against a wall at eye level, 40mm, locked camera, hard studio key. Everything is covered in thick glossy acrylic paint: raised plastic ridges, wet specular highlights on each stroke, hot pink, electric blue and lemon yellow blocks with crisp poster edges, his face still fully modeled in three dimensions.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Looks like thin watercolor, or gets confused with Canvas oil paint → Explicitly ask for thick acrylic impasto, poster edges and plastic-like wet highlights.
