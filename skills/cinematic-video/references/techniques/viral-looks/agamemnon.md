# Agamemnon

Summary: Glaring sun, bronze bounce light and a bruised dusk sky make the character look already doomed.

## Narrative Function

- Uses light and sky, not action or dialogue, to convey the tragedy of a "sealed fate".
- The camera barely moves, letting the oppressive environment and light drive the mood.
- Works in close shots or extreme wide shots; the key is hard light that doesn't flatter the face.

## How It Works

- Locked shot or a very slow push-in; eye level or slightly low angle, 35-50mm focal length.
- Hard sunlight enters from frame left or behind; fill comes from warm copper or dusty bounce, never white fill.
- Keep deep, opaque shadows; avoid neon or beauty lighting.

## When to Use

- Use: Mythic epics, war portraits, or scenes meant to make a modern character look "cursed".
- Avoid: Sequences that need glamour, romance or clean beauty lighting, or it becomes a perfume ad with added dust.

## Film Examples

- *Troy* (2004): An epic lighting vocabulary of harsh sun and metal armor.
- *The Northman* (2022): Mud, iron and a face already resigned to fate.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A weary king stands on a windswept rampart at eye level, 40mm lens, locked-off camera. Harsh sun rakes in from behind camera-left, bronze-toned bounce instead of white fill, deep opaque shadows, and a bruised violet dusk sky pressing down on him like a verdict.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turning it into a generic premium teal-orange grade or a beauty hard-light portrait → Emphasize bronze tones, a bruised dusk and the figure's scale within the environment.
- Adding fill that lights up the face → Remove the fill so shadows stay opaque.
