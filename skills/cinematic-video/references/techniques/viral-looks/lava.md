# Lava

Summary: Glowing orange light seeps through cracks in a black rock crust, lighting the figure from below while the air warps with heat.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys apocalypse, intense emotion and molten environments.
- Suggests the world is ending, or structures are still burning; not suited to mundane scenes unless played for comedy.

## How It Works

- Eye level or slightly high, 24-35mm, locked shot.
- Underlighting from below: glowing orange fissures across a black surface.
- Add heat-haze distortion to the air; shooting at night strengthens the effect.

## When to Use

- Use: End-of-the-world moments, scenes full of rage, settings still melting.
- Avoid: Just applying an orange grade to an ordinary street or a domestic argument, unless ironic.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Furnace-sourced orange light strikes bodies from below.
- *The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King* (2003): Lava flows are both setting and key light source.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A small cloaked figure stands on a ridge of cracked black basalt at night, 28mm slightly high, locked-off. Glowing orange fissures split the crust all around, the molten light striking her from below as the only key, heat haze warping the air above the rock, no signage, a sense of the world ending.
```

## Common Mistakes

- An ordinary street with an orange grade, or underlighting with no rock crust or heat haze → Add black rock, glowing fissures and atmospheric heat distortion.
