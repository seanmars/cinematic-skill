# Acid

Summary: Like a sun-baked, chemically damaged photo, with scorched color, melting edges and drifting silver tones.

## Narrative Function

- Represents a chemically damaged photo or a failed memory.
- Evokes "a night gone wrong" or a decayed poster, not a joyful party.

## How It Works

- Locked shot, 35-50mm, eye level, face clearly readable.
- Describe print damage: scorched chroma, melting edges, drifting silver tones.
- No motion trails (those belong to LSD).

## When to Use

- Use: Damaged memories, poster degradation, moods with a chemically sickly feel.
- Avoid: Supermarket-style realism, rainbow party aesthetics; don't mix with trail effects.

## Film Examples

- *Enter the Void* (2009): Layered, overlapping dye damage.
- *Spring Breakers* (2012): Over-scorched color intensity.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A static 40mm eye-level portrait of a man at a motel doorway at dusk, face clearly readable, but the image looks like a photo print left baking on a dashboard for a summer: scorched magenta and cyan chroma, edges blistering and melting inward, silver tones drifting, no motion trails.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Applying a rainbow LUT or misusing LSD light trails → Show genuine melting and burn damage.
- Damage overwhelms the face → Concentrate damage on color and edges; keep the face readable.
