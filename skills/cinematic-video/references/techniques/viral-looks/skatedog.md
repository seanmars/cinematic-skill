# Skatedog

Summary: A ground-level ultra-wide shot of an animal (or an unexpected rider) skillfully skateboarding, with asphalt texture filling the foreground.

## Narrative Function

- Street comedy: a character who shouldn't be able to do it does it with skill, and the joke comes from that contrast.
- Emphasizes speed and action; shooting from standing height or with heroic framing kills it.

## How It Works

- Camera 15-40cm off the ground, 14-24mm wide lens, parallel to the road surface.
- Ground-level axis, locked or a brief low tracking shot.
- Subject and skateboard/gear in the same frame, with strongly directional hard sunlight or sodium light.

## When to Use

- Use: Street comedy, youth culture, animals doing human activities.
- Avoid: Standing eye-height perspective, heroic rim light, formal dramatic situations or beauty shots.

## Film Examples

- *Kids* (1995): Like skate videos, uses ground-level shots to capture real, undirected physical movement.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Camera skimming 20cm above sun-baked asphalt with a 16mm lens, low tracking alongside a bulldog confidently cruising on a skateboard down a city street. Hard late-afternoon sun, gritty pavement texture filling the foreground, barely any sky, the dog's ears flapping, played straight like a real skate clip.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Shooting a cute animal standing on a board from normal height → Drop the camera to road-surface height.
- Too much sky, added heroic rim light → Stay wide, emphasize asphalt texture, and treat the subject comedically.
