# Flash Comic

Summary: A comic treatment of ink lines and halftone dots, plus a white burst of on-camera flash, like a panel stolen from a printed publication.

## Narrative Function

- Presents impact moments such as a punch, a reveal or a punchline as a published panel.
- Combines comic ink aesthetics with documentary-style flash photography.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 35-50mm, locked shot at the peak of the action.
- Inked edges and halftone dots, plus a white burst from hard on-camera flash.
- Use only one accent color; no speech bubbles; the base layer remains photographic.

## When to Use

- Use: Dynamic reveals, comedic punches and other moments imagined as a publication.
- Avoid: Quiet contemplative portraits or intimate character studies.
- Ink lines and flash are both required.

## Film Examples

- *Dick Tracy* (1990): Print-like faces combined with flash elements.
- *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* (2018): Its print vocabulary works as a grading reference, not something to copy frame by frame.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A boxer lands a knockout punch at the peak of the swing, 40mm eye-level, locked frame. A harsh on-camera flash bursts white across the scene while bold ink outlines and halftone dots render the shadows, a single yellow spot color on his gloves, photographic faces underneath, no speech balloons.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only comic ink lines with no flash, or only paparazzi flash with no illustration treatment → Show both inked edges and a white flash burst at the peak of the action.
