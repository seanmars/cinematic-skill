# Ultraviolet

Summary: A black light is the only light source; fluorescent whites and violets blaze while the surroundings sink into pitch black.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a nightclub or forensic-scene atmosphere, with black light dominating the entire frame.
- Fluorescent materials glow while the surroundings fall into darkness, telling an environmental story at almost radioactive intensity.

## How It Works

- Use a real black light (in frame or implied) as the only key light.
- Whites glare, violets are intense, and only certain colors are visible.
- Fluorescent elements such as teeth and cotton clothing glow strongly, while the background recedes into shadow.

## When to Use

- Use: Nightclubs, forensic investigation sequences, poster-style stylized imagery, surreal nightclub dreams.
- Avoid: Just applying a violet tint to daytime exteriors; don't use a purple LUT in place of real black-light photography.

## Film Examples

- *Spring Breakers* (2012): Fluorescent black-light photography in party scenes.
- Nightclub and forensic black-light sequences are the main applications of this technique.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A crowded underground club lit only by a black-light tube above the DJ booth, 35mm static shot. White T-shirts, teeth and shoelaces blaze with fluorescent glow, violet haze hangs in the air, everything non-fluorescent sinks into near-total black, no neon signs or lettering.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a violet grade, with no real black-light source → Ensure an actual black light is present and fluorescent whites glare brightly.
- Adding neon sign lettering → Remove signage and let the surroundings fall dark, almost to a void.
