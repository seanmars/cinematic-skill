# Orbital Presence

Summary: A tiny figure floats like a satellite in pitch-black vacuum, with a sharp planetary arc and glaring sunlight behind.

## Narrative Function

- Creates emotional impact through scale and isolation.
- Depicts awe, exile or a god's-eye view, with the figure like a satellite in space rather than standing on Earth.

## How It Works

- Wide 24-35mm, locked shot.
- Unfiltered glaring sunlight, pure black vacuum, a clearly visible planetary limb arc.
- The figure is deliberately tiny, with no atmospheric haze around it.

## When to Use

- Use: Isolation in space, god-like perspectives, prayer-like moments of small contemplation.
- Avoid: A studio globe backdrop, or scenes that need atmosphere enveloping the figure.

## Film Examples

- *Gravity* (2013): Human bodies against the planetary arc, bathed in hard sunlight.
- *Ad Astra* (2019): Figures floating in black space above the world.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A tiny astronaut drifts alone in deep black space, 28mm wide, locked-off, the curved blue limb of a planet razor-sharp across the lower frame. Brutal unfiltered sunlight from one side, pure vacuum with no haze around the figure, no HUD, the human barely a speck against the scale of the world.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Looks like an in-studio Earth backdrop, or adds a zoom down to street level → Must be vacuum, hard light and a genuinely tiny figure.
- Atmospheric glow or HUD text around the figure → Remove all glow and interface elements; keep a crisp planetary limb.
