# Race Track

Summary: Speed contained within a closed circuit, with painted curbs, catch fencing and grandstands forming the subject of the frame.

## Narrative Function

- Presents speed as a closed sporting event rather than an open-road journey.
- The track itself is the visual star, emphasizing sport, pursuit, and characters who find meaning racing within boundaries.

## How It Works

- Shoot the track geometry from the infield with a wide lens (about 24mm), or compress space on a straight with a telephoto (about 135mm).
- Essential elements: painted curbs, catch fencing, grandstands.
- Add heat-haze distortion or motion blur to the cars; keep sponsor logos illegible.

## When to Use

- Use: Lap sequences, sporting pursuits, characters trapped in a track environment.
- Avoid: Ordinary highways or open roads with no track infrastructure.

## Film Examples

- *Grand Prix* (1966): The track is treated as architectural space.
- *Ford v Ferrari* (2019): People and machines placed within track infrastructure.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Long 135mm telephoto shot down the main straight of an oval circuit: three race cars compressed together, heat shimmer rising off the asphalt, red-and-white painted curbs, tall catch fencing and a packed grandstand blurring behind them, sponsor decals unreadable, motion blur on the wheels.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using an ordinary highway, or readable sponsor signage → Add curbs, catch fencing and grandstands, keeping a closed-loop composition.
