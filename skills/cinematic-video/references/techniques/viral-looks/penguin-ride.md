# Penguin Ride

Summary: Someone rides a penguin with a straight face across an ice field or an incongruous city street, the absurdity shot like a documentary.

## Narrative Function

- Creates deadpan comedy, with the penguin as the engine of the joke.
- Shoots an obviously wrong "vehicle" as fact; the background can be ice and snow or an out-of-place city.

## How It Works

- Wide 24-35mm, at rider height or slightly lower.
- The penguin, the rider and atmospheric elements such as airborne slush or ice crystals must all be in frame.
- Light can range from overcast white to glaring polar sun.
- There must be a clear sense of movement, with the rider fully committed.

## When to Use

- Use: Comic sequences, dream logic, absurd postcard style.
- Avoid: Wildlife documentaries that respect animals; static compositions where the penguin just stands beside a person.

## Film Examples

- No representative penguin-riding film; borrow the shot grammar of polar documentaries, aiming for a postcard feel that "looks like it really happened".

## Prompt Examples

```text
Wide 24mm shot at low rider height across a windswept ice field under hard polar sun: a man in a puffy orange parka sits astride a giant emperor penguin waddling at full speed, slush and ice crystals kicking up around them. His face is dead serious, penguin anatomy clearly readable, framed like real expedition footage.
```

## Common Mistakes

- The penguin just stands beside the person, not ridden → Must actually be ridden, with the penguin moving.
- The rider winks at the camera, self-consciously funny → Keep the expression serious and use a documentary frame.
- Static composition → Add splashing slush and forward motion.
