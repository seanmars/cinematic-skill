# Lost in a Book

Summary: An open book becomes a doorway; light and weather pour from its pages and the reader falls into an illustrated story world.

## Narrative Function

- Expresses childhood wonder, emotional processing or immersion in a story.
- The book is a literal threshold; the binding is the "hinge" between reality and story.

## How It Works

- The book occupies the foreground, 24-50mm.
- Light or weather radiates from the pages; locked shot or a very small push toward the gutter.
- Text stays illegible, serving only as texture.
- The key light comes from within the pages, combined with the room's ambient light.

## When to Use

- Use: A character at the threshold of a story, facing grief through reading, moments of being swallowed by a story.
- Avoid: Static reading on a sofa or a closed book; the book must be an active portal.

## Film Examples

- *The NeverEnding Story* (1984): The book is a threshold that can be crossed.
- *Inkheart* (2008): Reading itself is a portal with consequences.

## Prompt Examples

```text
An open antique book fills the foreground on a bedroom floor at night, 28mm, slow creep toward the spine. Warm golden light and drifting snow pour out of the pages, the unreadable text dissolving into a glowing winter forest beyond, a small child leaning over the gutter, lit from below by the book.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only passive reading, with no portal activated → Place the book in the foreground with its contents spilling out of the gutter.
- Multiple worlds visible, scattering focus → Show only one destination world so the audience crosses the threshold too.
