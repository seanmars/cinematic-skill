# Blue Depth

Summary: Layer upon layer of cyan-blue planes deepening into the distance, making space extend endlessly like underwater or a foggy night city.

## Narrative Function

- Creates loneliness and wonder through successively receding cyan planes.
- Suits underwater, night-city and atmospheric shots; not suited to comedy.
- The key is a "gradient along depth", not a flat wash of cyan.

## How It Works

- A telephoto lens (85-135mm) compresses space, or an underwater wide lens with atmospheric haze.
- Locked shot; describe layered glass, fog or water.
- Place one subject in the foreground to establish scale against the planes behind.

## When to Use

- Use: Swimming sequences, night skylines, isolated figures in thick air.
- Avoid: Applying an overall teal-orange grade to daytime scenes, or identical hues in foreground and background.

## Film Examples

- *The Abyss* (1989): Water as layered blue distance.
- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Cyan distant planes against warmer foreground objects.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A lone diver hangs in open water in the foreground, wide lens, locked camera. Behind her, layer after layer of suspended haze recedes into progressively deeper cyan and navy, the nearest plane pale and slightly warm, the farthest almost black-blue, giving immense scale and silence.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Flat cyan, foreground and background equally blue → Build clear layers, with cyan deepening as distance increases.
- No scale reference → Place a recognizable subject in the foreground.
