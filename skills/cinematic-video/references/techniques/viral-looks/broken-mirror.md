# Broken Mirror

Summary: Shot through a real shattered mirror, each shard reflects the face and room from a slightly different angle, so identity contradicts itself.

## Narrative Function

- Visualizes psychological fracture, narcissism, deception or emotional breakdown.
- Presents identity as fragmented rather than whole; suits a single character in inner conflict, not groups.

## How It Works

- Locked shot, 35-50mm, shooting the broken mirror as a practical prop.
- Each shard captures a different part of the face and room, at a slightly different angle or moment.
- The shards must genuinely "disagree", not be one image sliced up.

## When to Use

- Use: Psychological breakdown, narcissism, scenes of lying.
- Avoid: Overlaying a crack texture on a portrait, or using it for group shots.
- Prerequisite: Each shard actually shows a different face angle and environmental detail.

## Film Examples

- *Black Swan* (2010): The bathroom mirror scene fractures the protagonist's identity.
- Film noir often uses mirrors as a tool for moral commentary.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A man stares into a shattered bathroom mirror under a single vanity bulb, 40mm locked-off, shot through the glass. Each jagged silver shard shows his face from a slightly different angle and moment: one eye wide, another slice smirking, another showing the tiled wall behind him, the pieces refusing to agree, no kaleidoscope effect.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Adding a crack layer over a single-angle image, with identical content in every shard → Use real glass, with each shard showing a different angle and part of the room.
- Making it a kaleidoscope filter → Shards are irregular and each shows different content.
