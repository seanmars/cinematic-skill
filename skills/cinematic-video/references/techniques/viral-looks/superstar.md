# Superstar

Summary: Polished beauty lighting, bright catchlights and an implied wall of camera flashes make the subject look watched by an audience.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys public recognition and a glamorous entrance; light and composition imply the audience is already watching.
- Emphasizes exposure that is "willingly seen", not candid capture.

## How It Works

- Eye level, 85mm or 50mm, locked shot.
- A large beauty light source, prominent catchlights and slight soft focus, optionally with a rear-side kicker.
- Use specular points to imply a wall of flashes or a crowd behind; no readable text.
- Wardrobe shows wealth and refinement.

## When to Use

- Use: Red-carpet entrances, stage moments, the reveal of a character the audience knows well.
- Avoid: People being chased on a sidewalk, secret or basement scenes.

## Film Examples

- *A Star Is Born* (2018): The stage and public gaze become environmental forces shaping the shots.
- *Dreamgirls* (2006): Stage exposure dominates how the characters are presented.

## Prompt Examples

```text
An actress in a sequined gown pauses on a red carpet, 85mm at eye level, locked camera. Large soft beauty key with bright catchlights in her eyes, a gentle kicker on her hair, and a blurred wall of camera flashes sparkling behind her, polished and adored, no readable signage.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only soft beauty light, with no star mythology → Add catchlights and an implied wall of flashes.
- Drifting into a paparazzi candid feel → Use "sanctioned" light and keep a steady eyeline.
