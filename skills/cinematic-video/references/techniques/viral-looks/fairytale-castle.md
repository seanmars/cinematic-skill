# Fairytale Castle

Summary: A stone castle at dusk becomes the star of the frame, with a tiny figure within it, the whole image like a storybook illustration.

## Narrative Function

- The architecture itself is the subject, conveying destiny and wonder rather than serving as mere background.
- Makes a single figure look small within a grand setting, creating a storybook mood.

## How It Works

- Wide locked shot, eye level or slightly low angle, 24-35mm.
- The figure occupies no more than a third of the frame.
- The key is dusk light: indigo sky + gold rim light on the edges of the stone walls.
- The stone should look both real and hand-painted, with atmospheric haze; avoid a game-engine plastic look.

## When to Use

- Use: Arriving in a kingdom, a character's exile, romantic sequences that need architectural symbolism.
- Avoid: Tourist documentaries of real European castles, figures that need heroic scale over the architecture, sequences with no weather or atmosphere.

## Film Examples

- *The Fall* (2006): Builds kingdoms as illustrated spaces around the actors' bodies.
- *Pan's Labyrinth* (2006): Stone and dusk follow storybook visual logic.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Wide locked shot at 28mm, slightly low: a towering storybook castle with impossible spires rises above a misty causeway at dusk, indigo sky, warm gold rim light catching weathered hand-drawn-looking stone. A tiny cloaked traveler walks the path in the lower third, film-like atmospheric haze, no glossy CG sheen.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Generic fantasy composite with no time of day or weather → Specify the time of day, path details and illustrated-texture stone.
- Touristy wide shot with the protagonist too large → Shrink the figure and emphasize the architectural mass.
- Plastic, game-engine-like battlements → Switch to weathered, hand-painted-looking stone and natural light gradients.
