# Action Figure

Summary: Turns the subject into toy merchandise, with plastic joints, painted-on costume and blister-pack product lighting.

## Narrative Function

- Reduces a hero to a commodity, for satire or myth-making.
- Used deliberately, it is commentary; applied without irony to a sincere story, it fails.

## How It Works

- Slightly high locked shot, 50mm.
- Hard product lighting that mimics blister packaging.
- Visible joints and rivets, painted-on costume, optional display base; no readable text.

## When to Use

- Use: Satire of "hero as merchandise", children's mythology.
- Avoid: Sincere dramatic material, unless "being reduced to a toy" is itself the theme.

## Film Examples

- *Toy Story* (1995): Realistic plastic materials and lighting, no packaging needed.
- *The Lego Movie* (2014): Toy material texture dominates the entire world.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A firefighter reimagined as a 6-inch plastic action figure standing on a small molded base, 50mm from slightly above, locked shot. Hard glossy product lighting, visible ball joints at shoulders, elbows and knees, painted-on uniform details with slight paint edges, toy-scale proportions, no packaging text.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using a costumed real person instead of a toy, with visible fabric texture → Emphasize joints, rivets and toy-scale plastic sheen.
