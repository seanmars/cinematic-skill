# LSD

Summary: Light sources leave afterimage trails, walls seem to breathe, colors are oversaturated, depicting altered perception.

## Narrative Function

- Depicts distorted perception in intense moments such as nightlife, music or emotional breakdown.
- The architecture itself becomes unstable, and light sources leave trails.

## How It Works

- Locked or slowly drifting camera, eye level, 35mm, keeping spatial geography consistent.
- Light trails, a slight double-exposure feel, oversaturated light sources, walls slowly shifting.
- No drug paraphernalia or similar props.

## When to Use

- Use: Unsettling night sequences, musical moments, psychological disintegration.
- Avoid: Formal scenes that need spatial stability, such as courtrooms or procedurals.

## Film Examples

- *Enter the Void* (2009): Streets become an oversaturated experience of light trails.
- *The Trip* (1967): Interior spaces become an unstable optical environment.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Inside a single cramped apartment at night, 35mm at eye level with a slow drift, a man sits on the floor, face clearly readable. Every lamp and neon sign leaves long glowing trails as he moves, the walls gently swell and exhale, colors pushed to oversaturation with faint double-exposure ghosting.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Only a rainbow grade, or mixing in kaleidoscope effects or Acid's melting damage → Focus on light trails and breathing architecture.
- Straight walls, lights with no trails → The effect hasn't landed; explicitly ask for afterimages and deformation.
- Jumping between locations → Keep to a single room with the subject clearly readable.
