# Two Color

Summary: The whole frame carries all information in just two colors (duotone, or monochrome plus one accent color), with every other color eliminated.

## Narrative Function

- Assigns two hues to shadows/highlights or subject/background, removing all other colors.
- Suits posters, fables and political imagery; natural skin tones break the effect unless skin is one of the two colors.

## How It Works

- Locked shot, 35-50mm.
- Wardrobe and production design use only the two chosen colors.
- Other hues are converted to these two colors with masks or grading, or turned to black and white plus one accent color.

## When to Use

- Use: Poster-style compositions, fables, recurring color signals, isolating a single colored object.
- Avoid: Using it as a gentle overall grade, or letting stray colors leak through during action.

## Film Examples

- *Schindler's List* (1993): An isolated red coat in a black-and-white frame.
- *Pleasantville* (1998): Color gradually appears as characters change.
- *Sin City* (2005): Specific colors accent high-contrast black and white.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Duotone image in only deep navy and bright orange: a cyclist crosses an empty bridge at dawn, 45mm static camera. Shadows and sky rendered in navy, highlights and her jacket in orange, no other hue anywhere in the frame, colors holding perfectly stable as she moves past the railings.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Other colors leak through randomly → Strictly maintain two-color discipline, keeping masks stable through motion and occlusion.
- Wardrobe and production design don't cooperate → Plan in advance for wardrobe and sets to use only the two colors.
