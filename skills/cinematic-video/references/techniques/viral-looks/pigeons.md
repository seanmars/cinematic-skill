# Pigeons

Summary: A figure stands firm and still in a city square as a huge flock of pigeons bursts into flight around them, wings filling the frame like confetti.

## Narrative Function

- The still subject acts as an anchor while the flock creates dynamic urban chaos.
- The birds act like moving confetti or weather; the climax is the moment the face appears between the wings.
- There must be enough birds for it to feel like the square exploding, not just "a few birds".

## How It Works

- City square, framed at chest height, 24-35mm.
- Hard sunlight or light reflected off buildings.
- Locked or very light handheld, not following individual birds.
- The flock bursts out around the figure, with wings showing natural takeoff physics.

## When to Use

- Use: Urban transitions, entrance moments, fashion shots that need an environmental rhythmic beat.
- Avoid: Interior scenes, sparse birds, a camera wandering around touring the space.
- Don't freeze the flock unless showing a suspended moment.

## Film Examples

- *Mary Poppins* (1964): Square pigeons become the urban atmosphere around the characters.
- *The Godfather* (1972): Visualizes panic and commotion in a public place with birds bursting into flight.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A woman in a red coat stands perfectly still in a sunlit stone piazza, framed chest-up at 28mm, locked camera. Hundreds of pigeons explode into flight all around her, wings filling the frame with real takeoff motion, her face glimpsed clearly between the beating wings, then the flock thins and settles.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Too few birds, or generic particles instead → Let masses of wings fill the frame.
- The subject's face is blocked → Frame the peak moment when the face emerges between the wings.
- Birds just perched on the eaves → Needs the energy of an explosive takeoff.
