# Comic

Summary: Live-action footage overlaid with inked outlines, halftone dots in the shadows and a limited palette, like a printed comic panel, while the face keeps its photographic texture.

## Narrative Function

- Wraps origin stories or punchline moments in printed-comic vocabulary.
- The image stays grounded in real space; comic elements are only a print treatment on top.

## How It Works

- Locked 35mm or 50mm at eye level, a single hard or semi-hard key so ink lines match the light direction.
- Ink lines on outlines, halftone dots only in the shadows; the face keeps its photographic texture.
- Palette limited to a few accent colors; no speech bubbles; minimal subject movement.

## When to Use

- Use: Punchlines, origin moments, or making a cityscape look like a publication.
- Avoid: Shots that need documentary skin texture, or situations that should really be fully animated.

## Film Examples

- *Dick Tracy* (1990): Specific accent colors and stylized makeup layered on live actors.
- *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* (2018): Its animation vocabulary can be adapted as a reference for grading approaches.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A detective under a streetlamp, 50mm lens at eye level, one hard key from the upper left. Bold inked outlines trace his coat, halftone dots fill only the shadow side, palette limited to black, cream and one red accent, his face still photographic with natural grain, no speech bubbles, only a slight head turn.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Over-processed into a cartoon, losing lens character and film grain → Keep the photographic texture of the face.
- Stacking Ink Riot, oil paint and other effects at once → Use only one print treatment.
- Halftone across the whole frame → Halftone only in the shadows, with a single light direction.
