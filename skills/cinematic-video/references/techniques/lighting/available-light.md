# Available Light

Summary: No added lights; only the sun, sky and practical fixtures already present at the location.

## Narrative Function

- Treats location conditions (sun angle, weather, practical fixtures) as a creative constraint.
- Conveys documentary-like realism and environmental honesty.
- Unsuitable when precise control, a consistent contrast ratio or night exteriors are needed.

## How It Works

- No artificial lights; expose only for existing sources.
- Hardness depends on weather: harsh sun = hard light; overcast = soft light.
- Color temperature is the actual Kelvin of the time of day and the practicals; when contrast is too high, choose to expose for either the face or the sky.

## When to Use

- Use: When the schedule can fit a specific time and place, and environmental realism serves the story.
- Avoid: When hidden supplemental lights, continuity across several hours, or a specific portrait lighting pattern are needed.
- Avoid: Sneaking in HMIs or reflectors while calling it available light.

## Film Examples

- *Days of Heaven* (1978): Waited for the ideal sun position, using natural daylight edge light.
- *The Revenant* (2015): Insisted on available sunlight, overcast skies and real firelight.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Documentary-style late-afternoon scene in a farmhouse kitchen, lit only by low 4pm sun through the west window and a single existing ceiling bulb. Harsh window patches and deep untouched shadows remain; no film lights, reflectors or added fill.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Calling motivated lighting available light (giveaway: shadows perfectly filled under harsh noon sun) → Remove all supplemental lights entirely, or call it motivated lighting.
