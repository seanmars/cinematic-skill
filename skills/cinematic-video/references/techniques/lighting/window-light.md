# Window Light

Summary: Large, directional light enters through a window and gradually falls off toward the depth of the room.

## Narrative Function

- Makes daytime interiors look unlit.
- Establishes a readable sense of time.
- Suits painterly rooms, period mansions and quiet dramatic scenes.

## How It Works

- Real sky or a large exterior source placed outside the glass; sheer fabric creates wrap.
- Hardness depends on weather: overcast or north window = soft; direct sun = harder.
- Use the inverse-square law and flags to shape room falloff; color temperature is daylight, cooler from the north, warmer toward evening.

## When to Use

- Use: Daytime interiors that need an architectural rather than studio feel, such as living rooms and studios.
- Avoid: Night scenes (unless moonlight through a window is intended).
- Avoid: Using frontal diffusion while ignoring the window's actual position.

## Film Examples

- *Girl with a Pearl Earring* (2003): Interiors keyed by windows like Vermeer paintings.
- *The Danish Girl* (2015): Large, directional daylit interiors with pronounced falloff.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A painter works in a 17th-century studio lit only by a tall window on the left wall under an overcast sky. Large, soft, directional daylight models her face and hands, then falls off gradually into the darker back of the room, painterly and still.
```

## Common Mistakes

- A frontal soft key that doesn't match the window wall; hard venetian-blind shadows when wrap is wanted → Turn the lit side toward the glass and preserve the room's falloff.
