# Kicker

Summary: A hard highlight from the rear side that skims the edge of the far jaw or cheek.

## Narrative Function

- Adds a glamorous or menacing accent streak on the far side of the face.
- Separates the jaw and cheek from the background with a hard highlight in shadowy scenes.
- Adds dimension, giving the portrait more texture.

## How It Works

- A small hard source placed about 30-60 degrees behind the subject to one side, at roughly cheek height or slightly above.
- Flag off light toward the lens and nose.
- Harder than the key, often warm tungsten or amber against a cooler key.

## When to Use

- Use: Emphasizing the far jawline or shoulder in keyed interior scenes.
- Use: Adding glamour or danger to night scenes.
- Avoid: Kickers on both sides (that becomes cross lighting); also unsuitable for even beauty lighting.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Rim Light](rim-light.md) | Outlines the whole silhouette, with a darker cheek |
| [Backlight](backlight.md) | Positioned closer to directly behind |
| [Hair Light](hair-light.md) | Lights hair rather than jaw |
| [Side Lighting](side-lighting.md) | 90-degree side light as key; a kicker never becomes the key |

## Film Examples

- *Double Indemnity* (1944): Hard accents on jaws and shoulders in film noir.
- *The Big Combo* (1955): The same noir approach; hard streaks carve facial edges.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night interior close-up of a detective keyed softly from camera-left, while a compact warm tungsten kicker from behind camera-right skims a thin, sharp highlight along his far jawline and coat shoulder. The accent stays small and localized, with no visible beam or outline around the whole body.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Becoming a fully outlining rim, or a "lightsaber" shaft extending from the neck → Shrink the source and flag carefully, keeping the highlight on the jaw and clothing only.
- Positioned too close to the key, losing the accent effect → Keep the kicker and key positions separate.
