# Butterfly Lighting

Summary: The key shines down from directly above the camera, forming a small butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose for a symmetrical, glamorous face.

## Narrative Function

- Symmetrical, glamorous beauty lighting that still keeps structure.
- Emphasizes cheekbones and maintains flattering symmetry.
- Unsuitable for characters framed as dishonest, damaged or asymmetrical.

## How It Works

- A medium-to-large source placed directly above the camera, aimed down.
- Adjust height so a small shadow appears under the nose on the philtrum.
- Usually soft to medium hardness, neutral color temperature; a reflector can add fill under the chin.

## When to Use

- Use: Glamour close-ups, singers facing the lens, studio beauty shots.
- Avoid: Crime profiles that need short lighting, or characters that need asymmetrical shadows.

## Film Examples

- *Shanghai Express* (1932): Classic Hollywood glamour; a centered high key creates star portraits.
- *Gilda* (1946): The same classical glamour light; symmetrical, radiant star close-ups.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Glamour close-up of a singer facing the lens, lit by a soft key centered directly above the camera and angled down. A small butterfly-shaped shadow sits just under her nose; both eyes and both cheekbones are evenly bright, with a subtle reflector lifting under the chin.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Nose shadow drifts to one side, or eye sockets go hollow → Center the key and lower it until the eye sockets open and the butterfly shadow lands right on the philtrum.
