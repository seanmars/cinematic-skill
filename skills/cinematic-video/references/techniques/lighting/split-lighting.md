# Split Lighting

Summary: The key comes from 90 degrees to the side, leaving the face half lit and half dark along the midline.

## Narrative Function

- Visualizes inner conflict, duality or moral ambiguity through a geometric split.
- Emphasizes tension rather than approachability.
- Unsuitable for romantic scenes or scenes needing character charm.

## How It Works

- A harder key placed about 90 degrees to the face, roughly at eye level.
- The shadow boundary runs vertically through forehead, nose and chin.
- Little fill; flag off wrap if needed to keep a clean midline.

## When to Use

- Use: Depicting moral complexity, interrogation scenes, theatrical situations.
- Avoid: Intimate moments needing warm connection, or when both eyes and both cheeks must convey emotion.

## Compared With Similar Techniques

| Technique | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [Side Lighting](side-lighting.md) | Broader side light that wraps body and environment, without a strict facial split |
| [Rembrandt](rembrandt.md) | Keeps a lit triangle on the shadow-side cheek; split has none |
| [Loop Lighting](loop-lighting.md) | More frontal position |
| [Short Lighting](short-lighting.md) | About which cheek faces the camera; split is about 90-degree geometry |
| [Butterfly Lighting](butterfly-lighting.md) | Both cheeks lit |

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): Half the face lit, emphasizing the character's inner weighing.
- *Whiplash* (2014): Hard side light during performance cuts the face in half.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Interrogation-room close-up: a hard key at exactly 90 degrees to the suspect's face, at eye level, splits it down a clean vertical line through forehead, nose and chin. One half is lit, the other falls into shadow, with no fill and no light triangle on the dark cheek.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Accidentally becoming Rembrandt or wrapping side light (lit area on the dark cheek, nose wrapped in soft light) → Move the key to a true 90 degrees and remove fill until the midline is clean.
