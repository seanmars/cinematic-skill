# Rim Light

Summary: A narrow edge highlight traces the subject's shape while the face itself stays dark.

## Narrative Function

- Outlines the contour with a thin edge, creating a strong near-silhouette graphic look.
- The face stays dark and only the form's outline reads, suiting crime, espionage and urban night scenes.
- Unsuitable for dialogue scenes where mouth movements must be seen.

## How It Works

- Place a small hard source behind the subject, slightly to one side, and confine the light to the outline with a flag or snoot.
- Don't let the light wrap onto the front of the face.
- Use color contrast: a cool rim with a warm key, or a warm rim against night blue.

## When to Use

- Use: Night figures, rooftop scenes, coated figures on dark streets.
- Use: When form and silhouette matter more than expression.
- Avoid: Dialogue close-ups where the mouth and expression must be clear.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Figures are edged in smoky interiors, coats and hair separated from black backgrounds.
- *Skyfall* (2012): Night exteriors use thin, natural rims to trace outlines while faces stay dark.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night rooftop, a figure in a long coat and hat seen against a black city sky. A small hard light behind and slightly to the side draws a thin, natural highlight along the hat brim, hair and coat edges while the face stays dark. Photographic rim light, not a glowing cartoon outline.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Looking like a neon outline, or becoming a silhouette with no highlight at all → Let only a thin edge highlight land on hair and fabric, from a real source behind.
