# Gobo Lighting (Cucoloris)

Summary: Blinds, bars or cut-out patterns placed in the beam project graphic shadows onto figures and spaces.

## Narrative Function

- Uses cast shadows to "write" a character's situation onto face and body.
- Conveys entrapment, noir atmosphere, or the imprint of architecture on people.
- Unsuitable for natural images with no visible design.

## How It Works

- A hard, directional source passes through blinds, a cut-out pattern or a metal gobo.
- The distance between pattern and subject determines shadow sharpness.
- Key usually high and off-axis; flag off spill to keep the pattern clean.

## When to Use

- Use: Letting the environment visually define a character, e.g. imprisonment, deception, institutional spaces.
- Requires a hard enough source for the shadows to read.
- Avoid: Arbitrary overlaid texture, or natural scenes with no narrative purpose.

## Film Examples

- *The Conformist* (1970): Architectural blinds cast graphic shadows on faces and walls.
- Classic film noir: Venetian blind and bar shadows visually build a sense of confinement.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Noir office at night: a hard, high streetlamp beam cuts through venetian blinds and lays crisp horizontal bars of shadow across the detective's face, shirt and the wall behind him. The stripes are sharp and clearly readable, like a cage.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Pattern blurred and hard to read due to soft wrap or diffusion → Harden the source and place real blinds or a cucoloris in the light path.
