# Eye Light

Summary: A faint, small frontal light that leaves only a tiny catchlight in the pupils, keeping a dark face alive.

## Narrative Function

- Preserves "humanity" in low-key portraits; with reflections in the irises, the character looks thoughtful.
- The face stays dark while avoiding hollow eye sockets.
- Omit it when the character should look blind, dead or inhuman.

## How It Works

- A small, weak source placed beside or above the lens.
- Brightness low enough not to flatten the key's shadow shapes.
- White or matched to the key; the goal is a pinpoint reflection in the iris, not lighting the whole face.

## When to Use

- Use: Low-key close-ups where the key leaves the eyes in shadow.
- Use: Film noir and interrogation scenes that need to keep a character's presence in darkness.
- Avoid: When hollow eye sockets are intended, or pupils shouldn't look like light sources.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): At times even the smallest catchlight is deliberately withheld.
- *No Country for Old Men* (2007): Natural catchlights kept in dark rooms rather than glowing irises.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Low-key close-up of an old man in a dark room, most of his face in shadow. A tiny, dim white catchlight sits in each iris from a small source near the lens, keeping his eyes alive while his hazel eyes remain natural and non-luminous.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with fill and opening up the whole face, or turning it into supernatural glowing eyes → Dim the frontal light until only the catchlight remains, and describe eye color as a surface property.
