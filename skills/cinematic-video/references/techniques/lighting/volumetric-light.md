# Volumetric Light

Summary: Beams pass through haze, dust or rain, making the light's path through the air visible.

## Narrative Function

- The air itself becomes the subject, like an architectural element.
- Visible light paths imply a world beyond the frame.
- Ineffective in clean air; too much haze flattens depth.

## How It Works

- Requires a directional source, often hard, coming from behind or a high angle.
- Requires a scattering medium: haze, dust, rain, smoke.
- The camera must see the beam angle; a backlit position reveals volume best; color inherits the source's warmth or coolness.

## When to Use

- Use: Smoky interiors, early-morning forests, searchlight scenes.
- Avoid: Floating light streaks with neither source nor medium.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Searchlights pierce the smoke.
- *The Tree of Life* (2011): Sunlight through the forest as an architectural element.
- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Dust and haze turn interiors into visible architecture of light.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Early morning in a misty pine forest: low sun behind the trees sends distinct shafts of light through the haze, dust motes drifting inside each beam. A hiker walks through one shaft and briefly breaks it, the beams clearly tied to the sun's direction.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Looking like painted-on light streaks unrelated to the lamp position → Tie every beam to a real source, and let bodies interrupt it.
