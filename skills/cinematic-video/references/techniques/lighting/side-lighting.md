# Side Lighting

Summary: Light from about 90 degrees to the side leaves half the form bright and half in shadow, bringing out texture and volume.

## Narrative Function

- Emphasizes sculpture, texture and shape.
- The half-lit, half-dark split creates duality, conveying character depth and physical dimension.
- Unsuitable for two-shots where both people must be clearly readable.

## How It Works

- Key placed about 90 degrees to the camera-subject axis.
- Adjust height as needed: eye level for faces, a low raking angle for ground texture.
- Hard light brings out texture better, though soft sources like a large window also work; color follows the source; flag to control shadow wrap if needed.

## When to Use

- Use: Carving sculpture into faces, bodies or environments.
- Use: Emphasizing texture in action or crime sequences, or texture-rich landscapes.
- Avoid: As default coverage lighting for two-shots.

## Film Examples

- *Apocalypse Now* (1979): Hard side light rakes across faces in darkness.
- *Sicario* (2015): Low sun and side light emphasize landscape grain and facial texture.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A boxer sits on a bench in a dark locker room, lit by a single hard lamp at 90 degrees from camera-right that rakes across his face and torso. One half of his body glows with sweat and texture, the other half falls into shadow, with no opposing fill or second key.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with Rembrandt (too frontal) or cross lighting (two keys, producing a cheek triangle or rims on both sides) → Place a single key at 90 degrees and remove opposing fill.
