# Glam

Summary: A large soft source near the lens makes skin refined and eyes bright, presenting the face as a star product.

## Narrative Function

- "Sells" the face through stardom, fashion and romance.
- Smooth pores, lively eyes; the face is the product.
- Unsuitable for gritty, ailing, or dishonest characters in the dark.
- Unlike butterfly (a specific nose shadow) and high-key (overall brightness), it is a complete setup.

## How It Works

- A large soft source placed near the lens, slightly high.
- A bounce fill can be added below to avoid eye-socket shadows.
- Neutral or slightly warm beauty color temperature; diffusion on lens or source softens highlights.
- Catchlights sit inside the iris, not grid-shaped reflections of the fixture.

## When to Use

- Use: Star close-ups, beauty stills, romantic leads.
- Avoid: Corpse scenes, boxing injuries, documentary interviews that need realism.

## Film Examples

- *Carol* (2015): Source size and catchlights sell the face, withdrawn as the story darkens.
- *Phantom Thread* (2017): Fashion-style beauty lighting, removed as the story progresses.
- *A Star Is Born* (2018): Concert close-ups and makeover sequences use a large beauty key for star moments.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Beauty close-up of a film star: a large soft key right beside the lens and slightly above, with a gentle bounce from below to fill the eye sockets. Skin looks smooth yet natural, and a clean round catchlight sits inside each iris; no hard shadows or grid reflections.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Imitating butterfly with a small hard light; grid-shaped reflections on skin or eyes → Enlarge the key, move it close to the lens, and keep catchlights inside the iris only.
