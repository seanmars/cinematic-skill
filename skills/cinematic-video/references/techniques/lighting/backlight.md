# Backlight

Summary: A source behind the subject, facing the camera, outlines the edges and separates the subject from the background.

## Narrative Function

- Traces the edges of hair, shoulders and silhouette so a dark subject emerges from a dark background.
- Forms visible shafts in smoke or particles, implying an off-screen window, searchlight or sun.
- Renders form through outline rather than facial detail.

## How It Works

- Place the source behind the subject, usually high, aimed toward the camera; flag off unwanted flare.
- Hard sources (sun, PAR) create crisp edges; soft sources create a gentle glow on hair.
- A color temperature slightly warmer than the key makes the edge light look natural; the beams only become visible with airborne particles.

## When to Use

- Use: Separating dark figures in dark environments, e.g. smoky interiors or dim rooms with a window behind.
- Avoid: Using it as the only source when the face must be readable.

## Film Examples

- *Blade Runner* (1982): Searchlights pierce smoky interiors; the beams become architectural elements.
- *The Exorcist* (1973): The arriving character is backed by strong light spilling from the house.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A man stands in a dim, smoky hallway with a bright window behind him; strong backlight outlines his hair, coat collar and shoulders with a warm edge. His face stays in soft, low exposure but still faintly readable, and faint haze makes the rear light rays visible.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Turning into a fully black silhouette, or a neon outline disconnected from the scene → Keep slight exposure on the key side of the face, and tie the edge light to a real source behind.
- Describing it as a magical glow → State that the light is behind the subject, which edges it hits and how dark the face is; add haze only if beams are needed.
