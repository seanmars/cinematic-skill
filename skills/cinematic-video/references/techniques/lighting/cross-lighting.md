# Cross Lighting

Summary: Two opposing keys hit the subject from both sides, producing edge light on each side and charged stage energy.

## Narrative Function

- Two edge lights bring theatrical energy.
- Suits two-person scenes, performances, or shots that must cover both sides of several people.
- Unsuitable for single-lamp interiors or situations with only one motivated source.

## How It Works

- Two hard or medium-hard keys, each about 70-110 degrees off-axis and often slightly behind, serving as both key and rim.
- Gels can match, or go one cool and one warm to suggest two different worlds.
- Flag the lights away from the lens.

## When to Use

- Use: Stages, concerts, sweaty performances, two-person scenes that need keys on both sides.
- Avoid: Single-lamp rooms (unless deliberately imitating a concert aesthetic).

## Film Examples

- *Whiplash* (2014): Performance sequences use opposing keys to emphasize edges and physical tension.
- *A Star Is Born* (2018): Stage performances use two-way keys to outline both sides.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A sweating drummer mid-solo on a dark stage, lit by two hard opposing keys from left-rear and right-rear: a warm amber on one side and a cool blue on the other. Both edges of his face, arms and cymbals catch bright rims, with concert-level theatrical energy.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Mistakenly using a one-sided key or frontal fill setup, so only one side has a rim → Place two hard or medium-hard keys on opposite sides, slightly behind, ensuring edge light on both sides.
