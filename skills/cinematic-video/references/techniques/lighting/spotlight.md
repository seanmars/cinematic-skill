# Spotlight

Summary: A theatrical fixture throws a hard-edged circular pool, isolating the subject within it, surrounded by darkness.

## Narrative Function

- Uses a stage-like circle to mark the subject as a performer or a show in progress.
- Stepping out of the circle means leaving the scene.

## How It Works

- Use a focusable fixture (ellipsoidal, follow spot, or PAR with a narrow lens).
- Place it high, frontal or front-side, using shutters or an iris for a sharp edge.
- Leave the surroundings unlit; the circle edge falls off hard.

## When to Use

- Use: Stage performances, concerts, speeches, nightclub acts.
- Avoid: Ordinary interiors without theatrical fixtures, such as kitchens.

| Technique | Difference |

## Film Examples

- *Whiplash* (2014): Musicians stand in hard stage pools of light.
- *Birdman* (2014): Theatrical sequences treat follow spots as environmental elements.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A stand-up comedian on a dark club stage, caught in a single hard-edged circle from a follow spot mounted high in front. The pool's sharp, irised boundary is clearly visible on the floor around his feet, and everything outside it is black.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Making it a soft cameo, or just an ordinary hard key with no visible circle → Iris down a focusable fixture to build a clear pool edge.
