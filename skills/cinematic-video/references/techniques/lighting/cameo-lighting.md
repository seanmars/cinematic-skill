# Cameo Lighting

Summary: Only the figure is lit; the environment sinks into pure black, so the figure seems to emerge from a void.

## Narrative Function

- Erases the environment, keeping only the subject's presence.
- Creates theatrical isolation, suited to confessions or stage asides.
- Unsuitable when spatial geography or environmental context matters to the story.

## How It Works

- The subject can be lit with any portrait lighting pattern.
- Eliminate ambient and bounce light with black cloth, distance and flags.
- Crush the background to true black, not a dim but readable space; hardness and color are flexible, the point is isolation.

## When to Use

- Use: When the figure itself is the only "location" that matters.
- Use: Confessional or introspective moments.
- Avoid: Scenes that need spatial context, such as chases, corridors or weather-dependent narratives.

## Film Examples

- *The Elephant Man* (1980): Figures in isolated pools of light, with no set context.
- *Birdman* (2014): Dark stage sequences render actors like paper cutouts.
- *The Batman* (2022): Figures isolated in pools of light, with the environment deliberately hidden.

## Prompt Examples

```text
An old actor delivers a confession in close-up, his face softly modeled by a three-quarter key, while everything around him falls to absolute black with no visible furniture or walls. Theatrical isolation, as if he floats in a void, with no hard spotlight circle.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Furniture still visible in the background, or an unlit figure against a bright background → Fully expose the subject and let the background truly sink to black.
