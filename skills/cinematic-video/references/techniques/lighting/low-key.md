# Low-Key Lighting

Summary: High contrast with shadows dominating the frame; only a few areas are lit.

## Narrative Function

- Creates mystery and concealment through selective visibility.
- The ratio of lit to dark areas itself tells the story, suiting moral ambiguity.
- Unsuitable for comedic timing, or scenes where spatial geography must be clear.

## How It Works

- Place one motivated key; suppress bounce with flags and black cloth.
- Contrast ratio is usually 8:1 or higher.
- Hard light makes shadow edges more graphic; soft light also works if isolated; common looks are tungsten in a black room or a single cool streak.

## When to Use

- Use: Crime scenes, night offices, corridors lit only by practicals.
- Avoid: Simply dimming an evenly lit room.
- Avoid: When spatial orientation matters to the plot.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): Selective pools of light indoors, contrasted with bright exteriors.
- *The Big Combo* (1955): High-contrast night scenes where darkness itself is usable space.
- *Se7en* (1995): Only partial pools of light revealed in dark interiors.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Low-key night office: a single green-shaded desk lamp throws an isolated pool of light across papers and a man's hands, with contrast well beyond 8:1. The rest of the room falls into deep designed shadow; only faint edges of furniture are visible.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Making "dimmed high-key" (evenly darkened overall), or crushed blacks with no directional key → Place a single source, flag off spill, and expose for the pool of light.
