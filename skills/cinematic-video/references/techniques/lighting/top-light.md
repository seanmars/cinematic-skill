# Top Light

Summary: The source sits directly overhead; forehead and shoulders catch light while the eye sockets sink into shadow.

## Narrative Function

- Creates authority and menace, with eye sockets swallowed by shadow.
- Expresses power dynamics, guilt or interrogation for seated figures.
- Unsuitable for flattering portraits.

## How It Works

- A lamp or ceiling practical placed directly above the subject, flagged to avoid sweeping across the nose from the front.
- Hard light (bare bulb) creates a skull-like effect; softer light allows subtler treatment.
- Expose for the forehead; keep eye sockets dark and pupils without highlights.

## When to Use

- Use: Powerful seated figures, diner atmospheres, guilty expressions under garage lights.
- Avoid: As beauty or glamour lighting.

## Film Examples

- *The Godfather* (1972): Office top light hits forehead and hands, with eyes in shadow.
- *The Batman* (2022): Overhead rain and practicals form a "light cap" on faces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A crime boss sits at a desk beneath a single bare bulb hanging directly overhead. Light falls on his forehead, shoulders and folded hands while his eye sockets sink into deep shadow; his eyes stay natural and dark, with no glowing pupils.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Accidentally becoming butterfly lighting, or glowing pupils → Put the source truly directly overhead and keep the eye surface dark, leaving only a tiny reflection.
