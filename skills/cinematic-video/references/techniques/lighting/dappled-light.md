# Dappled Light

Summary: Hard light passes through irregular occluders such as leaves, water or blinds, scattering broken patches of light across the subject.

## Narrative Function

- Natural elements break up the light into organic patterns that don't look deliberately designed.
- Patches take irregular leaf shapes or water caustics, not clean graphic shapes.
- Unsuitable for clean beauty close-ups or deliberate graphic-shadow statements.

## How It Works

- Requires a hard sun or lamp, plus an occluding layer between the light and the subject.
- The distance between occluder and subject determines how soft or hard the patches are.
- Usually daylight-balanced; wind or moving water animates the patches.

## When to Use

- Use: Forest scenes, resting under trees, situations where natural light is broken up.
- Avoid: Using a clean, unobstructed key and adding patches in post.

## Film Examples

- *The Thin Red Line* (1998): The jungle canopy breaks up sunlight on the soldiers.
- *Moonlight* (2016): Broken daylight and night sources form irregular patches on bodies; occlusion is practical, not overlaid.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A woman naps in a hammock under an oak tree at midday. Hard sunlight filtering through moving leaves scatters irregular, leaf-shaped patches across her face, arms and cotton dress, the pattern shifting gently in the breeze. Organic and uneven, not a projected geometric pattern.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Using a clean key, or a designed gobo (parallel stripes) → Place real trees, water or irregular foliage in the hard light beam.
