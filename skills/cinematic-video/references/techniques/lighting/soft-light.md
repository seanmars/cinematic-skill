# Soft Light

Summary: A large source wraps the subject with softly graduated shadow edges while keeping direction.

## Narrative Function

- Conveys tenderness, memory, overcast or north-window light.
- The room breathes; nose shadows grade softly.
- Can still have contrast when fill is low, unlike directionless ambient light; unsuitable for gobos, noon showdowns or razor-edged splits.

## How It Works

- Enlarge the last emitting surface before the subject: diffusion, book light, overcast sky, large window, bounce wall.
- The closer and larger, the softer.
- Common setups are 5600K daylight through diffusion, or tungsten through a large shade; keep a clear key side.

## When to Use

- Use: Beauty, memory, tenderness, daylit interiors, close-ups needing refined skin tones.
- Avoid: Lighting that needs projected patterns or an interrogation feel.

## Film Examples

- *The Tree of Life* (2011): Sky and window light wrap skin and interiors.
- *Call Me by Your Name* (2017): Summer window light gently wraps the characters.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Close-up of a young man by a large sheer-curtained window on the left: broad, soft daylight wraps his face with gradual shadow transitions across the nose and cheek. The key side stays clearly on the left, skin looks luminous and gentle.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Sharp shadow edges, or no clear key side → Enlarge the source and place it to one side of the face.
