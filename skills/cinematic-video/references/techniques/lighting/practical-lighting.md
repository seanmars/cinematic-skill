# Practical Lighting

Summary: Lamps, neon, candles and screens visible in frame actually light the characters.

## Narrative Function

- Turns the set itself into the lighting department, with believable light logic.
- Replaces invisible studio lights with in-frame fixtures, strengthening realism.

## How It Works

- Place fixtures that actually emit light in frame, usually brighter than in a real room for exposure.
- Control with bulbs, dimmers and flags; if needed, hide a small light inside a matching shade.
- Deliberately mix color temperatures: tungsten 2700-3200K, neon, screen light.

## When to Use

- Use: Scenes where faces are lit by environmental light, such as night apartments, bars, car interiors.
- Avoid: Fixtures that are mere set dressing while a hidden frontal light does the real work.
- Avoid: In-frame lamps that are dark yet don't light the face.

## Film Examples

- *Barry Lyndon* (1975): Firelight as the night lighting logic.
- *In the Mood for Love* (2000): Table lamps and interior fixtures actually light the faces.
- *Blade Runner 2049* (2017): Neon and screen light do the work on faces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Night apartment scene lit only by what is in frame: a warm 2700K table lamp keys the woman's face from the left, while a cool blue TV screen spills across her shoulder. Mixed color temperatures are deliberate and no hidden film lights are implied.
```

## Common Mistakes

- In-frame lamps are dark props while the face is lit by an off-screen frontal key → Brighten the practical, or hide a matching-color light inside the shade; remove the isolated frontal light.
