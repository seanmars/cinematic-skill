# Blue Hour

Summary: Cool twilight after the sun has dropped below the horizon while the sky still glows, paired with freshly lit warm artificial lights.

## Narrative Function

- Creates a melancholic urban mood.
- Cool sky against warm artificial light adds emotional depth without harsh directional sunlight.

## How It Works

- Expose for the glowing sky, using it as a cool soft key from above to the side.
- Layer warm practicals (streetlights, windows, signs) as secondary accents.

## When to Use

- Use: Urban or open exterior dusk scenes where the sky is still bright and artificial lights have just come on.
- Avoid: When direct low-angle sunlight or hard shadows are needed.

## Film Examples

- *Heat* (1995): Los Angeles dusk; cobalt sky mixed with sodium and tungsten lights.
- *Collateral* (2004): Cool and warm sources interweave in urban twilight.
- *Drive* (2011): Blend of Los Angeles blue-hour sky and streetlights.

## Prompt Examples

```text
City rooftop just after sunset: the sky glows deep cobalt with no sun disk visible, wrapping the lone figure in soft cool light from above. Warm sodium streetlights and lit apartment windows below add small amber accents, melancholic and quiet.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing it with golden hour (warm sunlit shadows appear), or going straight to full-black night → Remove the sun, keep the wrap light cool, and turn on practicals under a still-bright sky.
