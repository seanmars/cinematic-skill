# Bounce Light

Summary: A light or sunlight is aimed at a reflective surface, which then lights the subject, softening shadows while keeping direction.

## Narrative Function

- Soft, wrapping light on faces without hard edges.
- Suits period interiors and daylit rooms.
- Incompatible with razor-edged shadows or gobo patterns.

## How It Works

- A light or sunlight hits a white or neutral surface, which lights the subject.
- The larger and closer the surface, the softer the light.
- The surface color affects the warmth of the light.

## When to Use

- Use: When you need softness without losing direction.
- Avoid: When a crisp nose shadow, hard graphic shadows or projected patterns are needed.

## Film Examples

- *Cries and Whispers* (1972): Wall bounce wraps faces in light with soft edges.
- *The Tree of Life* (2011): Bounce light renders soft interior light on faces.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Period sitting room: a lamp aimed at a large white wall bounces soft, directional light back onto the woman's face from camera-left. Shadows wrap gently with soft edges, and no small bare bulb reads as the source.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Sharp shadows that look like a small source mean the bounce surface is missing → Place a large wall or reflector in front of the subject as the final reflective surface.
