# Candlelight

Summary: A small, warm flame source with very fast falloff and natural flicker; faces are carved by firelight while the surroundings sink into darkness.

## Narrative Function

- Brings intimacy to period night scenes; light doesn't travel far, so people lean toward it.
- Darkness is an inhabitable space rather than evenly lighting the room.
- Suits rituals, card games and vigils; unsuitable for large halls or modern scenes that need even readability.

## How It Works

- Cluster real or LED candles near the face.
- Small sources produce fairly hard shadows; multiple flames add slight wrap.
- Very warm color temperature, about 1800K, far below standard tungsten; falls off steeply per the inverse-square law.
- Placed below the chin it becomes under-light; at eye level it acts as a frontal or 3/4 key.

## When to Use

- Use: Period night scenes, rituals, intimate dinner-table scenes.
- Avoid: Large halls, modern kitchens, situations that need even fill.

## Film Examples

- *Barry Lyndon* (1975): Candlelit card tables and night interiors, with fast lenses for believable falloff.
- *The Witch* (2015): Flame as a plausible night source in dark New England.

## Prompt Examples

```text
Period night scene: three card players lean over a table lit only by a cluster of candles. Warm 1800K flickering light carves their faces and hands, falling off steeply so the room's corners dissolve into darkness. Uneven illumination, visible flame detail, no modern fill or flat orange wash.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Evenly lighting areas far from the flame with large tungsten or film lights, or washing everything orange → Keep the steep falloff and hold the color temperature at real flame Kelvin.
