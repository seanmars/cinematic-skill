# Silhouette

Summary: Exposing for a bright background turns the subject into a fully black shape that tells the story through outline alone.

## Narrative Function

- Prioritizes shape and outline; identity and facial detail become secondary.
- Conveys action through shape alone, suiting departures, arrivals and symbolic gestures.
- Most effective when the silhouette itself is the narrative.

## How It Works

- Expose for the bright background (sky, doorway, firelight, sun).
- No key on the subject, or keep it far darker than the background.
- Limbs, props and hat brims must be separated so the outline reads; direct sun or a bright overcast sky both work.

## When to Use

- Use: When shape matters more than identity and the action reads from outline alone.
- Avoid: Dialogue needing visible mouth movements, or when facial detail or color matters.
- Avoid: When outline alone can't convey the action.

## Film Examples

- *The Searchers* (1956): Figures isolated against a bright doorframe and the wilderness.
- *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial* (1982): Pure silhouette of bicycle and riders flying across the moon.

## Prompt Examples

```text
A lone cowboy stands in a doorway facing out toward a blazing desert noon, rendered as a pure black silhouette. No facial detail or surface texture, the hat brim, arms and rifle clearly separated in outline against the bright sky, no rim glow.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Eyes still visible, or a dim background making the whole image muddy → Brighten the background, remove every key on the body, and separate the limb outlines.
- Adding an artificial glow or neon outline → A silhouette needs no edge light.
